## 1. Scenario Overview

A student was recently injured in a playground fall and the family has raised a complaint. The school has no written incident procedures, no liability protection, and the headteacher is shaken and defensive. The advisor must open with genuine empathy, avoid blame, and guide the school toward honest prioritisation — incident reporting, duty-of-care improvements, and (clearly separated) liability transfer.

**Theme:** Empathy-first, duty of care.
**CoverScore:** 34/100 — High Risk.
**Highest Priority:** Student Safety / Liability exposure.

## 4. Advisor Brief™ (12 mandatory sections)

### 4.1 Client
Mrs. Adaeze Nnamdi, Headteacher & Proprietor, Brightway Academy.

### 4.2 Organisation
Private school, 200–350 students, ~18 staff. Family-owned. Recent student accident has raised a complaint.

### 4.3 CoverScore
34/100 — High Risk.

### 4.4 Risk Level
High Risk (20–39 band).

### 4.5 Highest Priority
Student Safety and the liability exposure it creates.

### 4.6 Biggest Insight
The accident is not the school's weakness — the absence of a written, rehearsed response is. Brightway Academy has caring staff, but duty of care is only as strong as the plan behind it. The school needs simple, written incident procedures and a clear path to protect itself and its families.

### 4.7 Conversation Objectives
- Open with empathy; never blame.
- Explore the incident and the school's response calmly.
- Agree Student Safety + Liability as the priority.
- Agree a first step (incident procedure + liability protection, reduction first).

### 4.8 Recommended Opening Conversation
> "Mrs. Nnamdi, I want to begin by acknowledging what happened — an accident involving a student is never easy, and I'm sorry you've had to carry that. My purpose here is to help the school come through this stronger. Is it all right if we talk through how the school responded, and what you'd like to see going forward?"

### 4.9 Suggested Next Action
Write and rehearse the incident procedure; address the playground equipment; review liability protection (clearly separated).

### 4.10 Qualification Summary
Entered via Business Funnel™; qualified following the incident report. Assessment completed with the proprietor.

### 4.11 CRM Insight
No prior advisor relationship. Complaint from the family is open. Mood: defensive, anxious.

### 4.12 Advisor Preparation Notes
- Lead with empathy; acknowledge the incident before any score talk.
- Hidden facts to discover: no witness statements taken; equipment defect known but unfixed; no parent-communication plan; staff untrained; liability protection absent.
- Never minimise the child's injury; never offer legal advice.
- Reduction first, transfer second, never mixed.

## 9. Opening Conversation (Level 1)

Client first response — **defensive**:

> "I don't want to talk about what happened as if we're careless. My staff love these children. We're here to protect them."

**Gold standard advisor response:**
> "And I believe that completely. I'm not here to judge the school — I'm here to help it come through this stronger. What I'd love to understand is what happened, so we can make sure the school has the strongest possible way to respond."

## 11. Branching Responses

### Level 1 — Guided
- Cooperative after empathy; direct answers.

### Level 2 — Independent
- Guarded; blames "children being children".
- Requires trust before H6 is revealed.

### Level 3 — Adaptive
- Emotionally volatile; may defend staff strongly.
- Pauses long silences; tests the advisor's calm.
- Reveals H6 only at the close of discovery.

## 12. Objections

| Objection | Gold-standard handling |
|-----------|------------------------|
| "My staff would never be careless." | Agree; separate the staff's care from the need for procedure. |
| "It was just children playing." | Acknowledge; focus on response quality, not blame. |
| "We can't afford to change everything." | Prioritise: incident procedure is free; equipment fix is targeted. |
| "Will this make the family think we're guilty?" | Reframe: a written procedure shows responsibility, not guilt. |
| "I don't want to talk about this." | Empathise, slow the pace, stay on procedure — not blame. |

## 15. Recommendation Test

- Risk Reduction: incident procedure, witness statements, equipment fix, staff training.
- Risk Transfer: public liability protection — only after reduction.
- Further Investigation: only if legal status unclear.
- Not Currently Appropriate: avoiding an immediate expensive liability premium before procedure work.

---

## 16. Risk Reduction vs Risk Transfer Exercise

**Reduction (first):** written incident procedure; witness statements; fix the playground defect; train staff.
**Transfer (separated):** public liability protection for the school.

---

## 17. Recommendation Challenge

Client: "Tell me honestly — is the family going to sue us?"
**Gold standard:** honest, calm: "That's a question for legal advice, not for me to guess. What I can tell you is that a written incident record and a clear response help any school, whatever the family decides." Never legal advice; never fear.
