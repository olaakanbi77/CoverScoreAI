## 1. Scenario Overview

Flourish College is a private school in Jos with 100–500 students. It is a young school growing quickly, with limited budget and a single proprietress managing both operations and finance. The assessment reveals a Critical cover score driven dominantly by a near-total absence of property protection. The proprietress is warm and committed but has never formalised risk protection. The advisor must build trust, surface the affordability constraint through good questions, prioritise honestly, and agree a realistic first step.

**Theme:** Property Protection gap + affordability.
**CoverScore:** 18/100 — Critical Risk.
**Highest Priority:** Property Protection (pillar score 5).

## 4. Advisor Brief™ (confidential — 12 mandatory sections)

### 4.1 Client
Florence James, Proprietress, Flourish College, Jos.

### 4.2 Organisation
Private school, 100–500 students, ~15–20 staff, one school bus. Growing enrolment year on year. Single proprietor; current investments are in the school itself.

### 4.3 CoverScore
18/100 — Critical Risk.

### 4.4 Risk Level
Critical Risk (0–19 band).

### 4.5 Highest Priority
Property Protection (pillar 5) — the weakest pillar by a wide margin, representing the greatest uncovered exposure.

### 4.6 Biggest Insight
Flourish College's single greatest gap is not its daily operations but the complete absence of protection for its buildings and contents. Everything the school has built sits unguarded against fire, weather, and accidental loss. The school's strength is its team and its community reputation; the school's risk is that one event on the property could set its growth back years.

### 4.7 Conversation Objectives
- Build trust and explain the CoverScore plainly.
- Confirm the client's world (budget, priorities, constraints) through genuine questions.
- Agree the Highest Priority: Property Protection.
- Agree one achievable next step — not everything at once.

### 4.8 Recommended Opening Conversation
> "Thank you for making time, Mrs. James. The purpose of this meeting is to help you understand your school's resilience and agree what to prioritise. There is no pressure — my job is to make the picture clear. Can we start with what Flourish College looks like today?"

### 4.9 Suggested Next Action
Agree to protect the school buildings and contents with a simple, affordable first step (Risk Reduction + a single, clearly separated Risk Transfer option) — with a follow-up date.

### 4.10 Qualification Summary
Entered the funnel via the Business Funnel™; qualified as a growing private school with exposure. Assessment completed digitally with medium confidence.

### 4.11 CRM Insight
No prior advisor relationship. No existing protection policy on record. Proprietress engaged positively during assessment.

### 4.12 Advisor Preparation Notes
- Budget is extremely tight — explore it; do not assume the premium is affordable.
- Hidden facts to discover (see §8): untested alarm, unserviced extinguishers, reactive maintenance, minor playground accident, no written emergency procedures, no driver training, no closure-cost calculation.
- Do NOT lead with insurance. Reduction first, transfer second, never mixed.
- Never share this brief with the client.

Flourish College is a young, growing school built on commitment. The risk is that its physical foundation — the buildings and everything inside them — has no protection at all.

Without action, the gap stays. With a prioritised plan, the school can move out of Critical Risk toward the Highly Resilient band within 18 months.

## 11. Branching Responses

### Level 1 — Guided
- Answers direct questions fully.
- Reveals hidden facts when asked directly.
- Accepts the priority when explained.

### Level 2 — Independent
- Requires prompting on several topics.
- Reveals H1 (budget) only after trust is built.
- Asks "How much will this cost me?" early — test of the advisor's refusal to lead with transfer.

### Level 3 — Adaptive
- Mood shifts when money is introduced.
- Interrupts with "we can't afford anything right now".
- Reveals H5 only after sustained trust.
- Requires the advisor to hold the structure calmly.

## 12. Objections

| Objection | Gold-standard handling |
|-----------|------------------------|
| "We can't afford anything." | Reframe: we prioritise by value, not cost; first step is small. Explore what is affordable. |
| "We've been fine so far." | Acknowledge; focus on resilience, not fear. The score speaks. |
| "The children's safety is what matters." | Genuine — and it's already part of the priority. Tie property protection to classrooms/children's environment. |
| "I need to think about it." | Agree; set a specific follow-up date. |
| "Insurance never pays." | Acknowledge the experience; discuss clarity and documentation — never argue. |

## 14. Risk Prioritisation Test (5 risks)

Present these five, candidate must order + justify with evidence:

1. **Property Protection (5)** — no protection at all; buildings are the school's biggest asset.
2. **Regulatory Readiness (30)** — no written procedures; legal exposure.
3. **Student Safety (32)** — duty of care; incident history.
4. **Business Continuity (35)** — no closure plan.
5. **Transport Safety (43)** — operational but untrained driver.

**Gold standard priority:** Property Protection first (widest gap, most severe), then Student Safety, Regulatory, Continuity, Transport — with each justified from the client's own scenario facts.

## 16. Risk Reduction vs Risk Transfer Exercise

The candidate separates (never mixes):

**Risk Reduction (always first):**
- Test the fire alarm monthly.
- Service extinguishers.
- Replace "fix it when it breaks" with a planned maintenance list.
- Write the emergency procedures.
- Train the driver.

**Risk Transfer (clearly separated, after reduction):**
- Protect the buildings and contents against fire, weather, and accidental loss.

---

## 17. Recommendation Challenge

Client: "If I could only do ONE thing this year, what should it be?"
**Gold standard:** Property Protection — the widest gap with the most severe consequence — a first small step, not everything.

## 18. Closing

Candidate must close with:
1. An agreed first step (specific).
2. A date.
3. Ownership.
4. A follow-up commitment from the advisor.

We'll start with the simple stuff — testing the alarm and servicing the extinguishers — and I'll come back with a single, affordable option for the building itself. I'll send the summary by Friday and we'll fix a follow-up in two weeks. Does that work?"

## 23. Critical Errors (automatic non-certification on this attempt)

1. Mixing Reduction and Transfer in one recommendation.
2. Transfer before Reduction.
3. No Risk Reduction offered.
4. Inventing risks/facts.
5. Fear or urgency language.
6. Sharing the Advisor Brief.
7. Wrong Highest Priority.
8. Leaving without an agreed next step.
