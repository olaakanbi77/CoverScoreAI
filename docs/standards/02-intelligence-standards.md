# CoverScore Intelligence Standards™

**Version:** v1.0 | **Status:** Canonical | **Applies to:** All products, all platforms, all communications

---

## 1. Purpose

The Intelligence Standards define how CoverScore produces, presents, and communicates intelligence. They guarantee that every assessment, report, Advisor Brief, and conversation is consistent, professional, and true to the CoverScore promise:

> **CoverScore does not sell insurance. CoverScore builds resilience.**

---

## 2. Scoring Bands

Every assessment produces a single **CoverScore™** from **0 to 100**.

| Score | Risk Level™ | Meaning |
|-------|-------------|---------|
| 80 – 100 | Highly Resilient | Strong protection; opportunities to refine |
| 60 – 79 | Moderately Resilient | Solid foundations; meaningful improvement available |
| 40 – 59 | Needs Improvement | Important gaps; prioritised action needed |
| 20 – 39 | High Risk | Significant exposure; urgent prioritised attention |
| 0 – 19 | Critical Risk | Severe exposure; immediate focused action required |

**Rules**
- One CoverScore per assessment.
- Score bands never overlap and never change per product.
- Bands are the only permitted risk-level labels. Do not substitute "Good", "Bad", "OK", or any informal label.

---

## 3. Risk Pillars

- Every assessment produces **5–9 Risk Pillars™**.
- Pillar sets are defined per product in the **Assessment Catalogue**.
- Each pillar has its own 0–100 score, confidence, and evidence.
- Pillars are ranked by score (lowest first) to surface the weakest areas.

---

## 4. Intelligence Outputs

### 4.1 CoverScore™
The single 0–100 score. Always shown with the Risk Level™ label.

### 4.2 Highest Priority™
Exactly **one** risk. The most urgent and impactful issue for this client at this time.

### 4.3 Biggest Insight™
**80–150 words.** The most valuable single insight from the assessment — the thing the client would most benefit from understanding.

### 4.4 Risk Story™
**150–300 words.** The narrative that connects the assessment to the client's reality. Written for the client's ear, not a compliance file.

### 4.5 Resilience Forecast™
The likely trajectory if nothing changes, and the levers that change it.

### 4.6 Improvement Potential™
**Current score → potential score + points available.** Shows what improvement is realistic.

### 4.7 Recommended First Step™
Exactly **one** action. Stated simply, clearly, and immediately actionable.

---

## 5. Recommendation Standards

### 5.1 Two Categories Only
Every recommendation falls into exactly one category:

| Category | Definition |
|----------|-----------|
| **Risk Reduction™** | Actions the client takes to reduce the likelihood or impact of a risk |
| **Risk Transfer™** | Transferring a risk to a third party (e.g., insurance, contracts) |

### 5.2 The Non-Negotiable Rules
1. **Risk Reduction and Risk Transfer are never mixed in one recommendation.**
2. Risk Reduction is always presented **before** Risk Transfer.
3. Every recommendation is specific, actionable, and framed in resilience terms.

### 5.3 Never
- Never present insurance as the first or only answer.
- Never mix reduction and transfer in a single statement.
- Never use fear, urgency, or pressure to create action.

## 6. Advisor Brief™ Standard

The Advisor Brief is **confidential** and intended for the advisor only. It is the advisor's complete preparation document.

### Mandatory sections
1. **Client** — name, role, context.
2. **Organisation** (if applicable) — profile, size, operations.
3. **CoverScore** — score and Risk Level.
4. **Risk Level** — band label.
5. **Highest Priority** — the single priority risk.
6. **Biggest Insight** — the 80–150 word insight.
7. **Conversation Objectives** — what the conversation should achieve.
8. **Recommended Opening Conversation** — suggested opening for the meeting.
9. **Suggested Next Action** — the Recommended First Step.
10. **Qualification Summary** — how the client entered the funnel.
11. **CRM Insight** — relevant CRM history and context.
12. **Advisor Preparation Notes** — confidential guidance, questions to explore, flags.

### Rules
- Advisor Brief is never shared with the client.
- It is produced by the platform, not the advisor.
- It always contains all 12 sections before a meeting is scheduled.

## 7. CoverScore Report™ Standard

The client-facing report. Mandatory structure:

1. **Executive Summary**
2. **CoverScore** — score + Risk Level
3. **Risk Level** — band explanation
4. **Highest Priority** — the one priority risk
5. **Risk Pillars** — pillar scores and explanation
6. **Biggest Insight** — 80–150 words
7. **Risk Story** — 150–300 words
8. **Resilience Forecast**
9. **Improvement Potential** — current → potential
10. **Risk Reduction Recommendations**
11. **Risk Transfer Recommendations**
12. **Recommended First Step** — exactly one
13. **Advisor Invitation** — next step to work with an advisor

### Report writing rules
- Written in plain language a client understands.
- Client-first tone: strength before gap.
- No jargon. No fear. No selling language.
- All scores labelled with their band.

---

## 8. AI Writing Standards

- **Lengths:** Biggest Insight 80–150 words; Risk Story 150–300 words.
- **Tone:** calm, professional, hopeful, specific.
- **Vocabulary:** resilience, protection, preparedness, improvement, priority, planning, confidence, continuity, review.
- **Avoid:** cheap, expensive, failure, danger, disaster, scary, "must buy", "guaranteed".
- **Person:** second person ("you", "your") for the client-facing report.
- **Structure:** short paragraphs, plain sentences, one idea per paragraph.

## 9. The CARE™ Framework

CARE is the conversational framework every advisor uses in every client conversation.

| Letter | Stage | Purpose |
|--------|-------|---------|
| **C** | **Connect** | Establish rapport and context — build trust, confirm purpose |
| **A** | **Ask** | Explore the client's concerns, priorities and experiences — ask, don't assume |
| **R** | **Reframe** | Connect the client's concerns to the risk intelligence |
| **E** | **Enable** | Help the client understand practical next steps and move toward action |

### CARE rules
- C before A. A before R. R before E. Never skip a stage.
- Ask is driven by genuine curiosity, not script.
- Reframe connects the client's concerns to the risk intelligence; responses are always reduction-first.
- Enable ends with agreed next steps, dates, and ownership.

---

## 10. Funnel Standards

- **Business Funnel™** and **Personal Funnel™** follow identical six stages: Discover, Engage, Assess, Advise, Protect, Review.
- Advisors never skip stages. Qualification precedes assessment. Advisor Brief precedes conversation.

---

## 11. CRM Standards

- Every client interaction is logged in the CRM.
- Every conversation records: date, stage, decisions, actions, next steps.
- Every Recommendation and Protection Strategy item is tracked to completion.
- CRM entries are factual, professional, and client-focused.
- CRM notes never contain fear-based or pressure language.

---

## 12. Naming Standards

- Products: `[Subject] Resilience™` (business) / `[Subject] Readiness™`, `Protection™`, `Intelligence™` (personal).
- Intelligence objects: CoverScore™, Risk Level™, Risk Pillars™, Highest Priority™, Biggest Insight™, Risk Story™, Resilience Forecast™, Improvement Potential™, Recommended First Step™, Advisor Brief™, Protection Strategy™.
- Never invent names outside this catalogue.

---

## 13. Quality Standards

Every intelligence output is checked against:

1. **Accuracy** — does it reflect the assessment data?
2. **Clarity** — can a non-specialist understand it?
3. **Tone** — calm, professional, resilient.
4. **Consistency** — matches the band, the pillars, the product.
5. **Completeness** — all mandatory sections present.
6. **Actionability** — does it point to a next step?

---

## 14. Governance

- These standards are the single source of truth.
- Changes require a versioned change request.
- Training, prompts, and advisor operations must reference the current version.
- Non-conforming content is corrected, not exempted.
