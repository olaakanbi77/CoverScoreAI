# CoverScore Advisor Playbook™

**Version:** v1.0 | **Status:** Canonical | **Owner:** CoverScore Advisory Leadership

---

## 1. Purpose

The Advisor Playbook is the operating manual for every Certified CoverScore Risk Advisor™. It defines who the advisor is, how they work, the Risk Review conversation, the recommendation framework, communication and CRM standards, the annual review, referral strategy, KPIs, and professional standards.

> **The advisor's promise:** CoverScore does not sell insurance. CoverScore builds resilience.

---

## 2. The Mission

- Build resilience with every client, one conversation at a time.
- Protect what matters: people, assets, income, continuity, and the future.
- Earn trust through competence, honesty, and genuine care.
- Never pressure. Never fear-monger. Never oversell.

---

## 3. The Advisor Operating Model

The platform (engine) performs the heavy lifting before the advisor enters:

1. The prospect is discovered and qualified by the funnel.
2. The assessment is completed by the client (digitally or guided).
3. The engine produces the CoverScore, pillars, intelligence outputs, Advisor Brief, and Report.
4. **The advisor's responsibility begins at the Advisor Brief.**

The advisor:
- Prepares from the Advisor Brief.
- Conducts the Risk Review conversation.
- Agrees a Protection Strategy.
- Manages next steps through the CRM.
- Maintains the relationship through the annual review.

The advisor does **not** manually prospect, qualify, or assess.

---

## 4. Core Functions

1. **Prepare** — study the Advisor Brief, the Report, the client context.
2. **Connect** — open the conversation with trust and purpose.
3. **Ask** — explore the client's concerns, priorities and experiences; ask, don't assume.
4. **Reframe** — connect the client's concerns to the risk intelligence.
5. **Enable** — help the client understand practical next steps and move toward action.

---

## 5. Daily Workflow

1. Review today's meetings from the CRM and Advisor Briefs.
2. Prepare each Risk Review.
3. Conduct the conversations.
4. Log outcomes and next steps in the CRM the same day.
5. Follow up on commitments.
6. Manage the pipeline and referrals.

---

## 6. The Risk Review Conversation

### 6.1 Before the meeting
- Read the Advisor Brief completely.
- Read the CoverScore Report.
- Prepare questions for exploration (from the Advisor Brief preparation notes).
- Confirm logistics and attendees.

### 6.2 During the meeting
Follow the CARE framework:

| Stage | Action |
|-------|--------|
| **Connect** | Welcome, purpose, agenda, time check — establish rapport and context |
| **Ask** | Explore the client's concerns, priorities and experiences — listen deeply |
| **Reframe** | Connect the client's concerns to the risk intelligence; reduction-first |
| **Enable** | Help the client understand next steps, agree the Protection Strategy and ownership |

### 6.3 The structure
1. Open with the client's strength: acknowledge the good.
2. Present the CoverScore and Risk Level in plain language.
3. Work through the pillars, weakest first, at the client's pace.
4. Identify and confirm the Highest Priority together.
5. Discuss Risk Reduction actions.
6. Discuss Risk Transfer options.
7. Agree the Protection Strategy and Recommended First Step.
8. Set next steps, dates, and ownership.
9. Close with the client's next action clear.

---

## 7. Recommendation Framework

| Category | What it is | When |
|----------|-----------|------|
| **Risk Reduction™** | Actions to reduce likelihood/impact | Always presented first |
| **Risk Transfer™** | Transfer to a third party (insurance, contracts) | Presented after reduction; never mixed with reduction |

**Non-negotiables:**
- Never mix Risk Reduction and Risk Transfer in a single recommendation.
- Risk Reduction is always presented before Risk Transfer.
- Every recommendation is specific, actionable, and in resilience language.

---

## 8. Communication Standards

- Plain language. No jargon.
- Client-first: strength before gap.
- Ask, don't assume. Listen more than you speak.
- Never use fear, urgency, or pressure.
- Confirm understanding before moving on.
- Every conversation ends with agreed next steps.

### Words we use
Resilience, protection, preparedness, improvement, priority, planning, confidence, continuity, review.

### Words we avoid
Cheap, expensive, failure, danger, disaster, scary, "must buy", "guaranteed".

---

## 9. CRM Standards

- Log every client interaction the same day.
- Record: date, stage, decisions, actions, next steps.
- Track every recommendation and Protection Strategy item to completion.
- CRM entries are factual and professional.
- CRM notes never contain fear or pressure language.

---

## 10. Annual Review

Every client receives an annual review:
1. Re-assess the entity (new CoverScore).
2. Compare against the previous score.
3. Celebrate improvement; address regression.
4. Update the Protection Strategy.
5. Re-affirm the next year's priorities.

---

## 11. Referral Strategy

- Every satisfied client is invited to refer — naturally, without pressure.
- Referrals are entered into the CRM funnel.
- The advisor never asks for referrals before delivering value.

---

## 12. Advisor KPIs

| KPI | Standard |
|-----|----------|
| Risk Reviews completed | Weekly target |
| Protection Strategies agreed | Conversion standard |
| Next-step follow-through | 100% within agreed time |
| CRM timeliness | Same-day logging |
| Client satisfaction | Monitored via reviews |

---

## 13. Professional Standards

- Honesty before revenue.
- The client's resilience is the measure of success.
- Confidentiality: Advisor Brief is never shared with the client.
- Competence: continuous learning through CCA certification.
- Ethics: no fear, no pressure, no misrepresentation.

### What advisors never do
- Never sell before the advisor is engaged at the Advisor Brief stage.
- Never mix Risk Reduction and Risk Transfer.
- Never invent risks that do not exist.
- Never manufacture urgency.
- Never guarantee outcomes.
- Never share the Advisor Brief with the client.

4. A partner who stays with them through the annual review.
