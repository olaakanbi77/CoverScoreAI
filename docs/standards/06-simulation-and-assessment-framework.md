# CoverScore Advisor Simulation & Assessment Framework™

**Phase:** Phase 1 — School Resilience™ | **Status:** Canonical | **Owner:** CoverScore Academy

---

## 1. Purpose

The Simulation & Assessment Framework defines how Certified CoverScore Risk Advisors™ are practically trained, assessed, and certified. It uses a structured library of client scenarios (SR-001 to SR-010) to test the full advisory skill set — from reading an Advisor Brief through to the agreed Protection Strategy and follow-through.

---

## 2. Entry Point

The advisor's responsibility begins at the **Advisor Brief™**. In real life, the platform handles prospecting, qualification, and assessment. In simulation, the trainee receives exactly what the platform produces:

1. **Advisor Brief™** (confidential, advisor-only).
2. **CoverScore Report™** (client-facing).
3. **Client profile** and context.
4. **CRM notes** (history and context).

Trainees must **not** manually prospect, qualify, or assess.

---

## 3. Simulation Philosophy

- Test **judgement**, not memory.
- Test **understanding**, not script repetition.
- Test **communication**, not speed.
- Test **prioritisation**, not coverage of everything.
- Every simulation mirrors a real client encounter and every exit is the same: an agreed next step.

---

## 4. Architecture — Seven Stages

1. **Preparation** — reading the Advisor Brief and Report; planning the conversation.
2. **Assessment Interpretation** — explaining the CoverScore, bands, pillars, priority.
3. **Discovery & Listening** — CARE Ask: exploring facts; asking; listening; building the picture.
4. **Professional Judgement** — prioritising risks with the client's own evidence.
5. **Recommendations** — Risk Reduction first, Risk Transfer second, never mixed.
6. **Agreement & Next Steps** — the Protection Strategy, dates, ownership.
7. **Documentation & Follow-Through** — CRM logging, follow-up on commitments.

## 5. The 100-Point Scoring Model

| # | Criterion | Marks |
|---|-----------|-------|
| 1 | Preparation | 10 |
| 2 | Assessment Interpretation | 15 |
| 3 | Discovery & Listening | 15 |
| 4 | Professional Judgement | 15 |
| 5 | Risk Prioritisation | 10 |
| 6 | Recommendation Quality | 10 |
| 7 | Communication & Empathy | 10 |
| 8 | Objection Handling | 5 |
| 9 | CRM Documentation | 5 |
| 10 | Next-Step Management | 5 |
| | **Total** | **100** |

### Level descriptors per criterion (example — 15-mark criteria)
- **13–15 (Exceptional):** full command, natural, client-led, evidence-based.
- **10–12 (Good):** strong, minor omissions.
- **7–9 (Adequate):** meets the requirement, some gaps.
- **4–6 (Partial):** significant gaps.
- **0–3 (Not demonstrated):** missed or incorrect.

## 6. Certification Thresholds

| Score | Outcome |
|-------|---------|
| 80 – 100 | **Competent** — certified |
| 70 – 79 | **Conditional** — pass with required coaching |
| 60 – 69 | **Remediation** — repeat assessed scenario after coaching |
| 0 – 59 | **Not Yet Competent** — retrain, re-sit |

---

## 7. Critical Failure Conditions

The following result in **automatic non-certification** for that attempt:

1. Mixing Risk Reduction and Risk Transfer in one recommendation.
2. Presenting Risk Transfer before Risk Reduction.
3. Failing to offer Risk Reduction at all when gaps exist.
4. Inventing risks or facts not present in the scenario.
5. Manufacturing urgency or fear.
6. Sharing or showing the Advisor Brief to the client.
7. Failing to identify the Highest Priority correctly.
8. Leaving the meeting without an agreed next step.

## 9. Scenario Standard Structure

Every scenario in the library is built with the same three-layer structure:

**Layer 1 — Advisor brief** (what the platform produces):
1. Scenario overview
2. Client profile & organisation
3. Assessment snapshot (CoverScore, bands, pillars)
4. Advisor Brief (12 mandatory sections)
5. CRM notes

**Layer 2 — Simulation content** (how the encounter runs):
6. Opening conversation
7. Discovery map (topics to explore)
8. Branching responses (Level 1/2/3)
9. Objections
10. Advisor Intelligence Test pauses
11. Risk Prioritisation Test
12. Recommendation Test

**Layer 3 — Assessment content** (how the candidate is scored):
13. Assessor observation sheet
14. Behavioural indicators
15. Critical errors checklist
16. Scoring triggers (positive/negative)
17. Rubric (per criterion)
18. Performance report
19. Certification decision

---

## 10. The Branching Engine

Each scenario supports three branching levels:

| Level | Behaviour |
|-------|-----------|
| **Level 1 — Guided** | Client reveals facts when asked directly; limited complexity |
| **Level 2 — Independent** | Client requires prompting; some facts hidden; moderate complexity |
| **Level 3 — Adaptive** | Client reveals at own pace; incomplete or conflicting facts; changing mood |

At Level 3, clients can:
- Reject a recommendation politely.
- Reveal a hidden affordability constraint.
- Present a previously unstated fact.
- Shift focus, requiring the advisor to hold the structure.

Branches are defined in each scenario for the key decision points.

---

## 11. Advisor Intelligence Test

Throughout the simulation the advisor is **paused** at key moments with the question:

> **"What should you do next?"**

Conditions:
- Full credit only for the **contextually appropriate** answer.
- Partial credit for a related but secondary answer.
- Zero credit for an action that violates the standards (e.g., jumping to risk transfer, sharing the Advisor Brief).

Pauses occur at: after reading the brief, before opening, after the CoverScore explanation, after discovery, before recommending, before closing.

---

## 12. Risk Prioritisation Test

The candidate is presented with a set of risks (typically 5) and must, for each:

1. State the **priority order**.
2. **Justify** the priority using the client's own evidence from the scenario.
3. Explain the **operational impact**.
4. Explain the **protection implication** (reduction vs transfer — correctly separated).

---

## 13. Recommendation Test

The candidate's recommendations are classified:

| Class | Definition | Scoring |
|-------|-----------|---------|
| Risk Reduction | Action to reduce likelihood/impact | Full credit if prioritised correctly |
| Risk Transfer | Transfer to third party | Credit only after reduction presented |
| Further Investigation | Additional facts needed first | Credit only when genuinely required |
| Not Currently Appropriate | Correctly deferred | Credit when justified |

**Zero credit** for: mixing classes in one item, transfer before reduction, invented risks, unnecessary urgency.

---

## 14. CRM Assessment

Candidates log the meeting in the CRM — assessed on:
- **Completeness** (date, attendees, stage, decisions, actions).
- **Accuracy** (matches what happened in the simulation).
- **Professional tone** (no jargon, no fear).
- **Next-step clarity** (owner, date).

---

## 15. Handoff Test

After the meeting, the candidate must describe the **handoff**: who does what, by when, and what the advisor will do next. Credit for:
- Correct owner for each action.
- Correct timing.
- The advisor's own follow-up commitment.
- Clean handoff of any transfer paperwork to the appropriate next party.

---

## 16. Assessor Observation Sheet

The assessor records per criterion:
- Observed behaviours (evidence).
- Score (using the rubric).
- Notes for feedback.

### Behavioural indicators (examples)

**Exceptional indicators**
- Asked the client before assuming.
- Explained the score in the client's language.
- Prioritised using the client's own facts.
- Presented reduction then transfer, clearly separated.
- Closed with an agreed date-specific next step.

**Weak indicators**
- Lectured instead of listening.
- Jumped to insurance immediately.
- Mixed reduction and transfer.
- Left the meeting without an agreed next step.
- Used fear or pressure language.

---

## 17. Scoring Triggers

### Positive triggers (earn credit)
- "That's a good question" response from the client.
- Discovery of a hidden fact through good questioning.
- Correct identification of the Highest Priority.
- Risk Reduction presented before Risk Transfer.
- Specific, dated next step agreed.

### Negative triggers (lose credit)
- Immediate pitch for insurance.
- Risk Transfer before Risk Reduction.
- Mixing reduction and transfer.
- Missing a hidden fact that was available to be found.
- Showing the Advisor Brief.
- Fear-based language.
- No agreed next step.

## 18. Simulation Progression

| Level | Name | Description |
|-------|------|-------------|
| 1 | **Guided** | Full support; direct facts; training use |
| 2 | **Independent** | Reduced support; some hidden facts |
| 3 | **Adaptive** | Incomplete/conflicting info; changing mood |
| 4 | **Advanced** | Multiple stakeholders; complex decisions |
| 5 | **Certification** | Random scenario + personality; no assistance |

Candidates progress through levels during training and sit Level 5 for certification.

## 19. AI Simulation Version

The same scenario library is available as AI roleplay:
- **AI client** plays the scenario persona (personality, motivation, hidden facts).
- **AI assessor** scores using the same 100-point model and rubric.
- **AI feedback** identifies gaps and recommends coaching.
- **Release levels 1–4** control how much the AI client reveals and resists.

### AI client release levels
| Level | Behaviour |
|-------|-----------|
| 1 | Cooperative; reveals facts on direct question |
| 2 | Cooperative but guarded; some redirection |
| 3 | Resistant; hidden facts only via exploration |
| 4 | Difficult; changing mood; long silences |

### AI scoring triggers (example)
Answer given in AI roleplay is scored against the scenario's gold-standard responses.

## 20. Simulation Record

Each candidate maintains a simulation record:
- Scenario ID and level.
- Date, duration, attempt number.
- Criterion scores and total.
- Critical error flags.
- Certification decision.
- Coaching notes and re-sit plan.

---

## 21. Final Practical Assessment (Certification)

The certification assessment:
1. Assessor selects **one scenario at random** from the library.
2. Candidate receives the Advisor Brief, Report, client profile, CRM notes.
3. 40–45 minutes for the simulated Risk Review.
4. 15 minutes for the CRM documentation task.
5. 10 minutes for the debrief (handoff + reflection).
6. Assessor completes the observation sheet and rubric.
7. Candidate is scored on the 100-point model.

## 23. Competency Model

A certified advisor demonstrates:
1. **Prepare** — read and interpret the Advisor Brief.
2. **Connect** — open with trust and purpose.
3. **Interpret** — explain score, bands, pillars plainly.
4. **Discover** — ask and listen; find the hidden facts.
5. **Prioritise** — right priority, justified with client evidence.
6. **Recommend** — reduction first, transfer second, never mixed.
7. **Agree** — Protection Strategy and next steps.
8. **Document** — complete CRM entries.
9. **Follow through** — commitments kept.

## 24. Final Standard

> "A Certified CoverScore Risk Advisor™ understands the client's world, explains resilience simply, prioritises honestly, recommends reduction before transfer, and always leaves the client with an agreed next step."
