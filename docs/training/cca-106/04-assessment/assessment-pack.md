# CCA 106 — Part 4: Assessment Pack

**Module:** CCA 106 — Risk Advisory Practice, Business Development & Client Growth
**Format:** One live 15-minute client-engagement simulation, scored on 8 parts, 100 points.
**Pass mark:** 80+ Competent (see pass bands below).
**Scenario source:** SR-001 — Flourish College (canonical School Resilience™ library;
assessors may substitute SR-002–SR-010 at comparable difficulty).

---

## 1. Assessment Overview

CCA 106 certifies that the Advisor can **use** CoverScore intelligence: communicate it
professionally, build trust, understand the client's motivations, run a CARE™
conversation, identify legitimate opportunities, develop the relationship, and secure a
clear next step — without reverting to product-first selling.

The certification instrument is a **15-minute live client-engagement simulation** scored
across eight parts, exactly matching the module's competency model:

| Part | Area | Points |
|------|------|--------|
| 1 | Professional Communication | 15 |
| 2 | Trust & Rapport | 10 |
| 3 | Client Motivations | 10 |
| 4 | CARE™ Application | 20 |
| 5 | CoverScore Conversation | 15 |
| 6 | Business Development | 10 |
| 7 | Relationship Growth | 10 |
| 8 | Professional Judgment | 10 |
| | **Total** | **100** |

**Formative rubric:** Lesson 8 uses the 9-category in-lesson simulation rubric
(Professional Communication 15, Trust Building 15, Motivation Discovery 10, CARE™
Framework 20, CoverScore Interpretation 10, Opportunity Identification 10, Business
Development Judgment 10, Relationship Development 5, Next-Step Conversion 5). This
assessment pack is the summative, certification-level instrument.

---

## 2. Assessment Scenario

The learner receives, in advance, the **SR-001 intelligence pack**:

- **Client:** Florence James · **Business:** Flourish College · **Location:** Jos
- **CoverScore:** 18 / 100 · **Risk Level:** Critical
- **Primary Gap:** Property Protection
- Available intelligence: CoverScore Report™, Advisor Brief™, Client Assessment Data,
  Risk Pillars, Risk Story™, Improvement Potential™.

**Task:** Conduct a 15-minute client engagement covering all seven stages (Connect, Ask,
Reframe, Enable, Business Development, Relationship Expansion, Next Step). The assessor
plays the client and must reveal, at a natural point, the personal concern: *"I'm also
concerned about what would happen to my family if I couldn't continue earning."*

The conversation must demonstrate: professional communication, trust building,
motivation discovery, CARE™, risk interpretation, opportunity discovery, business
development, and relationship development.

---

## 3. Part 1 — Professional Communication (15 points)

Observed across the whole engagement.

| Mark | Descriptor |
|------|------------|
| 13–15 | Plain, calm, professional language throughout; zero jargon; every message tied to the client's situation; no fear-based or pressure language anywhere |
| 9–12 | Mostly clear and professional; one or two jargon slips or slightly stiff phrases |
| 5–8 | Frequent jargon or formulaic language; occasional pressure or fear-adjacent phrasing |
| 0–4 | Product/marketing script; unclear or intimidating language dominates |

**Absolutes:** no fear-based urgency, no manufactured catastrophe, no jargon walls.
Every sentence should be a sentence the client could repeat to a friend.

---

## 4. Part 2 — Trust & Rapport (10 points)

| Mark | Descriptor |
|------|------------|
| 9–10 | Competence, consistency, honesty, empathy, reliability all visible; client-led from the first minute; the client visibly relaxes |
| 6–8 | Warm and credible; occasional advisor-led dominance or a soft over-promise |
| 3–5 | Tension or scepticism persists; some exaggeration or unsupported reassurance |
| 0–2 | Pressure, bluff, or disregard for the client's feelings; trust damaged |

**Absolutes:** never over-promise a guarantee, never pretend to know something, never
pressure. Accuracy over convenience.

---

## 5. Part 3 — Client Motivations (10 points)

| Mark | Descriptor |
|------|------------|
| 9–10 | Discovers the client's true decision driver (e.g., student safety → reputation/rebuild/regulation) using discovery questions, and explicitly reflects it |
| 6–8 | Asks discovery questions and picks up the driver, but does not reflect it back or use it later |
| 3–5 | Assumes price or a generic driver; few open questions |
| 0–2 | Assumes motivation and proceeds on it; no discovery of the driver |

**Absolutes:** never assume every client is motivated by price; ask before assuming.
The questions "What concerns you most…?" and "What would be the biggest consequence…?" are
expected.

---

## 6. Part 4 — CARE™ Application (20 points)

The four stages must all appear, in order.

| Stage | Points | Evidence expected |
|-------|--------|-------------------|
| Connect | 5 | Introduces self, confirms purpose, establishes comfort, sets expectations; opens with curiosity |
| Ask | 5 | Explores biggest concern, previous incidents, current protection, priorities, realities, financial concerns |
| Reframe | 5 | Connects the client's words to the CoverScore intelligence honestly (reduction-first) |
| Enable | 5 | Immediate priorities, areas for further review, potential protection areas, and next steps |

**Deduct all Enable points if the learner reaches next steps without completing Ask and
Reframe.** Product-first or self-serving advice is a critical failure (see §11).

---

## 7. Part 5 — CoverScore Conversation (15 points)

| Mark | Descriptor |
|------|------------|
| 13–15 | Explains score, risk level, pillars, Risk Story, and Improvement Potential™ in plain language; treats the score as a starting point, not a diagnosis; reads the client's reaction |
| 9–12 | Accurate explanation; one or two numbers delivered without translation |
| 5–8 | Numbers recited without meaning; the score is treated as a verdict |
| 0–4 | Score used as fear leverage or misrepresented; intelligence presented as certainty |

**Absolutes:** the score is never a diagnosis; AI-generated intelligence is never
presented as professional certainty.

---

## 8. Part 6 — Business Development (10 points)

| Mark | Descriptor |
|------|------------|
| 9–10 | Identifies legitimate opportunities strictly supported by the conversation; applies the one-client rule; no product dumping |
| 6–8 | Relevant opportunities identified; one item introduced without conversational support |
| 3–5 | Product-pulling behaviour; opportunities not earned by the conversation |
| 0–2 | Immediate product pitch or maximum-penetration selling |

**Absolutes:** one client ≠ one policy, and one client ≠ every policy — the objective is
maximum relevance, not maximum product penetration.

---

## 9. Part 7 — Relationship Growth (10 points)

| Mark | Descriptor |
|------|------------|
| 9–10 | Recognises the revealed personal need and offers a separate, respectful personal CoverScore conversation; keeps business and personal decisions distinct |
| 6–8 | Acknowledges the concern and defers appropriately, but less naturally |
| 3–5 | Bundles or casually dismisses the personal concern |
| 0–2 | Ignores the revealed personal need, or pushes a personal product immediately |

**Absolutes:** never mix personal and business decisions; never introduce a personal
solution merely because it exists — the conversation must reveal the need.

---

## 10. Part 8 — Professional Judgment (10 points)

| Mark | Descriptor |
|------|------------|
| 9–10 | Robust judgement throughout: separates operational improvements from financial protection, prioritises honestly, screens opportunities, secures a sensible next step |
| 6–8 | Sound overall; one instance of weak judgement or a forced opportunity |
| 3–5 | Questionable priorities; transfer before reduction; score over-interpreted |
| 0–2 | Dangerous judgement; misleading the client or ignoring evidence |

**Absolutes:** Risk Reduction™ before Risk Transfer™, never mixed; every exit is an
agreed next step.

---

## 11. Critical Failure Conditions

The simulation triggers **remediation** (with mandatory coaching before resit) if the
Advisor:

1. Immediately pitches products.
2. Ignores client concerns.
3. Uses fear to create urgency.
4. Makes unsupported coverage promises.
5. Recommends irrelevant products.
6. Overwhelms the client with products.
7. Ignores personal needs revealed by the client.
8. Attempts to close before understanding the client.
9. Treats the CoverScore score as a diagnosis.
10. Misrepresents AI-generated intelligence as professional certainty.
11. *(Framework absolute)* Presents Risk Transfer before Risk Reduction, or mixes the two.

---

## 12. Scoring Bands

| Total | Verdict |
|-------|---------|
| 80–100 | **Competent** — certified |
| 70–79 | **Conditional Pass** — one targeted re-assessment of the weakest part |
| 60–69 | **Remediation Required** — coaching on the failed parts, then a full re-sit |
| Below 60 | **Not Yet Competent** — full re-sit with mentoring |

Any critical failure (§11) means **not competent**, regardless of total, until coaching
is completed.

## 13. Administration Notes

- **Simulation load:** One fresh SR scenario per attempt. SR-001 is the default;
  assessors may draw SR-002–SR-010 at comparable difficulty. The story be revealed by
  the learner's questioning, not given in advance.
- **Time:** 15-minute engagement, then a 5-minute assessor debrief on the rubric.
- **The client role:** The assessor plays Florence James (or the substitute client),
  revealing the personal family concern at a natural stage if the learner creates space
  — and noting it if the learner does not.
- **Intelligence pack:** The learner receives the cover score, report, brief, pillars,
  Risk Story™, and Improvement Potential™ beforehand. Preparation is part of the skill.
- **Grading:** Two assessors recommended; scores logged per part with evidence quotes.
- **Ethics flags:** Any fear-based language or pressure tactic is recorded as an ethics
  flag and is a coaching priority even where the learner passes.
- **Re-sits:** Maximum two attempts per module cycle; remediation plan required between.
- **Audit:** Score breakdown retained per learner (Academy records).

## 14. Gold-Standard Performance

A strong submission includes all of the following, in order:

1. **Connect** — opens with curiosity, confirms purpose, establishes comfort.
2. **Ask** — explores student safety, previous incidents, current protection, decision
   priorities, and financial concerns.
3. **Reframe** — connects her story to the CoverScore intelligence (Critical Risk,
   Property Protection priority) honestly and plainly.
4. **Enable** — separates operational improvements from remaining financial exposure and
   sequences immediate priorities.
5. **Business Development** — maps only conversation-supported opportunities (e.g.,
   Property/Fire, Liability, Business Interruption for the college).
6. **Relationship Expansion** — responds to the family concern with the expected line:
   *"That's an important concern. Since we're discussing the resilience of the business,
   we can also look separately at your personal financial resilience. I wouldn't want to
   assume what you need, so perhaps we can explore that through the appropriate personal
   CoverScore assessment."*
7. **Next Step** — secures an agreed next action (e.g., a focused Risk Review to
   validate findings and decide which actions are operational and which need financial
   protection).

**Debrief note:** notice what the gold standard does not do — it does not sell first,
read the report word-for-word, or assume the client's priorities.
