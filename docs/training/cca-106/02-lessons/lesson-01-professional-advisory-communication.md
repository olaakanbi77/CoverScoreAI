# CCA 106 — Lesson 1: Professional Advisory Communication

**Module:** CCA 106 — Risk Advisory Practice, Business Development & Client Growth
**Video duration:** ~45 minutes | **Workshop:** ~25 minutes | **Independent:** ~15 minutes

---

## 1. Lesson Overview

This opening lesson reframes how a CoverScore Advisor speaks. Learners see the
difference between a conventional insurance seller and a professional Risk Advisor —
starting with the very first sentence. The lesson establishes the five communication
principles, professional language that replaces jargon and fear, and a rule the whole
module depends on: the Advisor starts with the client, not with a product.

## 2. Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, learners will be able to:

1. Distinguish professional advisory communication from conventional insurance selling.
2. Apply the five communication principles: Clear, Relevant, Curious, Professional, Useful.
3. Translate technical and negative messages into calm, plain client language.
4. Avoid fear-based communication while remaining honest about exposure.
5. Recognise and select professional advisory language in real scenarios.

## 5. Learner Handbook

### 5.1 Product-first vs client-first

The conventional seller begins with a product catalogue and leads with what they want
to sell. The Risk Advisor begins with the client's situation — their assessment, their
concerns, their exposure. The opening line sets everything that follows: "I've reviewed
your assessment" is an invitation to a conversation; "I'd like to introduce some
products" is an announcement of a pitch.

### 5.2 The five communication principles

- **Be Clear** — avoid unnecessary jargon. If a term needs explaining, replace it.
- **Be Relevant** — connect every comment to the client's own situation and context.
- **Be Curious** — ask before assuming; discovery beats guesswork.
- **Be Professional** — no pressure, no manipulation, no false urgency.
- **Be Useful** — every interaction should leave the client better equipped to decide.

### 5.3 Professional language

A score is a starting point, not a verdict. When a client says they don't understand
their CoverScore, do not make the number sound like a sentence. Lead with the areas that
contribute to the score and which deserve attention first. The same honesty, delivered
calmly, builds the foundation for action.

### 5.4 Fear-based communication and the alternative

Never manufacture urgency with hypothetical catastrophe. The professional alternative
states the exposure plainly and moves to practical measures and financial protection.
Both messages can be honest; only one respects the client.

### 5.5 Communication principles and CARE™

These principles support the CARE™ framework (Connect → Ask → Reframe → Enable) taught
in Lesson 4. Clear, relevant, curious, professional, useful communication is the surface
of every CARE stage.

## 6. Practical Exercises

**Exercise 1.1 — Rewrite the opening (15 min)**
Take the conventional seller's line ("We have some excellent insurance products I would
like to introduce to you") and rewrite it as a professional Risk Advisor opening for the
SR-001 client (Florence James, Flourish College, Jos). Read both aloud; compare the
feeling each creates.

**Exercise 1.2 — Translate the jargon (15 min)**
Convert five engine-style phrases into client language: "CoverScore 27 — Critical
Risk"; "Protection Gap NGN 40M"; "Highest Priority: Property Protection"; "low-moderate
confidence"; "Exposure Index high". Write one calm, plain sentence for each.

**Exercise 1.3 — Fear audit (10 min)**
Write down three fear-based sentences an inexperienced seller might use. Rewrite each
using the professional pattern from Scene 4: plain exposure + practical measures +
financial protection.

## 7. Case Study

**Scenario:** Florence James, Proprietress of Flourish College (Jos), is meeting her
CoverScore Advisor for the first time. Her CoverScore is 18/100 — Critical Risk — with
Property Protection the lowest pillar. She opens with: "I don't really understand what
my CoverScore means. And I'm terrified something will happen to the school."

**Tasks:**
1. Apply Scene 3's "better response" to her score — write what you would say about 18/100.
2. Apply Scene 4's professional pattern to one real exposure in her report (no fire
   insurance; untested alarm) without resorting to fear.
3. Identify which of the five principles you used in each line, and mark any line that
   crossed into pressure or jargon.

   a. "You need this policy." *(incorrect)*
   b. "Everyone in your industry buys this policy." *(incorrect)*
   c. "Your assessment identifies this exposure. Let's understand its potential impact and determine what response makes sense." *(correct)*
   d. "If you don't act now you will regret it." *(incorrect)*

2. Which of the five communication principles says "ask before assuming"?
   a. Be Clear *(incorrect)*
   b. Be Relevant *(incorrect)*
   c. Be Curious *(correct)*
   d. Be Useful *(incorrect)*

3. A client says, "I don't understand my CoverScore." The professional response:
   a. "Your score is 27, so your risk is very high." *(incorrect)*
   b. "The number isn't the most important thing — what matters is understanding the areas behind it and deciding which deserve attention first." *(correct)*
   c. "Don't worry, you can improve it." *(incorrect)*
   d. "Let me explain how insurance works." *(incorrect)*

4. Which phrase is fear-based?
   a. "Your assessment indicates a major property loss could create significant financial pressure." *(incorrect)*
   b. "If you don't insure your school, one fire could destroy everything." *(correct)*
   c. "Let's examine the exposure and consider what response makes sense." *(incorrect)*
   d. "The report points us toward a few practical actions." *(incorrect)*

   a. Always present a quotation. *(incorrect)*

**Answers:** 1-c, 2-c, 3-b, 4-b, 5-b.

## 9. AI Tutor Knowledge Base

- **KB-1.1 Professional advisory definition:** The Risk Advisor starts with the client,
  not the product. Communication is client-first, plain, calm, and honest — a departure
  from conventional product-led selling.
- **KB-1.2 The five principles:** Clear (no jargon), Relevant (tied to the client's
  situation), Curious (ask before assuming), Professional (no pressure), Useful (every
  interaction creates value).
- **KB-1.3 Scores are starting points:** A CoverScore is diagnostic, not a verdict.
  Lead with which areas contribute to the score and which deserve attention first — the
  same honesty as quoting a low number, tempered with usefulness.
- **KB-1.4 Fear test:** A message is fear-based when it manufactures urgency or
  catastrophe without evidence. Replace it with: plain statement of exposure + practical
  measures + appropriate financial protection.
- **KB-1.5 Module case (SR-001):** Flourish College, Jos — Florence James, Proprietress.
  CoverScore 18/100, Critical Risk; primary gap Property Protection. CCA 106 reuses this
  client for communication, trust, motivation, and simulation practice.

## 12. Final Takeaway

Professional communication is not about sounding impressive. It is about making complex
risk understandable and helping the client make better decisions. Master this, and
trust — the subject of the next lesson — has a place to grow.
