# CCA 106 — Lesson 7: Growing Long-Term Client Relationships

**Module:** CCA 106 — Risk Advisory Practice, Business Development & Client Growth
**Video duration:** ~45 minutes | **Workshop:** ~25 minutes | **Independent:** ~15 minutes

---

## 1. Lesson Overview

This lesson moves from individual transactions to long-term client relationships.
Learners study the transaction model they must leave behind, the advisory model that
replaces it, the eight-stage client life cycle, value-based contact that keeps clients
engaged between renewals, and how retention is earned — not requested.

## 2. Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, learners will be able to:

1. Contrast the transactional and advisory relationship models.
2. Map a client through the eight-stage life cycle.
3. Identify value-contact moments beyond renewal, payment, and new products.
4. Respond to growth events (e.g., a new branch) with a reassessment mindset.
5. Handle the cheaper-renewal objection professionally.
6. Conduct the annual value conversation that reassesses and adapts.

## 5. Learner Handbook

### 5.1 Two models of relationship

The transactional model loops: buy, renew, repeat. The advisory model grows:
Assess → Advise → Protect → Monitor → Reassess → Adapt → Grow. One paper-thin, the
other continuously informed.

### 5.2 The client life cycle

Prospect → Assessment → Risk Review → Solution → Implementation → Follow-up →
Reassessment → Relationship Expansion. Each stage has work the advisor owns; the cycle
is designed to repeat and expand.

### 5.3 Value after the sale

"A client relationship becomes valuable when the Advisor remains useful after the
original sale." Value between renewals is what makes the relationship durable.

### 5.4 Value-contact moments

Contact clients when a material risk changes, a business expands, new assets are
acquired, staff increase, a new location opens, an incident occurs, a major regulatory
requirement changes, a reassessment is due, or a relevant personal need emerges. These
replace the tired triggers of renewal, arrears, and new products.

### 5.5 Responding to growth

When the client shares growth ("we've just opened another branch"), celebrate it — then
reassess. Growth changes the risk profile; existing protection may no longer reflect the
business.

### 5.6 Retention and cheaper renewals

When a client announces a cheaper offer, do not argue price. Compare what has actually
changed: scope of protection, limits, exclusions, and service. Price is a signal that a
value conversation is due, not a crisis to be won.

### 5.7 The annual value conversation

At each annual review, reassess what has changed since the last assessment. Never assume
last year's protection still fits. The annual conversation is a reassessment, not a
renewal pitch.

## 6. Practical Exercises

**Exercise 7.1 — Two models (15 min)**
Draw the transactional loop and the advisory spiral. For the advisory model, write one
action the advisor owns at each stage.

**Exercise 7.2 — Life cycle map (15 min)**
Map a real or imagined client through the eight stages. Write the value conversation
that moves them from Follow-up to Reassessment.

**Exercise 7.3 — Contact calendar (15 min)**
Build a 12-month contact plan for a school client using only value-contact moments
(no renewals/arrears/product announcements).

**Exercise 7.4 — The cheaper-renewal conversation (15 min)**
Role-play a client with a cheaper offer from a competitor. Hold the Scene 7 response —
compare scope, limits, exclusions, and service.

## 7. Case Study

**Scenario:** Flourish College completed its first engagement a year ago (SR-001,
CoverScore 18). Since then the school opened a second branch, bought a minibus, and grew
staff. The proprietress says, "Another company offered us a much cheaper renewal."

**Tasks:**
1. Identify the changed risk areas since last year.
2. Write your Annual Value Conversation opening (Scene 8) for Florence.
3. Write your response to the cheaper-renewal claim (Scene 7).
4. Describe how you would move the relationship to Reassessment and Relationship
   Expansion stages.
