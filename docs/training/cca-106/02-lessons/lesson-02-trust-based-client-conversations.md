# CCA 106 — Lesson 2: Trust-Based Client Conversations

**Module:** CCA 106 — Risk Advisory Practice, Business Development & Client Growth
**Video duration:** ~45 minutes | **Workshop:** ~25 minutes | **Independent:** ~15 minutes

---

## 1. Lesson Overview

This lesson teaches how credibility and trust are earned before any attempt to
influence a client's decision. Learners explore the five pillars of trust, the anatomy
of the first minutes of a conversation, a disciplined listening sequence, and how to
recover trust when a client challenges the advisor directly.

## 2. Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, learners will be able to:

1. Explain how trust is built through competence, consistency, honesty, empathy, and reliability.
2. Open a conversation with curiosity instead of a product.
3. Apply the listening sequence: Listen → Clarify → Confirm → Respond.
4. Distinguish poor from professional active listening.
5. Respond to trust-challenging questions without over-promising or bluffing.

## 5. Learner Handbook

### 5.1 The five pillars of trust

- **Competence** — the client believes you can do the job and understand the subject.
- **Consistency** — your behaviour and message stay steady across interactions.
- **Honesty** — you are accurate even when accuracy is inconvenient.
- **Empathy** — you understand the client's situation from their side.
- **Reliability** — you do what you say, when you say.

### 5.2 Trust precedes influence

Trust is the currency of the advisory conversation. A client does not act because you
know the most products; they act when they believe you understand their situation and
have their interests in mind. Attempting influence before trust is established reads as
pressure.

### 5.3 The first minutes

Open before solutions. Ask what concerns the client most about the risks in their
report, then follow their answer. "That's helpful. What makes [X] the biggest concern
for you?" creates space for the client to speak — the marker of a real conversation.

### 5.4 The listening sequence

**Listen** — hear fully, without planning your reply. **Clarify** — test your
understanding. **Confirm** — check the client agrees with your understanding.
**Respond** — only now offer your response. This sequence prevents premature advising.

### 5.5 Active listening in practice

The poor response ("That's why you need Public Liability") jumps straight to product.
The professional response reflects what was said and asks which concern is primary:
safety, financial consequences, or reputation. Precision shows you were actually
listening.

### 5.6 Trust breakers and recovery

Avoid exaggeration, false urgency, unsupported promises, misleading comparisons,
unnecessary jargon, pretending to know what you don't, and pressure. When trust is
challenged (e.g., "Can you guarantee cover?"), do not bluff. Be accurate about policy
terms and exclusions and commit to explaining what is being proposed.

## 6. Practical Exercises

**Exercise 2.1 — The trust audit (15 min)**
Write the five pillars of trust. For each, give one concrete behaviour you can
demonstrate in a client conversation this week.

**Exercise 2.2 — Open without selling (15 min)**
Write a 45-second opening for Florence James (SR-001) that creates space for her to
speak before any solution is mentioned. No product names allowed.

**Exercise 2.3 — Poor vs professional (15 min)**
For each of three client statements, write a "poor" response (jumps to product) and a
"professional" response (reflect + clarify). Compare with a partner.

**Exercise 2.4 — The guarantee challenge (10 min)**
Role-play the client asking "Can you guarantee this will cover us?" Deliver the trust
recovery response without making a single unsupported promise.

## 7. Case Study

**Scenario:** Florence James (Flourish College, CoverScore 18, Critical Risk) is anxious.
She says, "We've had accidents before and I'm worried about what could happen. And quite
frankly, can you even guarantee your insurance will cover us?"

**Tasks:**
1. Identify which pillars of trust are at stake in her question.
2. Write the response that reflects her concern (Scene 4) before answering the guarantee.
3. Deliver the trust-recovery response (Scene 6) — accurate, no unsupported promises.
4. Note any temptation to exaggerate the school's Critical Risk to "close fast" — and
   explain why resisting it is exactly what builds the relationship.

## 8. Knowledge Check

1. Which statement best describes how clients trust an Advisor?
   a. Because the Advisor knows the most products *(incorrect)*
   b. Because the Advisor understands their situation and has their interests in mind *(correct)*
   c. Because the Advisor has the best prices *(incorrect)*
   d. Because the Advisor talks the most *(incorrect)*

2. The listening sequence in order is:
   a. Respond → Clarify → Confirm → Listen *(incorrect)*
   b. Listen → Clarify → Confirm → Respond *(correct)*
   c. Clarify → Listen → Respond → Confirm *(incorrect)*
   d. Listen → Respond → Clarify → Confirm *(incorrect)*

3. The client says, "We've had accidents before and I'm worried." The professional response:
   a. "That's why you need Public Liability." *(incorrect)*
   b. "When you say worried, is your main concern student safety, the financial consequences, the reaction from parents, or all three?" *(correct)*
   c. "This is why the score is critical." *(incorrect)*
   d. "Don't worry, we'll sort it out." *(incorrect)*

4. Which is a trust breaker?
   a. Confirming the client's words back to them *(incorrect)*
   b. Making an unsupported promise about coverage *(correct)*
   c. Asking what concerns them most *(incorrect)*
   d. Checking policy exclusions before answering *(incorrect)*

5. A client asks "Can you guarantee this will cover us?" The professional response:
   a. "Yes, 100% guaranteed." *(incorrect)*
   b. "Trust me, you're fully covered." *(incorrect)*
   c. "I wouldn't guarantee without reviewing the specific policy terms and circumstances — let me explain the protection and the key terms and exclusions." *(correct)*
   d. "All policies cover everything." *(incorrect)*

**Answers:** 1-b, 2-b, 3-b, 4-b, 5-c.

## 9. AI Tutor Knowledge Base

- **KB-2.1 Trust model:** Trust = Competence + Consistency + Honesty + Empathy +
  Reliability. Clients trust advisors who understand their situation and hold their
  interests in mind.
- **KB-2.2 Trust precedes influence:** Any attempt to influence before trust is in place
  reads as pressure. Open with curiosity, not solutions.
- **KB-2.3 Listening sequence:** Listen, Clarify, Confirm, Respond. Never respond before
  listening and confirming understanding.
- **KB-2.4 Active listening:** Reflect the client's words, then clarify which concern is
  primary. The "poor" pattern jumps straight to a product suggestion.
- **KB-2.5 Trust breakers:** exaggeration, false urgency, unsupported promises,
  misleading comparisons, unnecessary jargon, pretending to know, pressure. Avoid all.
- **KB-2.6 Trust recovery:** When challenged, be accurate — explain the protection being
  considered, the key terms and exclusions, and never over-promise a guarantee.

## 11. Lesson Completion Standard

This lesson is complete when learners can:
- Explain the five pillars of trust and how each shows up in conversation.
- Open a conversation with curiosity before any solution.
- Apply Listen → Clarify → Confirm → Respond.
- Turn a trust challenge into an accurate, non-defensive response.
- Pass the knowledge check at 80% or higher.
