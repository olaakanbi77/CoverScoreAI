# CCA 106 — Lesson 6: Business Development Using CoverScore™

**Module:** CCA 106 — Risk Advisory Practice, Business Development & Client Growth
**Video duration:** ~45 minutes | **Workshop:** ~25 minutes | **Independent:** ~15 minutes

## 1. Lesson Overview

This lesson turns CoverScore into a business-development engine without turning the
platform into a product-selling machine. Learners contrast the old product-led funnel
with the risk-led CoverScore model, learn how to prepare from an Advisor Brief, map
opportunities only where the conversation supports them, and build a referral engine
embedded in trust.

## 2. Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, learners will be able to:

1. Contrast the old product-pitch pipeline with CoverScore business development.
2. Read a qualified lead from the Advisor Brief™ and prepare to discover.
3. Map business and personal opportunities only where the conversation supports them.
4. Apply the one-client rule: not one policy, and not every policy — maximum relevance.
5. Turn a satisfied conversation into a natural referral.

## 5. Learner Handbook

### 5.1 Two models of business development

The old model moves down a product funnel: cold list, pitch, quote, follow-up, sale. The
CoverScore model moves through risk: concern → assessment → intelligence → human review →
advisory conversation → opportunity mapping → solution → relationship. The first hunts
for sales; the second solves problems.

### 5.2 The qualified lead

The Advisor Brief™ arrives only after the client has completed an assessment and asked
for help. Example: a school proprietor with CoverScore 22, primary concern student
safety, gaps in emergency procedures, liability, and property protection. The lead is
already engaged — the advisor's job is to advise, not re-prospect.

### 5.3 Advisor preparation — four questions

- **What do I know?** — the assessment data.
- **What don't I know?** — client motivations and context.
- **What should I discover?** — priority, urgency, decision process, budget, existing
  protection.
- **What opportunities may exist?** — only those supported by the conversation.

### 5.4 Opportunity mapping

For a business client: Public Liability, Property/Fire, Motor, Business Interruption,
Group Life, Group Personal Accident, HMO/healthcare, and other relevant commercial
protection. For a personal client: Income Protection, Retirement Readiness, Young
Professional Protection, Entrepreneur Protection. These are possibilities — the
conversation decides which are relevant.

### 5.5 The one-client rule

One client is not one policy — relevant needs exist across the relationship. But one
client is also not every policy. The objective is maximum relevance, not maximum product
penetration. Every mapped opportunity must be earned by the conversation.

### 5.6 The referral engine

Referrals grow naturally from delivered value. When a client mentions a peer's concern,
respond lightly: "If you think it would be useful, I'd be happy to have a similar
conversation with them."

## 6. Practical Exercises

**Exercise 6.1 — Old vs new funnel (15 min)**
Draw both funnels side by side. For each stage of the new funnel, write what the advisor
does to protect it from becoming product-led.

**Exercise 6.2 — Brief preparation (15 min)**
Using the Advisor Brief from Scene 3, complete the four preparation questions: what you
know, don't know, should discover, and opportunities that could exist.

**Exercise 6.3 — Relevance scoring (15 min)**
For a school client with the Scene 3 gaps, list the mapped opportunities. Flag each as
conversation-supported or not, and remove the unsupported ones.

**Exercise 6.4 — Referral line practice (10 min)**
Practise the referral line in partners until it sounds relaxed, not rehearsed.

## 7. Case Study

**Scenario:** An Advisor Brief arrives for a school proprietor (Flourish College, Jos).
CoverScore 18 (Critical Risk); primary gaps include property protection, emergency
procedures, and liability. The client completed the assessment and requested help.

**Tasks:**
1. Answer the four preparation questions for this lead.
2. Map the opportunities the conversation could legitimately support (business side).
3. Apply the one-client rule: which mapped items stay, which go?
4. At the end of a successful Risk Review, Florence mentions her business partner runs a
   small transport company and is worried about fleet risks. Write your referral line.

  solution → relationship.
- **KB-6.2 Qualified lead:** A lead who completed the assessment and requested help —
  e.g., school proprietor, CoverScore 22, concern student safety, gaps in emergency
