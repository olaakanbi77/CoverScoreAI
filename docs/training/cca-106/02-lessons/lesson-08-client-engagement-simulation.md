# CCA 106 — Lesson 8: Client Engagement Simulation

**Module:** CCA 106 — Risk Advisory Practice, Business Development & Client Growth
**Video duration:** ~45 minutes | **Workshop:** ~90 minutes (full simulation) | **Independent:** ~30 minutes

---

## 1. Lesson Overview

This final lesson demonstrates the learner's ability to conduct an end-to-end CoverScore
advisory conversation. Using the module's client — Florence James and Flourish College —
learners run a 15-minute client engagement covering professional communication, trust,
motivation discovery, CARE™, risk interpretation, opportunity discovery, business
development, and relationship development. The lesson closes the module, introduces the
certification assessment, and marks the transition from selling to advising.

## 2. Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, learners will be able to:

1. Prepare from the CoverScore Report™ and Advisor Brief™ before the conversation.
2. Run a 15-minute client engagement through the seven stages: Connect → Ask → Reframe →
   Enable → Business Development → Relationship Expansion → Next Step.
3. Recognise and respond to a revealed personal need without product dumping.
4. Secure an appropriate next action at the end of the conversation.
5. Self-assess a performance against the simulation scoring rubric and the critical
   failure conditions.

**ADVISOR**

> "I understand. What happened?"

**FLORENCE**

> *(Client explains.)*

**ADVISOR**

> "Thank you for explaining that. It helps put the assessment into context.
>
> Your report identifies Student Safety and Property Protection as important areas, but I'd like to understand how you're currently managing those risks."

**FLORENCE**

> *(Client responds.)*

**ADVISOR**

> "Based on what you've told me, I think there are two separate things we should consider.
>
> First, there are operational improvements that can reduce the likelihood of incidents.
>
> Second, there are financial consequences that may still remain even after those improvements, and we should review whether appropriate protection is in place."

**FLORENCE**

> "What would you recommend?"

**ADVISOR**

> "I'd recommend that we start with a focused Risk Review. We'll validate the key findings, understand the exposures in more detail and then determine which actions should be operational and which may require financial protection."

## 5. Learner Handbook

### 5.1 The module competency model

CCA 106's competency model connects the Academy: CCA 105 interprets the intelligence;
CCA 106 uses the intelligence; CCA 107 manages the relationship; CCA 108 demonstrates
the complete advisory cycle. This lesson is the capstone of "using the intelligence."

### 5.2 The simulation case and intelligence pack

The simulation reuses SR-001: Florence James, Flourish College, Jos, CoverScore 18/100,
Critical Risk, primary gap Property Protection. The advisor receives the CoverScore
Report™, Advisor Brief™, client assessment data, risk pillars, Risk Story™, and
Improvement Potential™.

### 5.3 The seven stages

1. **Connect** — introduce yourself, confirm purpose, establish comfort, set expectations.
2. **Ask** — biggest concern, previous incidents, current protection, decision priorities,
   operational realities, financial concerns.
3. **Reframe** — connect the client's responses to the CoverScore intelligence.
4. **Enable** — immediate priorities, areas for further review, potential protection
   areas, next steps.
5. **Business Development** — identify relevant opportunities without product dumping.
6. **Relationship Expansion** — recognise a revealed personal need and offer a separate,
   appropriate personal CoverScore conversation.
7. **Next Step** — secure an appropriate next action (Risk Review, review, quotation
   preparation, personal assessment, follow-up, referral, reassessment).

### 5.4 The gold standard

The gold-standard exchange shows what a strong performance looks like: the advisor opens
with curiosity, follows the client's story, reframes it against the intelligence,
separates operational improvements from financial protection, and recommends a focused
Risk Review. Note specifically what the advisor does not do — sell, read the report
word-for-word, or assume priorities.

### 5.5 The simulation scoring rubric

| Area | Points |
|------|--------|
| Professional Communication | 15 |
| Trust Building | 15 |
| Motivation Discovery | 10 |
| CARE™ Framework | 20 |
| CoverScore Interpretation | 10 |
| Opportunity Identification | 10 |
| Business Development Judgment | 10 |
| Relationship Development | 5 |
| Next-Step Conversion | 5 |
| **Total** | **100** |

### 5.6 Critical failures

Remediation is triggered if the Advisor:

- immediately pitches products;
- ignores client concerns;
- uses fear to create urgency;
- makes unsupported coverage promises;
- recommends irrelevant products;
- overwhelms the client with products;
- ignores personal needs revealed by the client;
- attempts to close before understanding the client;
- treats the CoverScore score as a diagnosis;
- misrepresents AI-generated intelligence as professional certainty.

### 5.7 The certification assessment

The module assessment (see `04-assessment/assessment-pack.md`) scores the same
competencies across eight parts — Professional Communication 15, Trust & Rapport 10,
Client Motivations 10, CARE™ Application 20, CoverScore Conversation 15, Business
Development 10, Relationship Growth 10, Professional Judgment 10 — totalling 100
points, with the standard pass bands: 80–100 Competent, 70–79 Conditional Pass, 60–69
Remediation Required, below 60 Not Yet Competent.

## 6. Practical Exercises

**Exercise 8.1 — Prepare the engagement (20 min)**
From the SR-001 intelligence pack, complete a one-page preparation: what you know, what
you will Ask, what you expect to Reframe, and what you will Enable. No product list yet.

**Exercise 8.2 — Seven-stage flow (20 min)**
Write a 15-minute conversation outline using all seven stages with timings. Mark where
each stage begins and ends.

**Exercise 8.3 — Relationship expansion response (15 min)**
Write and practise the expected response to the personal-need reveal. Use the script's
line, then personalise it.

**Exercise 8.4 — Peer simulation (30 min)**
Run the 15-minute simulation with a partner. Self-assess against the 9-category rubric,
then receive their feedback.
