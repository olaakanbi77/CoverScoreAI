# CCA 106 — Instructor Guide (Part 3)

**Module:** CCA 106 — Risk Advisory Practice, Business Development & Client Growth

## 1. Your Role

You are the instructor for the module that turns CoverScore intelligence into human
advisory skill. Your job is to model the professional, trust-based, motivation-centred
communication the module teaches, and to protect the room from product-first habits.
Learners enter with CCA 105 interpretation skills; you take them to "use the
intelligence" (CCA 106) before they move to "manage the relationship" (CCA 107).

## 2. Session Plan (workshops)

| Lesson | Workshop | Duration | Activity |
|--------|----------|----------|----------|
| 1 | Professional Communication | ~25 min | Opening-line rewrites; jargon audit; fear-free language clinic |
| 2 | Trust Building | ~25 min | First-minutes role-play; trust-recovery drill |
| 3 | Client Motivations | ~25 min | Motivation interview; price-objection role-play |
| 4 | CARE™ Framework | ~25 min | Stage tagging; four-line CARE™ build for SR-001 |
| 5 | Insights to Conversation | ~25 min | Report-opening practice; personal-funnel responses |
| 6 | Business Development | ~25 min | Four-question prep; relevance mapping with the one-client rule |
| 7 | Long-Term Relationships | ~25 min | Value-contact calendar; cheaper-renewal role-play |
| 8 | Client Engagement Simulation | ~90 min | Full 15-minute simulations scored on the 9-category rubric |

## 3. Room Setup

- Breakout triads: one advisor, one client, one observer/scorekeeper rotating each round.
- Shared artefacts: CARE™ diagram, one-client rule card, critical-failure poster, SR-001
  intelligence pack.
- Strict timekeeping for the 15-minute engagement; observers record stage transitions.
- A "stop" signal for critical failures, so a learner who pitches first can restart and
  learn in the moment.

## 4. Facilitation Script (debrief template)

After every simulation or role-play, run the same five questions:

1. What did the Advisor do well? (name it specifically)
2. Which CARE™ stages appeared, in order? Which were skipped or rushed?
3. Where did the Advisor listen versus where did they assume?
4. Was any personal need handled separately, respectfully, and without bundling?
5. What is the one thing to change for the next round?

Use the critical-failure list to highlight moments that would fail the assessment.

## 5. Common Learner Errors and Coaching

| Error | Coaching intervention |
|-------|------------------------|
| Opens with a product line | Replay the Lesson 1 seller/advisor split; redo the opening from the client's concern |
| Jumps to "you need this policy" | Stop the scene; demand discovery questions first (Lesson 3) |
| Uses fear to create urgency | Name it; rephrase with the "plain exposure + measures + protection" pattern |
| Assumes motivation is price | Run the same-risk/different-driver drill until drivers are discovered |
| Skips Connect or Reframe in CARE™ | Tag every line; rebuild the sequence until all four stages appear |
| Bundles personal and business needs | Rehearse the Lesson 5/8 personal-funnel response verbatim |
| Product dumps | Apply the one-client rule; remove every unsupported mapped opportunity |
| Argues a cheaper renewal | Drill the scope/limits/exclusions/service response (Lesson 7) |

## 6. Assessment Logistics

Use the module assessment in `04-assessment/assessment-pack.md`. Book one-on-one 15-minute
client-engagement simulations (assessor plays the client). Score the 8-part rubric,
flag any critical failure, and provide feedback within 48 hours. Conditional passes
(70–79) re-assess only the weakest part; a critical failure always mandates coaching.
