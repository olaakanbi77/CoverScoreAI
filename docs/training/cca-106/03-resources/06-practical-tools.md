# CCA 106 — Practical Tools (Part 3)

**Module:** CCA 106 — Risk Advisory Practice, Business Development & Client Growth

Practical, reusable tools for the working advisor and the training room.

## Tool 1 — The Five Communication Principles Card

Keep on the desk during any client call:

1. **Be Clear** — no unnecessary jargon.
2. **Be Relevant** — connect to the client's situation.
3. **Be Curious** — ask before assuming.
4. **Be Professional** — no pressure.
5. **Be Useful** — every interaction creates value.

## Tool 2 — The Fear-Free Pattern

Replace fear-based messages with: **plain exposure** + **practical measures** +
**appropriate financial protection**.

- Fear: "If you don't insure your school, one fire could destroy everything."
- Professional: "Your assessment indicates a major property loss could create
  significant financial pressure. Let's examine the exposure and consider practical
  measures and financial protection."

## Tool 3 — The CARE™ Card

**C** Connect (rapport and context) → **A** Ask (concerns, priorities, experiences) →
**R** Reframe (connect concerns to risk intelligence) → **E** Enable (practical next
steps and action).

Memory device: *Connect before you advise. Ask before you assume. Reframe before you
recommend. Enable before you close.*

## Tool 4 — The Four Discovery Questions

- "What concerns you most about this exposure?"
- "What would be the biggest consequence if this happened?"
- "What would you most want to protect?"
- "What would make you comfortable with the proposed approach?"

## Tool 5 — The Four Preparation Questions (from the Advisor Brief)

- **What do I know?** — assessment data.
- **What don't I know?** — client motivations and context.
- **What should I discover?** — priority, urgency, decision process, budget, existing protection.
- **What opportunities may exist?** — only those supported by the conversation.

## Tool 6 — The One-Client Rule Card

> ONE CLIENT ≠ ONE POLICY · ONE CLIENT ≠ EVERY POLICY
> The objective is maximum relevance, not maximum product penetration.

## Tool 7 — The Reference Lines Card

- **Personal need:** "That's an important concern. We can also look separately at your
  personal financial resilience. I wouldn't want to assume what you need."
- **Cheaper renewal:** "Let's compare what has actually changed — scope of protection,
  limits, exclusions, and the service you're receiving."
- **Referral:** "If you think it would be useful, I'd be happy to have a similar
  conversation with them."
- **Growth event:** "Let's review what has changed so your existing protection still
  reflects the business."

## Tool 8 — The Critical-Failure Poster

Post in the training room. The simulation/assessment fails (until coaching) if the
Advisor: pitches first · ignores client concerns · uses fear · makes unsupported
promises · recommends irrelevant products · overwhelms with products · ignores revealed
personal needs · closes before understanding · treats the score as a diagnosis ·
presents AI intelligence as certainty.

## Tool 9 — The Value-Contact Calendar

Contact clients when: a material risk changes · a business expands · new assets are
acquired · staff numbers increase · a new location opens · an incident occurs · a major
regulatory requirement changes · a reassessment is due · a relevant personal need
emerges. Never *only* at renewal, arrears, or new-product time.
