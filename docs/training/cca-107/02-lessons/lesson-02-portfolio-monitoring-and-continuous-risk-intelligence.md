# CCA 107 — Lesson 2: Portfolio Monitoring & Continuous Risk Intelligence

**Module:** CCA 107 — Professional Risk Advisory Practice & Client Portfolio Management
**Video duration:** ~45 minutes | **Workshop:** ~25 minutes | **Independent:** ~15 minutes

---

## 1. Lesson Overview

This lesson teaches how to remain continuously aware of changes affecting client risk.
Learners see that risk is never static — it moves whenever the client's circumstances
move. The lesson defines what the advisor should monitor (business, personal, and risk
changes), how CoverScore continuous intelligence produces risk-change signals, and the
monitoring cadence appropriate to high-priority, standard, and stable clients.

## 2. Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, learners will be able to:

1. Explain why risk changes whenever client circumstances change.
2. List the business, personal, and risk changes the advisor should monitor.
3. Follow the continuous-intelligence flow: new information → signal → review → engagement.
4. Respond to a risk-change alert with a resilience review, not a quotation.
5. Set a monitoring cadence for high-priority, standard, and stable clients.

## 5. Learner Handbook

### 5.1 Risk is never static

Risk changes whenever the client's circumstances change. A school that grows from 100 to
500 students, adds buses, opens a campus, and hires staff is a different risk in Year 3
than in Year 1 — even if the policy list looks the same.

### 5.2 What to monitor

- **Business changes:** expansion, new locations, new equipment, new vehicles, new
  employees, new activities, acquisitions, changes in revenue.
- **Personal changes:** marriage, dependants, income changes, career changes, retirement
  plans, major assets.
- **Risk changes:** incidents, claims, near misses, regulatory changes, new operational
  hazards.

### 5.3 The continuous-intelligence flow

**Client → new information → CoverScore Intelligence → Risk Change Signal → Advisor
Review → Client Engagement.** New information feeds intelligence; intelligence produces
signals; signals trigger review; review triggers the right engagement.

### 5.4 Responding to a signal

When a risk-change alert arrives (e.g., expansion to a second location), do not send a
quotation. Recommend a short resilience review so the change is understood before any
solution is proposed.

### 5.5 Early warning

The value of continuous intelligence is not waiting for renewal to discover that the
client's circumstances changed six months ago. Monitoring catches change when it
happens, not when it accumulates.

### 5.6 Monitoring cadence

- **High-priority client** — frequent monitoring.
- **Standard client** — periodic monitoring.
- **Stable client** — scheduled review.

The cadence matches the client's risk dynamism and priority.

## 6. Practical Exercises

**Exercise 2.1 — Monitor checklist (15 min)**
Build a personal monitoring checklist for one client: business, personal, and risk
change triggers you will watch.

**Exercise 2.2 — Alert response (15 min)**
Write your response to an alert that a client's transport fleet has doubled. Include the
line from Scene 4 adapted to this change. No quotation allowed.

**Exercise 2.3 — Cadence plan (15 min)**
Assign high-priority, standard, or stable status to three clients and set each one's
monitoring cadence with justification.

## 7. Case Study

**Scenario:** A CoverScore risk-change alert fires for Flourish College: the school
reported its expansion to a second campus and the acquisition of two additional buses.
The current portfolio review is not due for months.

**Tasks:**
1. Which business and risk changes does this alert combine?
2. Write the proactive response, adapting the Scene 4 line.
3. Explain why sending a quotation now would weaken the advisory relationship.
4. Set the monitoring cadence for this client after the change.

   a. Policies expire *(incorrect)*
   b. The client's circumstances change *(correct)*
   c. Premiums are reviewed *(incorrect)*
   d. Claims are settled *(incorrect)*

2. Which is a personal change the advisor should monitor?
   a. New vehicles *(incorrect)*
   b. New employees *(incorrect)*
   c. Retirement plans *(correct)*

3. A risk-change alert fires. The advisor should:
   a. Send a quotation immediately *(incorrect)*
   b. Recommend a short resilience review to determine what has changed *(correct)*
   c. Wait for renewal *(incorrect)*
   d. Log it and ignore it *(incorrect)*

4. The value of continuous intelligence is:
   a. Predicting exact claims *(incorrect)*
   b. Not waiting for renewal to discover that circumstances changed months ago *(correct)*
   c. Sending more policies *(incorrect)*
   d. Automating all conversation *(incorrect)*

   a. Frequent cadence *(incorrect)*
