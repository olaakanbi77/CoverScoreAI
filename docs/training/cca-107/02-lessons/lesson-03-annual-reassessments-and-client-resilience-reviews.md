# CCA 107 — Lesson 3: Annual Reassessments & Client Resilience Reviews

**Module:** CCA 107 — Professional Risk Advisory Practice & Client Portfolio Management
**Video duration:** ~45 minutes | **Workshop:** ~25 minutes | **Independent:** ~15 minutes

---

## 1. Lesson Overview

This lesson turns the annual review from a renewal formality into a strategic
relationship conversation. Learners contrast the old renewal pitch with an advisory
review, learn the seven annual review questions, and practise interpreting score changes
across a reassessment — understanding what changed, not chasing a number.

## 2. Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, learners will be able to:

1. Distinguish the old renewal conversation from an advisory resilience review.
2. Use the seven annual review questions to open and guide the conversation.
3. Interpret reassessment score changes (improvement and decline) professionally.
4. Explain why the score is not the objective — understanding what changed is.
5. Run the resilience review sequence: Assess → Compare → Understand → Prioritise → Act.

## 5. Learner Handbook

### 5.1 Renewal is a transaction; review is a conversation

The old renewal ("Your policy expires next month. Would you like us to renew it?") is a
transaction. The advisory review opens by acknowledging the interval and the change
since: "It's been a year since your last resilience assessment… let's review your risk
profile before we discuss renewal."

### 5.2 The seven annual review questions

1. What has changed?
2. What new risks have emerged?
3. What incidents occurred?
4. What controls have improved?
5. What protection has changed?
6. What concerns you now?
7. What should we prioritise next?

### 5.3 The score is not the objective

A movement from 48 to 61 is good news — but the number is not the objective. The
objective is understanding what changed between the two assessments and what that means
for the client.

### 5.4 Reading score changes

Treat improvement and decline symmetrically. When Property Protection rises 30 → 75,
understand what the client did (and celebrate it). When Transport Safety falls 72 → 49,
ask directly: "What has changed in your transport operation?"

### 5.5 The resilience review sequence

**Assess** (refresh the assessment) → **Compare** (against the previous year) →
**Understand** (why the changes happened) → **Prioritise** (what matters next) → **Act**
(agree next steps).

## 7. Case Study

**Scenario:** At Flourish College's annual reassessment, the CoverScore moved from 48 to
61. Property Protection improved from 30 to 75 (fire controls installed). Transport
Safety declined from 72 to 49 (two new buses added, procedures not updated). Florence
asks, "So we're better off?"

**Tasks:**
1. Explain the score movement without making the number the objective.
2. Respond to the improved property pillar (Scene 5 pattern).
3. Ask the transport question and note what you would listen for.
4. Close with a prioritised annual review decision.

## 9. AI Tutor Knowledge Base

- **KB-3.1 Renewal vs review:** The old renewal is transactional ("renew?"); the advisory
  review is a strategic conversation about what changed since the last assessment.
- **KB-3.2 Seven annual review questions:** Change, new risks, incidents, improved
  controls, protection changes, current concerns, next priorities.
- **KB-3.3 Score is not the objective:** Score movement is evidence; understanding what
  changed is the objective.
- **KB-3.4 Read both directions:** Celebrate and understand improvement; explore and
  understand decline with the same curiosity (e.g., "What has changed in your transport
  operation?").
- **KB-3.5 Resilience review:** Assess → Compare → Understand → Prioritise → Act.
