# CCA 107 — Lesson 1: Managing Client Risk Portfolios

**Module:** CCA 107 — Professional Risk Advisory Practice & Client Portfolio Management
**Video duration:** ~45 minutes | **Workshop:** ~25 minutes | **Independent:** ~15 minutes

---

## 1. Lesson Overview

This opening lesson reframes the advisor's job from maintaining policies to managing a
client's complete risk portfolio. Learners compare the policy view — a collection of
separate products — with the portfolio view — the client at the centre, surrounded by
their people, property, operations, income, and relationships. The lesson establishes
the portfolio mindset this module builds on: policy management asks what you have,
portfolio management asks what has changed, advisory management asks what should change.

## 2. Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, learners will be able to:

1. Distinguish the policy view from the client risk portfolio view.
2. Define a client risk portfolio: risks, controls, protection, gaps, changes, priorities.
3. Recognise when an existing set of policies no longer reflects the client's risk profile.
4. Replace renewal-based questions with change-based questions.
5. Apply the three management mindsets: policy, portfolio, advisory.

## 5. Learner Handbook

### 5.1 The policy view

A traditional approach treats a client's insurance as separate products: a fire policy,
a motor policy, liability cover, group life. Each is managed individually, often only at
renewal. This view answers "what policies does the client have?"

### 5.2 The portfolio view

The Risk Advisor places the client at the centre and scans the wider risk environment:
people, property, operations, vehicles, liability, income, continuity, reputation, and
personal protection. Policies are only one slice of that picture — the protection slice.

### 5.3 Defining the client risk portfolio

A client risk portfolio is a structured view of:

- **Risks** — what could harm the client;
- **Existing controls** — what reduces the likelihood or impact;
- **Existing protection** — what transfers the financial consequence;
- **Gaps** — where risks outrun controls and protection;
- **Changes** — how all of the above are moving;
- **Priorities** — what deserves attention first.

### 5.4 When policies no longer match

A private school may hold Fire, Motor, Public Liability, and Group Life. But if student
numbers grew, a bus was acquired, a second campus opened, staff increased, and a
laboratory was introduced, the risk profile has changed. The policies may still exist —
and may no longer reflect the school as it is today.

### 5.5 The advisor's question

Renewal is the wrong opening. "Are your policies due for renewal?" manages what you have.
"What has changed in your business since our last review?" manages what has changed —
and leads the client into the portfolio conversation.

### 5.6 The three management mindsets

- **Policy Management** — What do you have?
- **Portfolio Management** — What has changed?
- **Advisory Management** — What should change?

Advisory management is the goal: aligning protection strategy to the client's live, and
moving, risk picture.

## 6. Practical Exercises

**Exercise 1.1 — Draw both views (15 min)**
Draw your own client or the module case (Flourish College) as (a) a set of policy boxes
and (b) a portfolio map with the client at the centre. Compare what each drawing makes
you notice.

**Exercise 1.2 — Build a portfolio (20 min)**
For a private school, list risks, existing controls, existing protection, gaps,
changes, and priorities. Add three realistic "risk intelligence" changes and note how
each shifts the gaps.

**Exercise 1.3 — Rewrite the question (10 min)**
Take five policy-renewal questions and rewrite each as a change-focused portfolio
question.

## 7. Case Study

**Scenario:** Flourish College holds Fire, Motor, Public Liability, and Group Life
insurance. Since the last review, the school has grown from 100 to 300 students, bought
two buses, opened a second campus, hired 15 new staff, and built a science laboratory.

**Tasks:**
1. Draw the policy view and the portfolio view of Flourish College.
2. List the portfolio elements (risks, controls, protection, gaps, changes, priorities).
3. Given the changes, which existing policies may no longer reflect the school?
4. Write the change-focused opening question you would use in place of an renewal call.

   a. What do you have? *(incorrect)*

## 9. AI Tutor Knowledge Base

- **KB-1.1 Policy vs portfolio:** Policy management sees separate products; portfolio
  management sees the client at the centre of people, property, operations, vehicles,
  liability, income, continuity, reputation, and personal protection.
- **KB-1.2 Client risk portfolio:** Risks, existing controls, existing protection, gaps,
  changes, priorities — the six-part structured view.
- **KB-1.3 Policies can lag the risk profile:** Existing protection may remain correct
  products while the client's circumstances change (growth, new locations, new assets,
  new activities).
- **KB-1.4 The change question:** Ask "What has changed in your business since our last
  review?" not "Are your policies due for renewal?"
- **KB-1.5 Three mindsets:** Policy = what you have; Portfolio = what has changed;
  Advisory = what should change. The advisor's responsibility is alignment between
  changing risk and protection strategy.
- **KB-1.6 Module case (SR-001):** Flourish College, Jos — Florence James, Proprietress.
  Used across CCA 107 for portfolio practice; in this module's scenario the relationship
  is in its portfolio-management phase (the review-cycle values 41 and 58 are those of
  the CCA 107 simulation, later than the SR-001 baseline of 18).

## 12. Final Takeaway

Your responsibility is not simply to maintain policies. Your responsibility is to
maintain alignment between the client's changing risks and their protection strategy.
That alignment is only possible if you keep watching what changes — the subject of the
next lesson.
