# CCA 107 — Lesson 8: Portfolio Management Simulation

**Module:** CCA 107 — Professional Risk Advisory Practice & Client Portfolio Management
**Video duration:** ~45 minutes | **Workshop:** ~90 minutes (full simulation) | **Independent:** ~30 minutes

---

## 1. Lesson Overview

This final lesson brings the module together. Using the module's client — Florence James
and Flourish College — learners prepare for, and conduct, a portfolio review with a client
they already advise: the initial CoverScore of 41 that has since improved to 58 across one
year of advisory work. Learners run the portfolio review through Prepare → Open → Discover
→ Interpret → Advise → Document → Next Action, handle new information about the year since
the last review, prioritise risks honestly, recommend a next action, and close with the
discipline that forms the module's core principle: **a policy is a transaction, a
portfolio is a relationship.**

## 2. Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, learners will be able to:

1. Prepare a portfolio review from the Advisor Brief™, CRM record, and CoverScore history.
2. Reconcile new client information with the current portfolio and risk intelligence.
3. Interpret a changed CoverScore (improvement and remaining gaps) in plain language.
4. Prioritise risks honestly — unresolved high-priority gaps before new opportunities.
5. Recommend reduction-first, transfer-second actions and agree a next step.
6. Document the review and complete the six-question CRM discipline.
7. Self-assess against the simulation rubric and the critical failure conditions.

### Scene 5 — The Gold-Standard Simulation (5:20–8:00)

**ADVISOR**

> "Florence, thank you for the year. It is good to sit down and look at where the College
> is now.
>
> When we started, CoverScore was 41. Today it is 58. That improvement is real, and it
> belongs to the work the College has done.
>
> Before I walk through where things are, what has changed since our last review?"

**FLORENCE**

> "We opened the second campus. We bought two more buses. We have more students and more
> staff than we expected. And we had an accident — one of the students was injured on the
> playground."

**ADVISOR**

> "I'm sorry to hear about the accident. Is the student recovering?"

**FLORENCE**

> "Yes, thank you. It was serious moment but she is doing well."

**ADVISOR**

> "I understand that fire controls have improved. I want to acknowledge that — it is one
> of the reasons the score has moved from 41 to 58. What we now need to look at is how
> the new campus, the buses, and the larger population are covered by the current
> portfolio."

**FLORENCE**

> "I assumed the school was covered. The buses and the new campus are part of the school,
> so I thought the policy covered everything."

**ADVISOR**

> "That is exactly why we are having this review. The portfolio was built for the school as
> it was last year. A policy is a transaction. A portfolio is a relationship — and a
> relationship must be reviewed as the client grows.
>
> The current portfolio may not reflect the second campus, the two additional buses, the
> one hundred additional students, or the twenty-five new staff. And the transport
> procedures were not updated, which means the risk we identified last year is still
> present."

**FLORENCE**

> "What should we do?"

**ADVISOR**

> "Let us start with the things that reduce risk first. The transport procedures must be
> updated, and the new campus should be assessed against the same standards as this one.
> Those actions reduce the likelihood of incidents.
>
> Then — only after those are understood — we should check whether the financial
> protection reflects the new size of the school. The accident reminds us why this
> matters.
>
> After today I will update your record so we both have the same understanding, and we
> will agree a date for the next review."

### Scene 6 — The Simulation Debrief (6:00–6:40)

> "Notice what the Advisor did not do.
>
> They did not start by selling more cover.
>
> They did not ignore the student accident.
>
> They did not let the improvement hide the remaining gaps.
>
> They acknowledged progress, reconciled the changes, prioritised honestly, and kept the
> relationship ahead of the transaction."

## 5. Learner Handbook

### 5.1 The module competency model

CCA 107 manages the relationship across time: CCA 105 interprets the intelligence; CCA
106 uses the intelligence; **CCA 107 manages the client risk portfolio**; CCA 108
demonstrates the complete advisory cycle. The Portfolio Management Simulation is the
capstone of "managing the relationship."

### 5.2 The simulation case and intelligence pack

The simulation reuses Flourish College across a one-year advisory relationship: Florence
James, Jos, initial CoverScore 41, current CoverScore 58. The advisor receives the Advisor
Brief™, CoverScore history, CRM record, current portfolio, risk pillars, Risk Story™, and
Improvement Potential™.

### 5.3 The seven stages

1. **Prepare** — review the brief, score history, and CRM record before the conversation.
2. **Open** — confirm purpose, thank the client for the year, re-establish comfort, set expectations.
3. **Discover** — explore changes: premises, assets, people, incidents, operational improvements.
4. **Interpret** — explain the current score: what improved, what remains, in plain language.
5. **Advise** — sequence priorities: reduction first, transfer second, never mixed.
6. **Document** — confirm the record: what was discussed, agreed, and what happens next.
7. **Next Action** — agree the next review date and the client's actions.

### 5.4 Reconciling new information

Every change the client reports must be reconciled against the current portfolio: a new
campus, additional buses, more students, more staff, an incident, improved controls, and
an unchanged procedure. Protection is tested by how well it matches the current risk
profile — not by the original policy.

### 5.5 The gold standard

The gold-standard exchange shows a strong portfolio review: the advisor opens by
acknowledging the year and the client's work, discovers change before interpreting,
reconciles each change honestly, separates risk-reduction actions from transfer
questions, and documents and sets the next review. Note what the advisor does not do —
sell first, ignore the accident, or let the improvement obscure remaining gaps.

### 5.6 The simulation scoring rubric

| Area | Points |
|------|--------|
| Portfolio Preparation | 10 |
| Risk Monitoring | 15 |
| CoverScore Interpretation | 15 |
| Client Conversation | 15 |
| Post-Loss Advisory | 10 |
| Risk Prioritisation | 15 |
| Documentation | 10 |
| Relationship Management | 5 |
| Next-Step Planning | 5 |
| **Total** | **100** |

### 5.7 Critical failures

Remediation is triggered if the Advisor:

- treats the review as a renewal-only conversation;
- ignores the new campus, the buses, the extra students or the extra staff;
- ignores the student accident;
- ignores the unresolved transport procedures;
- raises products before understanding the exposure;
- fails to document the review;
- makes unsupported coverage assurances;
- fails to agree follow-up actions.

### 5.8 The certification assessment

The module assessment (see `04-assessment/assessment-pack.md`) scores the same
competencies across eight parts — Portfolio Management 15, Continuous Risk Intelligence
10, Resilience Reviews 15, Professional Risk Review 15, Claims & Post-Loss Advisory 10,
CRM & Documentation 10, Client Value 10, Portfolio Simulation 15 — totalling 100 points,
with the standard pass bands: 80–100 Competent, 70–79 Conditional Pass, 60–69 Remediation
Required, below 60 Not Yet Competent.

## 6. Practical Exercises

**Exercise 8.1 — Prepare the portfolio review (20 min)**
From the intelligence pack (brief, history 41 → 58, CRM record), complete a one-page
preparation: what you know, what changed, what you will Discover, and what you expect to
Advise. No product list yet.

**Exercise 8.2 — Reconcile the changes (20 min)**
List each piece of new information (campus, buses, students, staff, accident, controls,
procedures). For each, state whether the current portfolio reflects it, and the priority
it creates.

**Exercise 8.3 — Seven-stage flow (20 min)**
Write the portfolio review outline using all seven stages with timings. Mark where each
stage begins and ends.

**Exercise 8.4 — Peer simulation (30 min)**
Run the portfolio review with a partner. Self-assess against the 9-category rubric, then
receive their feedback.

## 7. Case Study

**Scenario:** You are the CoverScore Advisor for Florence James, Proprietress of Flourish
College (Jos). Your firm has advised the College for one year: CoverScore improved from 41
to 58. In this review she reports a second campus, two additional buses, roughly one
hundred additional students, twenty-five new staff, one student accident, improved fire
controls, and transport procedures that were not updated.

**Tasks:**
1. Write your Stage 1 Prepare notes (what you know from the brief and the CRM record).
2. Write your Stage 2 Open line that thanks her and sets expectations.
3. Write your Stage 3 Discover questions for the changes.
4. Write your Stage 4 Interpret explanation of 41 → 58 in plain language.
5. Write your Stage 5 Advise sequencing — reduction first, transfer second.
6. State what you would document and the next review date you would agree, and why.
