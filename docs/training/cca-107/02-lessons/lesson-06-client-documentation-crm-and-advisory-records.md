# CCA 107 — Lesson 6: Client Documentation, CRM & Advisory Records

**Module:** CCA 107 — Professional Risk Advisory Practice & Client Portfolio Management
**Video duration:** ~45 minutes | **Workshop:** ~25 minutes | **Independent:** ~15 minutes

## 1. Lesson Overview

This lesson establishes documentation as part of professional advisory practice, not
administrative overhead. Learners see what documentation protects, what should be
recorded, how good CRM notes differ from lazy ones, the anatomy of an advisory record,
and the discipline of answering Who, What, Why, When, Decision, and Next Step for every
meaningful engagement.

## 2. Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, learners will be able to:

1. Explain what documentation protects: client, advisor, organisation, decision quality, continuity.
2. Record client profile, risk intelligence, advisory activity, and follow-up.
3. Distinguish a good CRM note from a poor one.
4. Build an advisory record: knew → discovered → advised → decided → what happens next.
5. Apply the six-question CRM discipline to every meaningful engagement.

## 5. Learner Handbook

### 5.1 What documentation protects

- **Client** — the record holds the client's reality, decisions, and agreed plan.
- **Advisor** — a professional trail of what was understood, advised, and agreed.
- **Organisation** — a reliable corporate memory of engagements.
- **Decision Quality** — documented decisions are examined decisions.
- **Continuity** — if the advisor changes, the relationship survives via the record.

### 5.2 What to document

- **Client profile:** key contacts, decision makers, business information.
- **Risk intelligence:** assessments, risk changes, incidents, review findings.
- **Advisory activity:** meetings, discussions, recommendations, client decisions.
- **Follow-up:** actions, deadlines, responsibilities, next review date.

### 5.3 Good CRM notes

The bad note ("Spoke to client. Interested.") records a feeling. The good note records
facts and implications: expansion confirmed, protection gap identified, client
priorities named, review agreed, timing set. A good note enables the next conversation
to continue rather than restart.

### 5.4 The advisory record

For significant engagements, capture the chain: **What we knew → What we discovered →
What we advised → What the client decided → What happens next.**

### 5.5 The documentation standard

If an important advisory decision is not documented, the organisation may later have no
reliable record of what was understood, discussed, or agreed. Documentation is the
audit trail of professionalism.

### 5.6 CRM discipline

Every meaningful engagement answers six questions: **Who? What? Why? When? Decision?
Next step?**

## 6. Practical Exercises

**Exercise 6.1 — Protect the record (15 min)**
For each of the five things documentation protects, write one real scenario where the
record decides the outcome.

**Exercise 6.2 — Note rewrite (15 min)**
Take the BAD note and rewrite it as a GOOD note for the expansion scenario, covering
facts, implications, priorities, and an agreed next action.

**Exercise 6.3 — Advisory record (20 min)**
Build a full advisory record for a recent review: knew, discovered, advised, decided,
next steps.

## 7. Case Study

**Scenario:** You conclude a Risk Review with Florence James. She confirms the second
campus opening for September, notes the new buses arrived, prioritises continuity and
parent confidence, and agrees to a property and business-continuity review before
renewal.

**Tasks:**
1. Write the GOOD CRM note (Scene 3 pattern).
2. Build the five-step advisory record.
3. Complete the six-question discipline check.
4. Explain what the organisation could reconstruct from your record in a year.

## 8. Knowledge Check

1. Documentation protects:
   a. Client, advisor, organisation, decision quality, continuity *(correct)*
   b. Only the advisor *(incorrect)*
   c. Only the insurer *(incorrect)*
   d. Only the premium *(incorrect)*

2. Which is a GOOD CRM note?
   a. "Spoke to client. Interested." *(incorrect)*
   b. "Client confirmed expansion to second campus in September. Current property protection may not reflect the additional location. Agreed to conduct property and business-continuity review before renewal." *(correct)*
   c. "Client was friendly." *(incorrect)*
   d. "Call again later." *(incorrect)*

3. The advisory record runs:
   a. Advised → Decided → Knew → Discovered *(incorrect)*
   b. What we knew → What we discovered → What we advised → What the client decided → What happens next *(correct)*
   c. Decided → Advanced → Discovered *(incorrect)*
   d. Next step → Recommendation *(incorrect)*

4. "Good documentation is not administrative overhead" means:
   a. Documentation is optional *(incorrect)*
   b. It is part of professional advisory practice *(correct)*
   c. Only managers document *(incorrect)*
   d. Notes can be vague *(incorrect)*

5. The CRM discipline questions are:
   a. Who? What? Why? When? Decision? Next step? *(correct)*
   b. How much? When paid? *(incorrect)*
   c. Name? Age? Location? *(incorrect)*
   d. Policy? Premium? Commission? *(incorrect)*

**Answers:** 1-a, 2-b, 3-b, 4-b, 5-a.
