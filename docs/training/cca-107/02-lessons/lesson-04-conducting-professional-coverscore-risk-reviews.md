# CCA 107 — Lesson 4: Conducting Professional CoverScore Risk Reviews™

**Module:** CCA 107 — Professional Risk Advisory Practice & Client Portfolio Management
**Video duration:** ~45 minutes | **Workshop:** ~25 minutes | **Independent:** ~15 minutes

## 1. Lesson Overview

This lesson teaches the structured professionalism of the CoverScore Risk Review™.
Learners prepare from the full record of assessments, recommendations, actions, claims,
and CRM history; compare progress across assessments; identify unresolved gaps; and close
each gap with a disciplined four-response judgement — Reduce, Transfer, Accept, or Avoid.

## 2. Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, learners will be able to:

1. Assemble the full intelligence package before a Risk Review.
2. Prepare answers to the six review questions (improved, deteriorated, unresolved, changed, recommended, implemented).
3. Open a Risk Review professionally and compare previous vs current assessments.
4. Apply the four-response model: Reduce, Transfer, Accept, Avoid.
5. Finish a review with a professional output covering priorities, progress, exposures, actions, protection, and next steps.

## 5. Learner Handbook

### 5.1 Before the review

The professional review is evidence-based. The Advisor receives the previous CoverScore,
current assessment, Advisor Brief™, previous recommendations, previous actions, claims
history, portfolio information, and CRM records. Nothing relevant is left to memory.

### 5.2 The six preparation answers

Know, before the meeting starts: what improved, what deteriorated, what remains
unresolved, what changed, what was previously recommended, and what was actually
implemented.

### 5.3 The client opening

Open with the arc of the review: "We'll look at what improved, what remains exposed, and
whether your current protection still reflects your situation." This sets the tone —
historical comparison leading to a forward decision.

### 5.4 Comparing progress

Frame both directions with curiosity. On improvement (Property 25% → 65%): "This is a
significant improvement. What actions did you take?" On stall (Transport 40% → 42%):
"Transport safety has barely changed. What prevented progress in this area?" No blame, no
cookie-cutter praise.

### 5.5 The four-response model

For each gap, the Advisor decides which response applies:

- **Reduce** — operational improvement (Risk Reduction™ first).
- **Transfer** — insurance protection (Risk Transfer™).
- **Accept** — a conscious decision to retain the exposure.
- **Avoid** — stop or change the activity that creates the risk.

### 5.6 The professional review output

Close with: priority risks, progress made, unresolved exposures, recommended actions,
protection considerations, and agreed next steps. The output is what makes the review
actionable.

## 6. Practical Exercises

**Exercise 4.3 — Four-response decisions (15 min)**
For five gaps (untested alarm, no fire cover, undertrained drivers, disused building,
single delivery route), select Reduce/Transfer/Accept/Avoid and justify.

## 7. Case Study

**Scenario:** At Flourish College's Risk Review, Property Protection improved 25% → 65%
after fire-control work. Transport Safety sits at 40% → 42%. The previous review had
recommended updating transport procedures; the client confirmed these were not
implemented because the transport supervisor left.

**Tasks:**
1. Write the Scene 3 opening.
2. Write the Improvement response (Scene 4) and the Unresolved response (Scene 5).
3. Choose the four-response judgement for the transport gap and justify it.
4. Write the professional review output (six components) for the meeting.

## 8. Knowledge Check

1. Which item should the Advisor receive before a Risk Review?
   a. Only the current assessment *(incorrect)*
   b. Previous CoverScore, current assessment, Advisor Brief, previous recommendations and actions, claims history, portfolio and CRM records *(correct)*
   c. Only the policy list *(incorrect)*
   d. Only the premium history *(incorrect)*

2. Property Protection went 25% → 65%. The professional response:
   a. "Good. Next topic." *(incorrect)*
   b. "This is a significant improvement. What actions did you take after our previous review?" *(correct)*
   c. "You should still buy more cover." *(incorrect)*
   d. "The old score must have been wrong." *(incorrect)*

3. Transport Safety is stuck at 40% → 42%. The professional response:
   a. "Transport safety has barely changed. What prevented progress in this area?" *(correct)*
   b. "Why didn't you listen to me?" *(incorrect)*
   c. "At least it didn't fall." *(incorrect)*
   d. "You need fleet insurance today." *(incorrect)*

4. The four-response model judges each gap as:
   a. Buy, renew, upgrade, downgrade *(incorrect)*
   b. Reduce, Transfer, Accept, Avoid *(correct)*
   c. Slow, fast, urgent, normal *(incorrect)*
   d. Quote, bound, issue, renew *(incorrect)*

5. A professional Risk Review output concludes with:
   a. Priority risks, progress, unresolved exposures, recommended actions, protection considerations, agreed next steps *(correct)*
   b. Only the new premium *(incorrect)*
   c. Only the new score *(incorrect)*
   d. Only the policies to sign *(incorrect)*

**Answers:** 1-b, 2-b, 3-a, 4-b, 5-a.

## 9. AI Tutor Knowledge Base

- **KB-4.1 Intelligence package:** Previous and current CoverScore, Advisor Brief™,
  previous recommendations and actions, claims history, portfolio information, CRM
  records.
- **KB-4.2 Six preparation answers:** What improved, deteriorated, remains unresolved,
  changed, was recommended, was implemented.
- **KB-4.3 Professional opening:** Review what changed since the last engagement —
  improved, exposed, protection-fit.
- **KB-4.4 Compare with curiosity:** Improvement and stall are both explored with open
  questions; no blame, no formulaic praise.
- **KB-4.5 Four-response model:** Reduce (operational), Transfer (insurance), Accept
  (conscious retention), Avoid (stop or change the activity). Reduction first, Transfer
  second, never mixed.
- **KB-4.6 Review output:** Priorities, progress, unresolved exposures, recommended
  actions, protection considerations, agreed next steps.
