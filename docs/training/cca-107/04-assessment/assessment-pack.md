# CCA 107 — Part 4: Assessment Pack

**Module:** CCA 107 — Professional Risk Advisory Practice & Client Portfolio Management
**Format:** One live Portfolio Management Simulation, scored on 8 parts, 100 points.
**Pass mark:** 80+ Competent (see pass bands below).
**Scenario source:** Flourish College review-cycle state (initial CoverScore 41, current
CoverScore 58); assessors may substitute SR-002–SR-010 at comparable difficulty.

---

## 1. Assessment Overview

CCA 107 certifies that the Advisor can **manage the relationship and the client risk
portfolio across time**: monitor the portfolio, track continuous risk intelligence, run
annual reassessments and professional Risk Reviews™, advise after claims and incidents,
keep professional records, measure client value, and plan next actions — **because a
policy is a transaction and a portfolio is a relationship.**

The certification instrument is a **live Portfolio Management Simulation** scored across
eight parts, exactly matching the module's competency model:

| Part | Area | Points |
|------|------|--------|
| 1 | Portfolio Management | 15 |
| 2 | Continuous Risk Intelligence | 10 |
| 3 | Resilience Reviews | 15 |
| 4 | Professional Risk Review | 15 |
| 5 | Claims & Post-Loss Advisory | 10 |
| 6 | CRM & Documentation | 10 |
| 7 | Client Value | 10 |
| 8 | Portfolio Simulation | 15 |
| | **Total** | **100** |

**Formative rubric:** Lesson 8 uses the 9-category in-lesson simulation rubric
(Portfolio Preparation 10, Risk Monitoring 15, CoverScore Interpretation 15, Client
Conversation 15, Post-Loss Advisory 10, Risk Prioritisation 15, Documentation 10,
Relationship Management 5, Next-Step Planning 5). This assessment pack is the summative,
certification-level instrument.

## 2. Assessment Scenario

The learner receives, in advance, the **review-cycle intelligence pack**:

- **Client:** Florence James · **Business:** Flourish College · **Location:** Jos
- **Initial CoverScore:** 41 / 100 · **Current CoverScore:** 58 / 100
- **Relationship:** One year of advisory service
- Available intelligence: Advisor Brief™, CoverScore history (41 → 58), CRM record,
  current portfolio, risk pillars, Risk Story™, Improvement Potential™.

**Task:** Conduct a portfolio review for a client already being advised, covering all
seven stages (Prepare, Open, Discover, Interpret, Advise, Document, Next Action). The
assessor plays the client and reveals, through the learner's questions, what changed in
the year:

- a second campus opened;
- two additional buses acquired;
- approximately one hundred additional students;
- twenty-five new staff;
- one student incident/accident occurred;
- fire controls improved;
- transport procedures were **not** updated.

The conversation must demonstrate: portfolio preparation, risk monitoring, CoverScore
interpretation, client conversation, post-loss advisory, risk prioritisation,
documentation, relationship management, and next-step planning.

## 3. Part 1 — Portfolio Management (15 points)

Observed across the whole review.

| Mark | Descriptor |
|------|------------|
| 13–15 | Treats the client as a portfolio, not a policy: reconciles every reported change against current protection, sequences reduction before transfer, and keeps the relationship ahead of the transaction |
| 9–12 | Strong portfolio thinking; one change missed or one reconciliation deferred |
| 5–8 | Policy-level thinking dominates; changes discussed without reconciliation |
| 0–4 | Renewal-only mindset; no reconciliation of the new campus, buses, students, or staff |

**Absolutes:** the review is never a renewal ritual; the portfolio is reviewed as the
client grows. **A policy is a transaction. A portfolio is a relationship.**

---

## 4. Part 2 — Continuous Risk Intelligence (10 points)

| Mark | Descriptor |
|------|------------|
| 9–10 | Treats the client's new information as risk change signals: asks what changed, tests each against the portfolio, and names the priority each change creates |
| 6–8 | Captures most changes and links them to the portfolio; one signal handled passively |
| 3–5 | Waits to be told rather than probing change; signals not converted into review actions |
| 0–2 | Ignores changes or treats new information as a sales opening only |

**Absolutes:** new information flows Client → CoverScore Intelligence → Risk Change
Signal → Advisor Review → Client Engagement. The advisor is never passive between reviews.

---

## 5. Part 3 — Resilience Reviews (15 points)

| Mark | Descriptor |
|------|------------|
| 13–15 | Treats this as an annual reassessment: acknowledges the client's work behind 41 → 58, frames the review as progress-check plus honest gap-finding, and sets the next review expectation |
| 9–12 | Frames progress and gaps clearly; one moment of score-chasing or avoidance |
| 3–5 | Presents the improved score as the whole story, or ignores progress entirely |
| 0–4 | No review frame; no acknowledgement of improvement, no honest prioritisation |

**Absolutes:** the improved score is praised as the client's work — never as a reason to
stop, and never used to hide remaining gaps.

---

## 6. Part 4 — Professional Risk Review (15 points)

The seven stages must all appear, in order.

| Stage | Points | Evidence expected |
|-------|--------|-------------------|
| Prepare | 2 | Reviews the brief, score history, and CRM record before the conversation |
| Open | 2 | Thanks the client for the year; confirms purpose; sets expectations |
| Discover | 3 | Explores premises, assets, people, incidents, operational improvements |
| Interpret | 3 | Explains 41 → 58 plainly: what improved, what remains, what the change means |
| Advise | 3 | Sequences priorities — reduction first, transfer second, never mixed |
| Document | 1 | Confirms what was discussed and agreed |
| Next Action | 1 | Agrees the next review date and the client's actions |

**Deduct all Advise points if the learner reaches recommendations without completing
Discover and Interpret.** Raising products before understanding the exposure is a
critical failure (see §11).

---

## 7. Part 5 — Claims & Post-Loss Advisory (10 points)

| Mark | Descriptor |
|------|------------|
| 9–10 | Responds to the reported student accident professionally: acknowledges it, shows care, asks about recovery, and treats it as a priority finding — not a selling opportunity |
| 6–8 | Acknowledges the incident but does not connect it to the risk story |
| 3–5 | Uses the incident for urgency, or glosses over it because the score improved |
| 0–2 | Ignores the accident or blames the client |

**Absolutes:** the response is blame-free and human first. The post-loss framework is
Stabilise → Document → Understand → Learn → Improve → Protect — never "here is a policy
for what happened."

---

## 8. Part 6 — CRM & Documentation (10 points)

| Mark | Descriptor |
|------|------------|
| 9–10 | Documents the review explicitly: confirms what was understood, discussed, and agreed, and completes the six-question discipline (Who? What? Why? When? Decision? Next step?) |
| 6–8 | Refers to updating the record; one element of the advisory record unclear |
| 3–5 | Documentation mentioned but vague; no confirmed record of decisions |
| 0–2 | No documentation at all; nothing confirmed as recorded |

**Absolutes:** an important advisory decision that is not documented leaves no reliable
record. The advisory record is knew → discovered → advised → decided → what happens next.

---

## 9. Part 7 — Client Value (10 points)

| Mark | Descriptor |
|------|------------|
| 9–10 | Measures value beyond premium: names the resilience the client gained (41 → 58), links current protection to the current profile, and shows how value will be created in the year ahead |
| 6–8 | Mentions value generally; improvement not quantified or linked to the portfolio |
| 3–5 | Judges the relationship by premium or policies alone |
| 0–2 | No value framing; the relationship is only a book of business |

**Absolutes:** value is measured by risk improvement, protection alignment, response
quality, relationship strength, retention, and growth — not premium volume.

---

## 10. Part 8 — Portfolio Simulation (15 points)

| Mark | Descriptor |
|------|------------|
| 13–15 | Runs a complete, natural, end-to-end portfolio review: prepared, opened, discovered, interpreted, advised, documented, and agreed next actions — with the portfolio principle visible throughout |
| 9–12 | Full flow; one stage rushed or one connection forced |
| 5–8 | Incomplete flow; stages skipped or the review becomes a product conversation |
| 0–4 | No coherent review; transactional or selling behaviour dominates |

**Absolutes:** every review ends in an agreed next action and a next review date. The
module's measure of mastery is "How do I continuously create value for this client?"

---

## 11. Critical Failure Conditions

The simulation triggers **remediation** (with mandatory coaching before resit) if the
Advisor:

1. Treats the review as a renewal-only conversation.
2. Ignores the new campus, the additional buses, the extra students, or the extra staff.
3. Ignores the student accident.
4. Ignores the unresolved transport procedures.
5. Raises products before understanding the exposure.
6. Fails to document the review.
7. Makes unsupported coverage assurances (e.g., "the new campus is definitely covered").
8. Fails to agree follow-up actions.

## 12. Scoring Bands

| Total | Verdict |
|-------|---------|
| 80–100 | **Competent** — certified |
| 70–79 | **Conditional Pass** — one targeted re-assessment of the weakest part |
| 60–69 | **Remediation Required** — coaching on the failed parts, then a full re-sit |
| Below 60 | **Not Yet Competent** — full re-sit with mentoring |

Any critical failure (§11) means **not competent**, regardless of total, until coaching
is completed.

## 13. Administration Notes

- **Simulation load:** One fresh scenario per attempt. The Flourish College review-cycle
  case is the default; assessors may draw SR-002–SR-010 at comparable difficulty. The
  one-year changes are revealed by the learner's questioning, not given in advance.
- **Time:** 15-minute portfolio review, then a 5-minute assessor debrief on the rubric.
- **The client role:** The assessor plays Florence James, reporting the year's changes
  (campus, buses, students, staff, accident, fire controls, unchanged transport
  procedures) only as the learner's questions create the space — and noting it if the
  learner never asks.
- **Intelligence pack:** The learner receives the Advisor Brief™, CoverScore history,
  CRM record, current portfolio, pillars, Risk Story™, and Improvement Potential™
  beforehand. Preparation is part of the skill.
- **Grading:** Two assessors recommended; scores logged per part with evidence quotes.
- **Ethics flags:** Any fear-based language, pressure tactic, or blame of the client is
  recorded as an ethics flag and is a coaching priority even where the learner passes.
- **Re-sits:** Maximum two attempts per module cycle; remediation plan required between.
- **Audit:** Score breakdown retained per learner (Academy records).

## 14. Gold-Standard Performance

A strong submission includes all of the following, in order:

1. **Prepare** — reviews the brief, the 41 → 58 history, and the CRM record before the
   conversation.
2. **Open** — thanks the client for the year, confirms purpose, sets expectations, and
   frames the review as progress-check plus honest gap-finding.
3. **Discover** — asks what changed and reconciles each change (campus, buses, students,
   staff, accident, fire controls, transport procedures) against the current portfolio.
4. **Interpret** — explains 41 → 58 plainly: the client's work produced real
   improvement, and remaining gaps — especially the unchanged transport procedures —
   still need attention.
5. **Advise** — sequences reduction first (update transport procedures, assess the new
   campus to the same standards) and only then checks whether financial protection
   reflects the school's new size.
6. **Document** — confirms the record: what was understood, discussed, and agreed, and
   completes the six-question discipline.
7. **Next Action** — agrees the next review date and the client's actions.

**Debrief note:** notice what the gold standard does not do — it does not sell first,
ignore the accident, gloss over the improvement, blame the client, or let a new campus
remain unreconciled.

## 15. Portfolio Principle (recap)

> **A policy is a transaction. A portfolio is a relationship.**

The Advisor who understands this is not chasing business. They are building the
relationship that keeps business.
