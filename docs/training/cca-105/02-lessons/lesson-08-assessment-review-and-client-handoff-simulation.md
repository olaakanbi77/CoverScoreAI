# CCA 105 — Lesson 8: Assessment Review & Client Handoff Simulation

**Module:** CCA 105 — Practical Risk Advisory & Client Assessment
**Type:** Guided simulation + capstone practice | **Duration:** ~90 minutes
**Workshop:** ~45 minutes | **Independent:** ~45 minutes

---

## 1. Lesson Overview

Everything converges here. Learners conduct a complete, end-to-end simulated engagement:
receive an Advisor Brief and report, analyse the assessment, prepare for the Risk Review,
run the review conversation, and hand the client off to recommendations and next steps.
This is the rehearsal for the module assessment — and the first structured CoverScore
Risk Review of their career.

## 2. Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, learners will be able to:

1. Orchestrate the full AI-to-advisor flow within one engagement.
2. Deliver a structured Risk Review from open to close.
3. Build and present a prioritised client action plan live.
4. Perform a clean client handoff from insight to recommendations.
5. Self-assess against the module's eight assessment parts.

## 5. Learner Handbook

### 5.1 The five-phase simulation model (SR-001)

| Phase | What you do | Tools |
|-------|-------------|-------|
| **Review** | Read the Advisor Brief + report; verify facts; separate knowns from unknowns | Report, Brief, verification list |
| **Prepare** | Discovery questions; objection anticipation; define meeting purpose | Prep checklist, question bank |
| **Conversation** | Run the Risk Review; discover hidden information; handle decision points | Conversation guides |
| **Prioritise & Separate** | Agree the priority; separate Risk Reduction from Risk Transfer | Reduction/Transfer classification |
| **Agree & Handoff** | Agree next step + owner + date; complete the CRM handoff | CRM handoff form |

### 5.2 The eight assessment parts (self-assess)

1. **Knowledge** — did you demonstrate the core understanding?
2. **Assessment interpretation** — did you read the report correctly?
3. **Advisor Brief preparation** — did you convert intelligence into preparation?
4. **Discovery** — did you uncover hidden information with purposeful questions?
5. **Risk prioritisation** — right priority, right order, justified?
6. **Recommendation** — Risk Reduction before Risk Transfer, never mixed?
7. **Objection handling** — did you understand, not defeat?
8. **CRM / handoff** — complete, accurate, professional?

Pass standard: **80%** (80–100 Competent) on the weightings in
`04-assessment/assessment-pack.md`. Conditional 70–79; remediation 60–69; below 60 not
yet competent.

### 5.3 The handoff — "understand" to "decide"

The handoff is the final, clean transition:

1. **Summarise** the decision gap you uncovered: "You now know property protection is your top risk."
2. **Present** the recommended path — Risk Reduction first, Risk Transfer clearly separated.
3. **Confirm** the chosen next step and its date.
4. **Log** everything: CRM task, notes, follow-up slot.

The client should leave understanding what was decided, why, and when the advisor returns.

**Exercise 8.1 — Review the brief (15 min)**

**Exercise 8.2 — Prepare (15 min)**

**Exercise 8.3 — Review + Handoff (30 min)**
With a role-play partner, run Phases 3–5 live using the CRM handoff form. Then swap
roles and repeat.

## 7. Case Study (The Simulation Brief — SR-001)

**Flourish College** — private school in Jos, 100–500 students, ~15–20 staff, one school
bus. Proprietress **Florence James** manages operations and finance herself. CoverScore
**18/100 — Critical Risk**. Pillars: Transport Safety 43 · Business Continuity 35 ·
Student Safety 32 · Regulatory Readiness 30 · **Property Protection 5**. Evidence: no
Fire Insurance; fire alarm weakness; extinguisher servicing weakness; rare maintenance;
student incident; no written emergency procedures; no Public Liability; driver-training
gap. Hidden context (to be discovered): budget constraint; untested alarm; unserviced
extinguishers; reactive maintenance; minor playground accident; no written emergency
procedures; no driver training; no closure-cost calculation. Budget is tight; the
proprietress is warm but financially guarded.

**Tasks (full simulation):**
1. Produce the Phase 1 verification list (confirmed facts vs unknowns).
2. Produce the Phase 2 prep pack (purpose, questions, objections, opening).
3. Run the live AI conversation: discover hidden information; handle the five decision
   points; prioritise; separate Risk Reduction from Risk Transfer.
4. Agree a specific next step with owner and date.
5. Complete the CRM handoff form.
6. Self-assess against all eight assessment parts and write where you would improve.

## 8. Knowledge Check

1. The correct phase order is:
   a. Review → Prioritise → Conversation → Handoff
   b. Review → Prepare → Conversation → Prioritise & Separate → Agree & Handoff *(correct)*
   c. Prepare → Review → Handoff → Prioritise *(incorrect)*
   d. Handoff → Prepare → Review → Conversation *(incorrect)*

2. The client handoff is best completed when:
   a. The client has been left to think alone
   b. The next step, owner, date, and CRM log are agreed and recorded *(correct)*
   c. The premium has been quoted on the phone *(incorrect)*
   d. The assessment has been redone *(incorrect)*

3. For SR-001 (Flourish College), which priority should lead the review?
   a. Transport Safety (highest pillar)
   b. Property Protection — the widest gap at 5, with the most severe consequence *(correct)*
   c. Student Safety only *(incorrect)*
   d. No priority — scores don't matter *(incorrect)*

4. The module pass standard is:
   a. 50%
   b. 70%
   c. 80% (80–100 Competent) *(correct)*
   d. 100%

5. When recommending, Risk Reduction and Risk Transfer must:
   a. Be combined into one powerful recommendation
   b. Always be presented together in a single sentence
   c. Be kept separate — Reduction first, Transfer clearly separated, never mixed *(correct)*
   d. Be replaced by a product quote *(incorrect)*

**Answers:** 1-b, 2-b, 3-b, 4-c, 5-c.
