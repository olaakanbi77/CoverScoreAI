# CCA 105 — Lesson 4: Interpreting CoverScore Reports™

**Module:** CCA 105 — Practical Risk Advisory & Client Assessment
**Video duration:** ~45 minutes | **Workshop:** ~25 minutes | **Independent:** ~15 minutes

---

## 1. Lesson Overview

The CoverScore Report™ is what the client sees. This lesson gives learners a complete
section-by-section reading of the report — from the score banner to recommendations — and
the language to explain each part in plain, honest, non-technical terms. Learners also
learn how to handle the report's "harder" messages (low scores, uncovered risks) with
empathy and without fear-based selling.

## 2. Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, learners will be able to:

1. Name and explain every section of a CoverScore Report™.
2. Translate engine language (bands, pillars, exposure) into client language.
3. Distinguish what to show, what to explain, and what to prioritise first.
4. Communicate low scores with honesty and empathy — never fear or pressure.
5. Connect the report's findings to the Risk Review agenda.

## 5. Learner Handbook

### 5.1 Report sections at a glance

| Section | What it contains | Client-facing translation |
|---------|------------------|---------------------------|
| Header / identity | Client name, business, assessment type, date | "This is your personalised report." |
| Score banner | CoverScore™ (0–100), band | "Out of 100, how protected you currently are." |
| Score dial/level | Band label + colour | "Overall, preparation is strong / needs attention." |
| Pillar scores | One score per risk dimension | "Where you're strong and where the gaps are." |
| Risk fingerprint | Pattern visual of pillar scores | "Your overall risk picture at a glance." |
| Top risks | 3–5 highest priority gaps | "The most important things to work on." |
| Exposure index | Estimated potential loss | "What a major event could cost if unprotected." |
| Protection gap | Missing vs adequate protection | "The gap between where you are and where you aim to be." |
| Recommendations | Suggested next actions | "Practical next steps — most to least urgent." |
| Resilience note | Current vs potential resilience | "How quickly you'd recover today — and could recover." |

### 5.2 Translation cheat-sheet

| Engine term | Say this |
|-------------|----------|
| "CoverScore 46 — Needs Attention" | "Your report gives you 46 out of 100 right now. It means preparation is partially in place, and a few gaps could hurt you if something happened." |
| "Pillar score 22 — Property" | "Your property protection scores lowest — this is the area most at risk." |
| "Exposure index high" | "If a major incident happened, the uninsured cost could be significant." |
| "Protection gap NGN 40M" | "That's the value of cover you don't yet have in place." |
| "Recommendations (3)" | "These are the three steps we can take, in the order that protects you most." |

### 5.3 Reading order for the advisor

1. Header & date — is this the current, complete assessment?
2. Score & band — overall positioning.
3. Pillar scores — where are the clusters / single weak dimension?
4. Top risks & exposure — which carry the greatest financial weight?
5. Recommendations — draft the agenda (before the conversation, not during).
6. Set aside: what you will NOT discuss first (avoid overload).

### 5.4 Handling the low-score moment (empathy ladder)

When the score is low or the news is hard:

1. **Name it plainly but gently.** "Your score is 38. That means a few important gaps
   are currently unprotected."
2. **Normalise.** "This is exactly what the assessment is for — most businesses start
   here."
3. **Give the why.** Reference the actual gaps (their money, not generic risk).
4. **Reframe as controllable.** "The good news: each of these has a practical fix. We
   decide the order together."
5. **Offer a path.** Close on the next step, not the score.

**Never:** exaggerate danger, invent outcomes, or use urgency unsupported by the report.

### 5.5 From report to review agenda

Each top risk becomes a review agenda item:
- Gap 1 → confirm it (question) → measure its impact (chain) → propose fix (recommendation).

## 6. Practical Exercises

**Exercise 4.1 — Translate a report (15 min)**
Convert these into client language: "CoverScore 52, Needs Attention"; "Protection Gap
NGN 25M"; "Cyber pillar 31"; "Resilience: Developing". Write one sentence for each.

**Exercise 4.2 — Hard-message role-play (15 min)**
Practise the empathy ladder with a partner on a low-score scenario. The partner plays a
client who is visibly worried. Record what you said; review whether any sentence crossed
into fear-based selling.

**Exercise 4.3 — Agenda mapping (10 min)**
Take a report with 5 top risks. Select the 3 you would cover first in a Risk Review and
write the agenda with one reinforcing question per item.

## 7. Case Study

**Scenario:** A hospital (HOS prefix) report arrives: CoverScore 44 (Needs Attention).
Pillars: Governance 70, Staff & Safety 55, Property 60, **Medical Equipment 22**,
**Infrastructure & Utilities 30**, **Patient Care Support 35**, Data & Records 65,
Emergency Preparedness 50. Top risks listed: no medical equipment breakdown cover;
single power source for ICU; no equipment maintenance contract; exposed patient records
in an unencrypted system. Exposure index: high. The CEO is a commercial doctor who has
previously said "my business is medicine, not insurance." Confidence in the assessment is
medium-high (the COO completed it carefully).

**Tasks:**
1. Write the plain-language summary you would open with (calm, honest, no fear-mongering).
2. Which pillar is the "single weakest dimension"? How does it change the conversation?
3. Build the Risk Review agenda — which 3 items first, and why?
4. The CEO's objection ("my business is medicine") — how does the report help you answer
   without pressure?
5. Draft the one-sentence "next step" you would close the first conversation with.

## 8. Knowledge Check

1. The score banner communicates:
   a. The exact premium to pay
   b. Overall current protection level out of 100 *(correct)*
   c. The advisor's sales target *(incorrect)*
   d. The number of policies to buy *(incorrect)*

2. "Protection gap" means:
   a. The amount of extra money the client should save
   b. The difference between current protection and adequate protection *(correct)*
   c. The client's loan balance *(incorrect)*
   d. A gap between two policies *(incorrect)*

3. When a score is low, the advisor should:
   a. Avoid mentioning the number
   b. Emphasise urgency and fear
   c. Name it plainly, normalise it, reframe as controllable, and offer a path *(correct)*
   d. Blame the assessment engine *(incorrect)*

4. Which pillar interpretation is correct for a score of 22 in Property?
   a. Property is fully protected
   b. Property is the strongest area
   c. Property is the weakest area and most at risk *(correct)*
   d. Property is not measured *(incorrect)*

5. "Exposure index: high" should be explained as:
   a. "You're about to lose everything"
   b. "An uninsured major incident could carry significant cost" *(correct)*
   c. "Buy now before prices rise"
   d. "Your report is unreliable" *(incorrect)*

**Answers:** 1-b, 2-b, 3-c, 4-c, 5-b.

## 9. AI Tutor Knowledge Base

- **KB-4.1 Report anatomy:** Identity/header, score banner + band, pillar scores, risk
  fingerprint, top risks, exposure index, protection gap, recommendations, resilience
  note.
- **KB-4.2 Translation rules:** always map engine terms to plain client language; never
  invent outcomes; never alter scores or recommendations (hallucination prevention).
- **KB-4.3 Empathy ladder:** name plainly → normalise → give the why → reframe as
  controllable → offer a path.
- **KB-4.4 Anti-patterns:** fear-based messaging, pressure-selling, contradictory
  narratives, overly technical language, unsupported urgency — all prohibited.
- **KB-4.5 Report confinement:** every explanation must be traceable to the structured
  assessment data in the report.

## 11. Lesson Completion Standard

This lesson is complete when learners can:
- Walk through every report section and translate it into client language.
- Deliver a low score using the empathy ladder without fear-based tactics.
- Turn a report's top gaps into a prioritised review agenda.
- Pass the knowledge check at 80% or higher.

## 12. Final Takeaway

The report is your shared language with the client — honest, calm, and precise. If they
understand their gaps, they can decide. Your job is not to scare them into action, but to
make their next step feel obvious.
