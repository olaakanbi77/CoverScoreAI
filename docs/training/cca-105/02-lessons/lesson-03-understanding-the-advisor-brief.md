**Module:** CCA 105 — Practical Risk Advisory & Client Assessment

## 1. Lesson Overview

The Advisor Brief™ is the advisor's superpower: a one-page AI-generated briefing that
arrives in the CRM the moment an assessment completes. This lesson teaches learners to
read every section of the Brief, distinguish its facts from its suggestions, personalise
it, and convert it into a professional first contact — every time.

## 2. Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, learners will be able to:

1. Identify the nine sections of a standard Advisor Brief™.
2. Separate verified facts from AI suggestions within the Brief.
3. Personalise the Brief's opening line and suggested questions.
4. Detect weaknesses, contradictions, or gaps in a Brief.
5. Turn a Brief into a compliant first contact within the CRM's time windows.

## 5. Learner Handbook

### 5.1 What the Brief is — and is not

The Advisor Brief™ is a one-page briefing generated by the Advisor Copilot immediately
after a client completes their assessment. It compresses the entire assessment, risk
analysis, and coaching suggestions into a scannable page. It is **not** a script to read
to the client, **not** a proposal, and **not** a diagnosis of the client's financial
life. It is your starting scaffold: verified by you, personalised by you, owned by you.

### 5.2 The nine sections of the Brief

| # | Section | What it contains | Category |
|---|---------|------------------|----------|
| 1 | Customer Overview | Who the client is, domain, size, contact, source | Facts |
| 2 | Assessment Summary | CoverScore™, band, completion, confidence indicators | Facts |
| 3 | Priority Risks | Highest-ranked gaps (from engine + mapping) | Facts (ranked) |
| 4 | Strengths | What the client already does well | Facts |
| 5 | Conversation Goals | Suggested aims for the Risk Review | Suggestions |
| 6 | Recommended Questions | Question bank tailored to the case | Suggestions |
| 7 | Suggested Products | Products mapped to gaps with rationale | Suggestions (maps) |
| 8 | Learning Progress | Any academy content the client has completed | Facts |
| 9 | Potential Objections | Likely hesitations + ready responses | Suggestions |

### 5.3 Facts vs suggestions — the golden discipline

- **Facts:** client overview, scores, strengths, learning progress. These are engine
  output — you quote them precisely and never massage them.
- **Suggestions:** conversation goals, recommended questions, product suggestions,
  objection handling. These are AI judgement — you evaluate them, adjust them, and take
  ownership.
- Never treat a suggested product as a recommendation until your analysis confirms it.
- Never let a suggested question replace your own discovery instinct.

### 5.4 Personalising the opening

The Copilot gives a working opening line. Personalise it by layering three touches:

1. **Their words** — reflect a phrase the client used (e.g., "you mentioned your biggest
   concern is continuity if you step away").
2. **Their context** — reference their industry, size, or life stage.
3. **Their strengths** — acknowledge what they already do well before discussing gaps.

Example transformation:
> Generic: "Hi John, I've reviewed your CoverScore assessment and I can see you're doing
> well with your emergency procedures, but there are areas where your manufacturing
> operation could be better protected."
>
> Personalised: "Hi John, I've reviewed your assessment. Your safety procedures are
> genuinely strong — but with a three-shift plant on imported machinery, one
> unplanned breakdown could stop your line for months. I'd like to walk you through
> what I found and where a little preparation would go a long way."

### 5.5 Brief QA checklist (before first contact)

1. Facts verified against the client profile and report (scores, size, contact).
2. Confidence level noted; gaps flagged for the conversation.
3. Opening line personalised (their words, context, strengths).
4. 3 targeted questions chosen, not read out, from the Brief.
5. Contact scheduled inside the CRM time window and next action logged.

## 6. Practical Exercises

**Exercise 3.1 — Annotate a Brief (15 min)**
Using the sample Brief below, label every line as Fact (F) or Suggestion (S). Justify
each label.

```
Customer Overview: Ayo Bakery, 5 outlets, 90 staff, revenue NGN 300M (F)
Assessment Summary: CoverScore 46, band Needs Improvement, low-moderate confidence (F)
Priority Risks: No business interruption cover; no fire cover on outlet interiors (F, ranked)
Strengths: Documented hygiene procedures; 2 of 5 outlets at shopping malls (F)
Conversation Goals: Confirm priority of BI over other gap areas (S)
Recommended Questions: ask about seasonal revenue and catering contracts (S)
Suggested Products: Business Interruption; Fire & Perils; Money Insurance (S)
Objections: "I already have property cover" — clarify it is building-only (S)
```

**Exercise 3.2 — Personalise the opening (10 min)**
Rewrite the Copilot opening line for Mrs. Adaeze (bakery, confidence low-moderate, fear
about cash flow) using "their words, their context, their strengths."

**Exercise 3.3 — Brief QA (10 min)**
Run the 5-point QA checklist against the same sample. Write what you would check and log
before making the call.

## 7. Case Study

**Scenario:** You receive an Advisor Brief for a construction company (CON prefix): 60
staff, two active sites, one site with scaffolding nearing handover. Brief says: Customer
Overview — correct; Assessment Summary — score 38 (High Risk); Priority Risks —
no contractor's all risks, no public liability, no key-person cover on the site manager;
Strengths — documented safety induction; Recommended Questions include "how many workers
are on site daily?" and "who else depends on the site manager?"; Suggested Products —
Contractor's All Risks, Public Liability, Key-person; Potential Objections — "our client
mandates our insurance" (meaning: cover is already dictated by their contracts).

**Tasks:**
1. Annotate the Brief: which lines are facts, which are suggestions?
2. The contractor says their clients mandate insurance — how does that change the
   conversation goals? Rewrite them.
3. Which suggested question is weakest for this case, and what would you replace it with?
4. One recommendation in Section 7 conflicts with the strengths (safety induction exists).
   How do you reconcile and explain this to the client?
5. Write a personalised opening line (their words, context, strengths).

   c. Recommended Questions and Suggested Products *(incorrect)*
   d. All sections are equally factual *(incorrect)*

2. The Advisor Brief is generated:
   a. Every week on a schedule
   b. Immediately after the client completes their assessment *(correct)*
   c. When the client complains
   d. By the advisor manually *(incorrect)*

3. A "suggested product" in the Brief is:
   a. A binding recommendation you must sell
   b. AI mapping that you verify before acting on *(correct)*
   c. A price quote *(incorrect)*
   d. A renewal reminder *(incorrect)*

4. Personalising the opening line means:
   a. Using the client's own words, context, and strengths *(correct)*
   b. Reading the Copilot line word for word *(incorrect)*
   c. Using a longer script *(incorrect)*
   d. Adding the client's name only *(incorrect)*

5. Before first contact, you should:
   a. Assume the brief is complete and call immediately

## 9. AI Tutor Knowledge Base

- **KB-3.1 Advisor Brief definition:** one-page AI-generated briefing produced by the
  Advisor Copilot after assessment completion; summarises the client, risks, strengths,
  conversation guidance, products, and objections for the advisor.
- **KB-3.2 Nine sections:** Customer Overview, Assessment Summary, Priority Risks,
  Strengths, Conversation Goals, Recommended Questions, Suggested Products, Learning
  Progress, Potential Objections.
- **KB-3.3 Facts vs suggestions:** scores/strengths/learning progress are engine output
  (facts); goals/questions/products/objections are AI judgement (suggestions).
- **KB-3.4 Personalisation formula:** their words + their context + their strengths.
- **KB-3.5 Contact etiquette:** acknowledge strengths first, personalise, use 3 targeted
  questions, schedule inside CRM windows (e.g., contact high-opportunity leads within
  hours), log the next action.

## 11. Lesson Completion Standard

This lesson is complete when learners can:
- List the nine Brief sections and classify each as fact or suggestion.
- Personalise an opening line using the three-touch formula.
- Run the 5-point QA checklist and log first-contact actions in the CRM.
- Identify at least two weaknesses or contradictions in a sample Brief.

## 12. Final Takeaway

The Advisor Brief gets you to "qualified and prepared" in one page — but it is still your
conversation, your judgement, and your relationship. Facts are for quoting; suggestions
are for testing; the final word is always yours.
