# CCA 105 — Lesson 5: Preparing for a Risk Review

**Module:** CCA 105 — Practical Risk Advisory & Client Assessment
**Video duration:** ~45 minutes | **Workshop:** ~25 minutes | **Independent:** ~20 minutes

---

## 1. Lesson Overview

A Risk Review is a structured, prepared meeting — never an improvisation. This lesson
teaches the full preparation workflow: pulling data, verifying facts, forming risk
hypotheses, designing an agenda, pre-writing the opening, and readying materials. By the
end, learners can prepare for any Risk Review in a repeatable 6-step routine.

## 2. Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, learners will be able to:

1. Apply a 6-step Risk Review preparation routine.
2. Verify assessment facts against the client profile and report.
3. Build 2–4 risk hypotheses to test in conversation.
4. Design an agenda that prioritises the client's three biggest exposures.
5. Prepare opening lines, questions, materials, and meeting logistics.

## 5. Learner Handbook

### 5.1 The 6-step preparation routine

| Step | Action | Output |
|------|--------|--------|
| 1 | **Gather** | Pull the CRM lead card, Advisor Brief, CoverScore Report, chat history, and any prior notes. Keep them in one place. |
| 2 | **Verify** | Confirm client identity, size/scale, domain, date of assessment, confidence level. Flag anything to confirm in conversation. |
| 3 | **Hypothesise** | Write 2–4 risk hypotheses as questions to test (e.g., "Does their reliance on one supplier also threaten their business income?"). |
| 4 | **Agenda** | Select the 3 highest-impact gaps; allocate time; define one decision you want the client to leave the meeting having made. |
| 5 | **Open** | Pre-write a personal opening: acknowledge strengths, state purpose, ask permission to explore. |
| 6 | **Rehearse** | Read the opening and top-3 questions aloud at least twice. |

### 5.2 Verification discipline

- **Facts you can trust:** score, band, strengths, learning progress, client overview.
- **Facts to double-check:** contact details, business scale, sum assured on existing
  policies, anything the client typed quickly in free text.
- **Always re-verify in conversation:** coverage details, dependents count, current
  premium, assets that have changed value.

Never present an unverified number as fact. If uncertain, ask.

### 5.3 Risk hypotheses

Frame hypotheses as questions, not conclusions:
- "Does the single-supplier dependence also threaten their business income?"
- "Is their key-person gap really about the founder, or the site manager too?"
- "Is the low cyber pillar driven by confusion about what a backup actually is?"

Each hypothesis earns a slot on the review agenda; the conversation converts each into a
verified answer.

### 5.4 Building the agenda

| Priority | Item | Time | Success marker |
|----------|------|------|----------------|
| 1 | Confirm gap A + its financial impact | 10 min | Client articulates the impact in their words |
| 2 | Confirm gap B + options | 10 min | Client understands the trade-off |
| 3 | Agree next step | 5 min | A named action + date |
| Buffer | Open questions, rapport | 5 min | — |

End the meeting knowing: one decision made, one next step agreed, one follow-up slotted.

### 5.5 Materials to have ready

- Copy of the client's CoverScore Report
- Your Advisor Brief annotations
- Risk Review Notes template
- Client Question Bank (selected questions)
- Blank Client Action Plan to complete together
- Any product brochures/quote ranges ONLY if the client asks or after review

### 5.6 Meeting logistics

- Confirm time, channel (in person / call / video), and who attends.
- Set your own pre-meeting buffer (quiet, notes open, notifications off).
- Pre-book the follow-up time in your calendar.

## 6. Practical Exercises

**Exercise 5.1 — Build a full prep pack (25 min)**
Using the lesson-5 case client below, run all six steps and produce: verification table,
2–4 hypotheses, agenda, opening line, and a materials list.

**Exercise 5.2 — Rehearse the opening (10 min)**
Read your personalised opening aloud twice. Record it. Listen back and refine: shorter,
warmer, fewer filler words.

**Exercise 5.3 — The decision (10 min)**
For your case client, define the single decision you want them to make in the meeting.
Write three possible "winning" outcomes — none of which is "sign a policy" — proving the
review succeeds even without an immediate sale.

## 7. Case Study

**Scenario:** You are preparing a Risk Review for Mr. Bello, 52, founder of a food
distribution company (85 staff, 40 vehicles, revenue NGN 1.1B, cold-chain warehouse).
Assessment: CoverScore 41 (Needs Attention). Pillars: Property 50, Fleet 45, **Business
Income 22**, **Refrigeration/Equipment 25**, Liability 60, People 55, Cyber 70, Future 40.
Advisor Brief notes strength: documented maintenance schedule for the cold-chain.
Suggested products: Business Interruption, Refrigeration & Machinery Breakdown, Fleet
Physical Damage (comprehensive), Goods-in-Transit. Recommended questions include "what
happens to revenue if the cold-chain fails for a week?" Objects; the COO completed the
assessment. Next day the CEO personally requested "someone come and look at the cold
chain."

**Tasks:**
1. Run Step 2 (verify). List what you check and what you will confirm in conversation.
2. Write 4 risk hypotheses as questions.
3. Build the 3-priority agenda with success markers.
4. Pre-write the opening (strengths-first, personalised, permission-based).
5. List the materials you will bring and the one decision you want this meeting to end with.

## 8. Knowledge Check

1. The Risk Review begins:
   a. When the client walks in
   b. With the CRM filter and lead card, before the meeting *(correct)*
   c. When the advisor opens the product catalogue *(incorrect)*
   d. At renewal *(incorrect)*

2. A risk hypothesis should be framed as:
   a. A firm conclusion
   b. A question to be tested in conversation *(correct)*
   c. A product recommendation *(incorrect)*
   d. A premium estimate *(incorrect)*

3. Which item is BEST prepared before the meeting?
   a. The opening line *(correct)*
   b. The final premium *(incorrect)*
   c. The client's compliance file *(incorrect)*
   d. The claim form *(incorrect)*

4. In verification, an unverified number should be:
   a. Presented as fact
   b. Confirmed in conversation before being relied on *(correct)*
   c. Omitted forever *(incorrect)*
   d. Rounded up *(incorrect)*

5. A successful Risk Review ends with:
   a. A signed policy in every case
   b. A named next step, a decision, and a confirmed follow-up *(correct)*
   c. A new assessment
   d. A handshake only *(incorrect)*

**Answers:** 1-b, 2-b, 3-a, 4-b, 5-b.

## 9. AI Tutor Knowledge Base

- **KB-5.1 Six-step routine:** Gather → Verify → Hypothesise → Agenda → Open → Rehearse.
- **KB-5.2 Verification rules:** trust engine output for scores; verify identity, contact,
  scale, sums assured, and any free-text details in conversation.
- **KB-5.3 Hypothesis formatting:** hypotheses are questions ("Does X threaten Y?"), each
  with a slot on the agenda.
- **KB-5.4 Agenda design:** 3 priorities max, time-boxed, with one decision to leave with;
  include an early rapport/purpose block.
- **KB-5.5 Materials:** report copy, annotated brief, notes template, question selection,
  blank action plan; product material only after the review stage.

## 10. AI Coaching Prompts

- **Prompt 5.1 (Prep drill):** "Provide a client profile + assessment summary. Ask the
  learner to run all 6 preparation steps and hand back a completed prep sheet. Critique
  depth and prioritisation."
- **Prompt 5.2 (Opening retake):** "Role-play the client at the start of the review. The
  learner must deliver their personal opening. Coach on warmth, confidence, and avoiding
  filler. Two retakes minimum."
- **Prompt 5.3 (Hypothesis test):** "Take the learner's hypotheses and challenge each
  with a counter-scenario. They must defend or revise the hypothesis using the data."

## 11. Lesson Completion Standard

This lesson is complete when learners can:
- Produce a complete 6-step prep pack for a given client without prompting.
- Pre-write and deliver a personalised opening aloud.
- Build a 3-priority agenda with time boxes, success markers, and one decision.
- List required materials and verify facts before the meeting.

## 12. Final Takeaway

Every brilliant Risk Review you've ever watched was prepared — somewhere quiet, before
the meeting. Preparation is not overhead; it is the product. Show up prepared and the
client will feel it in the first sentence.
