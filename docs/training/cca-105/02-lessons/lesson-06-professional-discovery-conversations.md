# CCA 105 — Lesson 6: Professional Discovery Conversations

**Module:** CCA 105 — Practical Risk Advisory & Client Assessment
**Video duration:** ~45 minutes | **Workshop:** ~30 minutes | **Independent:** ~15 minutes

---

## 1. Lesson Overview

Discovery is where the assessment meets reality. The AI already gathered data; the
advisor now confirms, deepens, and humanises it — and in doing so, builds the trust that
makes recommendations stick. This lesson teaches a structured discovery conversation:
the 6-stage conversation arc, the question funnel, risk chains, and the empathy that
turns a review into a relationship.

## 2. Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, learners will be able to:

1. Run the 6-stage Risk Review conversation arc from open to close.
2. Confirm and enrich assessment data without interrogating the client.
3. Use the question funnel and risk chains to deepen understanding.
4. Practise the listening skills that build trust (Listen–Reflect–Probe).
5. Transition naturally from discovery to recommendations.

## 5. Learner Handbook

### 5.1 The 6-stage conversation arc

| Stage | Purpose | Key move |
|-------|---------|----------|
| 1. Open | Set context, build rapport | Personal greeting; thank them; state purpose |
| 2. Discovery | Confirm and enrich data | Warm-up, then funnel questions |
| 3. Confirm | Lock facts | "Let me check I've got this right…" |
| 4. Impact | Connect risk to money | Build risk chains together |
| 5. Options | Present gap options | 2–3 paths per priority, trade-offs |
| 6. Close | Agree next step | Named action, date, follow-up |

### 5.2 Confirming without interrogating

The client already answered the assessment. Discovery re-verifies the important bits
politely:

- "Your assessment mentions no business interruption cover — just to be sure, is that
  still the case? And has anything changed since you filled it in?"
- "You ticked that you have fire cover on the building — is that building insurance,
  or does it cover contents and stock too?"

You are not making the client feel tested; you are giving them permission to correct.

### 5.3 The question funnel

Start broad, then narrow, then deepen for impact:

1. **Broad / open:** "Tell me how the business has been growing over the last two years."
2. **Probing:** "You mentioned the cold-chain is critical — what happens if it fails?"
3. **Reflective:** "So continuity is your biggest fear right now — is that fair to say?"
4. **Consequence:** "If the cold-chain failed for a week, what would that cost you?"

### 5.4 Risk chains in conversation

Format: **Event → Consequence → Financial impact.**

Client confirms each link so the chain becomes *theirs*, not yours:
- Event: "If your founder-owner were hospitalised for three months…"
- Consequence: "…the sales team would have no one to approve procurement…"
- Impact: "…and that could cost the business around NGN X a month."

A client who can repeat the chain understands the recommendation.

### 5.5 The listening triad

- **Listen** without interrupting; keep the phone away.
- **Reflect** by paraphrasing: "So what worries you most is the gap if you step away."
- **Probe** once deeper: "Tell me more about that — what does it look like in practice?"

Do's: short questions, single questions, silence after the question. Don'ts: multiple
questions at once, answering for yourself, jumping to products mid-story.

### 5.6 Transitioning to recommendations

Three bridge sentences to leave discovery cleanly:
1. "I have what I need — let me bring this together for you."
2. "From everything you've shared, your three biggest priorities look like this…"
3. "Here's how I'd sequence the fix, starting with your highest concern."

## 6. Practical Exercises

**Exercise 6.1 — Write the arc (20 min)**
For Mr. Bello (lesson 5), write the full 6-stage arc: what you will say at each stage,
including two risk chains.

**Exercise 6.2 — Funnel practice (15 min)**
For a family protector client (personal domain), write a 6-question funnel: 2 broad, 2
probing, 1 reflective, 1 consequence.

**Exercise 6.3 — Listening triad (15 min)**
Triads: advisor, client, observer. The client describes a real worry; the advisor
practises Listen–Reflect–Probe. The observer counts questions asked, interruptions, and
uses of "so what I hear is…". Debrief each round.

## 7. Case Study

**Scenario:** You are in the Risk Review with Mr. Bello. Assessment says fleet is 45, but
the COO's answers indicate "all vehicles comprehensive." During discovery, Mr. Bello
mentions casually: "The new trucks, we only got third-party on three of them because cash
was tight." Later: "If the cold-chain goes down for more than two days, we start losing
stock and big clients ask questions." He is guarded about premiums and asks on two
occasions whether this will cost him money.

**Tasks:**
1. Using the confirm stage, how do you gently correct the fleet discrepancy in the record?
2. Build the risk chain for the cold-chain comment using his own words as the links.
3. Which listening triad move would you use after "clients ask questions"?
4. At the close, agree ONE decision and ONE next step that respect his cost sensitivity.
5. Write the exact bridge sentence that takes you from discovery into options.

## 9. AI Tutor Knowledge Base

- **KB-6.1 Six-stage arc:** Open, Discovery, Confirm, Impact, Options, Close. Applies to
  every Risk Review.
- **KB-6.2 Confirmation etiquette:** verification phrased as permission to correct; never
  make the client feel tested on the assessment.
- **KB-6.3 Question funnel:** broad open → probing → reflective → consequence.
- **KB-6.4 Risk chain:** event → consequence → financial impact; have the client confirm
  each link.
- **KB-6.5 Listening triad:** Listen, Reflect (paraphrase), Probe (one deeper question).
  Avoid multi-part questions and interruptions.
- **KB-6.6 Bridge sentences:** three phrasings to transition from discovery to options.

## 10. AI Coaching Prompts

- **Prompt 6.1 (Arc rehearsal):** "Run a full Risk Review simulation where the learner
  must hit all 6 stages in order and confirm at least two assessment facts. Step in and
  redirect if they skip confirm or close."
- **Prompt 6.2 (Funnel practice):** "The learner must extract a complete picture from a
  reluctant client who answers in one word throughout. Coach them to rebuild the funnel
  and use reflective pauses."
- **Prompt 6.3 (Detection drill):** "Play audio-like scenes with common discovery fails
  (interrupting, multi-part questions, product-pitching too early). The learner must
  detect each fail and replay the moment correctly."

## 11. Lesson Completion Standard

This lesson is complete when learners can:
- Run the 6-stage arc naturally in a simulated review.
- Confirm and correct at least two assessment facts respectfully.
- Build and deliver at least one risk chain using the client's own words.
- Demonstrate Listen–Reflect–Probe and a clean transition into options.

## 12. Final Takeaway

Discovery is not a data-entry task — it is the moment you prove you listen. When the
client hears their own words reflected back at them, in your voice, they begin to trust.
Master that, and recommendations become suggestions between friends.
