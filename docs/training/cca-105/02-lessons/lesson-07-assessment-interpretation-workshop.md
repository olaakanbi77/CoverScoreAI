# CCA 105 — Lesson 7: Assessment Interpretation Workshop

**Module:** CCA 105 — Practical Risk Advisory & Client Assessment
**Type:** Interactive workshop (video-guided) | **Duration:** ~75 minutes
**Workshop:** ~45 minutes | **Independent:** ~30 minutes

---

## 1. Lesson Overview

This is a hands-on interpretation workshop. Learners work through multiple industry
assessment records — SME, Healthcare, Manufacturing, School, Church, and Transport — and
practise reading reports, building risk-priority matrices, and deciding what to surface
in a Risk Review. The lesson is structured as guided practice: interpret, compare,
prioritise, present.

## 2. Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, learners will be able to:

1. Read reports across multiple assessment domains (SME, HOS, MFG, SCH, CHR, TRN).
2. Build a risk-priority matrix for an unfamiliar industry.
3. Convert differing pillar scores into a defensible priority list.
4. Present a prepared interpretation to the class, clearly and confidently.
5. Recognise cross-industry patterns (specific silver bullets vs systemic gaps).

## 5. Learner Handbook

### 5.1 The interpretation workflow

**Read → Fingerprint → Matrix → Message → Present.**

1. **Read** the report headers, score, and bands. Note assessment date and confidence.
2. **Fingerprint** the patterns: clusters, single weak pillars, divergence between
   related pillars.
3. **Matrix** each risk by likelihood and impact (Critical / Important / Possible / Monitor).
4. **Message** the top 3 into a clear narrative.
5. **Present** in 90 seconds: headline, evidence, priority, and one ask.

### 5.2 The four-quadrant matrix

| Quadrant | Likelihood | Impact | Action in review |
|----------|-----------|--------|------------------|
| Critical | High | High | Lead with this |
| Important | Low | High | Plan + monitor |
| Possible | High | Low | Monitor, low effort fixes |
| Monitor | Low | Low | Track periodically |

### 5.3 Cross-industry patterns

- **Universal top gaps:** business interruption / income continuity (MFG, SME, HOS),
  key-person dependence (everything), liability blind spots (CHR, SCH, CON).
- **Industry fingerprints:** health facilities (equipment + utilities), schools (child
  safety + liability), transport (fleet + goods in transit), churches (public liability +
  events), manufacturing (machinery breakdown + supply chain).
- When a *low score is the industry's silver bullet* (only one pillar drives the entire
  exposure), interpretation becomes short and confident. When problems are *systemic*
  (many pillars low), the narrative changes: comprehensive, sequenced plan, no single fix.

### 5.4 Examples for practice (fresh records)

**School (SCH):** CoverScore 58 (Needs Improvement). Pillars: Students' Safety 30, Staff 60,
Premises 75, Vehicles 68, Activities 45, Liability 40, Records 70, Policies 65.
Note: 2 "not sure" answers on liability; confidence medium.

**Church (CHR):** CoverScore 66 (Moderately Resilient). Pillars: Congregation & Visitors 55, Assets 80,
Events 35, Compliance 50, Vehicles 72. Note: strong asset pillar; events pillar low —
conference season upcoming.

**Transport (TRN):** CoverScore 51 (Needs Improvement). Pillars: Fleet 40, Drivers 60,
Cargo 25, Route 70, Liability 55, Maintenance 45. Note: cargo pillar 25 — goods in
transit not insured; 3 drivers without detailed records.

## 6. Practical Exercises

**Exercise 7.1 — Work three records (25 min)**
For each record above (SCH, CHR, TRN): draw the fingerprint, score the risks into the
matrix, and pick the top 3 priorities with a two-line justification.

**Exercise 7.2 — The 90-second presentation (15 min)**
Pick one record. Present in 90 seconds: headline (score + story), evidence (two pillar
numbers), priority (top risk), and one ask. Time yourself.

**Exercise 7.3 — Patterns hunt (10 min)**
Across all seven lessons' case clients, list every repeated gap (e.g., business
interruption) and every industry-specific gap. Which gaps appear in >75% of records?

## 7. Case Study

**Scenario — Four records, one morning (the workshop deck):**
You receive four fresh assessments before lunch:
1. **SME restaurant (SME):** CoverScore 49. Pillars: Property 55, Stock/Cash 40,
   Liability 62, People 70, Income Continuity 30, Compliance 60. Owner answered in
   6 minutes; medium confidence.
2. **Private hospital (HOS):** CoverScore 39. Pillars: Governance 64, Equipment 20,
   Utilities 28, Medical Records 45, Staff 58, Emergency Prep 32. Low-medium confidence
   (front desk staff completed it).
3. **Church (CHR):** CoverScore 66. Assets 80, Events 35, Visitors 55.
4. **Transport (TRN):** CoverScore 51. Cargo 25, Fleet 40, Drivers 60.

Prioritise your day: which client gets called first, which reviewed first, and which
needs re-verification before any conversation?

**Tasks:**
1. Rank the four by review urgency with reasons (use matrix thinking: impact × alignment
   with your response window).
2. For the hospital, flag the confidence issue and write the re-verification questions.
3. For each, state the ONE priority message you would deliver in a first call.
4. Which of the four would you treat as "systemic" vs "silver bullet" and why?

## 8. Knowledge Check

1. The interpretation workflow order is:
   a. Present → Matrix → Fingerprint → Read
   b. Read → Fingerprint → Matrix → Message → Present *(correct)*
   c. Matrix → Read → Present → Fingerprint *(incorrect)*
   d. Message → Read → Matrix → Present *(incorrect)*

2. A "silver bullet" industry pattern means:
   a. Every pillar is low
   b. One pillar drives the entire exposure *(correct)*
   c. The score is high
   d. No pillars are scorable *(incorrect)*

3. A risk with Low likelihood and High impact belongs in:
   a. Critical
   b. Important (plan + monitor) *(correct)*
   c. Possible
   d. Monitor

4. Which is a common interpretation error?
   a. Reading pillar divergence with context
   b. Trusting the total score without the fingerprint *(correct)*
   c. Noting assessment confidence
   d. Asking what changed since the assessment *(incorrect)*

5. For which kind of assessment should you re-verify before conversing?
   a. High-confidence, complete records
   b. Low-confidence records (rushed, skipped, not-sure-heavy) *(correct)*
   c. Records with strong property pillars
   d. Records completed exactly 6 minutes *(incorrect)*

**Answers:** 1-b, 2-b, 3-b, 4-b, 5-b.

## 9. AI Tutor Knowledge Base

- **KB-7.1 Workflow:** Read → Fingerprint → Matrix → Message → Present (the 5-step
  interpretation workflow from Lesson 4, applied to multiple industries).
- **KB-7.2 Matrix:** Critical (high×high), Important (low×high), Possible (high×low),
  Monitor (low×low) — mapping from Lesson 5's priority thinking.
- **KB-7.3 Patterns:** universal gaps (income continuity, key-person, liability) vs
  industry fingerprints (HOS equipment/utilities, SCH student safety, TRN cargo, CHR
  events, MFG machinery/supply).
- **KB-7.4 Silver-bullet vs systemic:** single driving pillar vs widespread low scores —
  changes narrative and urgency.
- **KB-7.5 Confidence in workshops:** always note confidence and re-verify low-confidence
  records before any advisory conversation.

## 10. AI Coaching Prompts

## 11. Lesson Completion Standard

This lesson is complete when learners can:
- Interpret reports from at least three different domains unaided.
- Place risks into the four-quadrant matrix with justification.
- Spot cross-industry patterns and name the pattern category.
- Deliver a 90-second interpretation presentation.

## 12. Final Takeaway

Interpretation is repetition, and repetition builds fluency. Every report you read — every
industry you translate — makes the next one faster and the client before you more
confident. By the end of this workshop, reading a report should feel like reading a
language you already speak.
