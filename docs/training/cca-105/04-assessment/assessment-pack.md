# CCA 105 — Part 4: Assessment Pack

**Module:** CCA 105 — Practical Risk Advisory & Client Assessment
**Total:** 100 points | **Pass:** 80–100 (Competent) | **Time:** ~2h30m

> **Canonical source:** the School Resilience™ Scenario Library
> (`docs/standards/scenarios/`) is the official practical workbook for CCA 105. In
> certification the assessor selects one scenario (SR-001–SR-010) at random. The
> standard in-module assessment uses **SR-001 — Flourish College**; assessors may
> substitute any library scenario at the same difficulty. Scoring aligns with the
> 100-point Simulation & Assessment Framework (`docs/standards/06-simulation-and-assessment-framework.md`).

## 2. Assessment Scenario — SR-001 (Flourish College)

Learners receive exactly what an advisor receives in real life:

1. **AI-generated Advisor Brief™** — Flourish College, Jos; Florence James,
   Proprietress. CoverScore **18/100, Critical Risk**. Primary gap: Property
   Protection (5). Evidence: no Fire Insurance; fire alarm weakness; extinguisher
   servicing weakness; rare maintenance; student incident; no written emergency
   procedures; no Public Liability; driver-training gap.
2. **CoverScore Report™** — full report including pillar scores (Transport Safety 43,
   Business Continuity 35, Student Safety 32, Regulatory Readiness 30, Property
   Protection 5), the risk story, resilience forecast, and improvement areas.
3. **Client profile** — Florence James, Proprietress, warm but financially guarded.
4. **CRM notes** — assessment timing, confidence (medium), no prior advisor
   relationship.

The learner must:
1. **Review** the Advisor Brief and report (knowns vs unknowns).
2. **Prepare** the Risk Review (purpose, discovery questions, anticipated objections).
3. **Conduct** the simulated Risk Review with the client.
4. **Discover** hidden information (budget constraint, untested alarm, unserviced
   extinguishers, reactive maintenance, playground accident, no written procedures, no
   driver training, no closure-cost calculation).
5. **Interpret** the assessment and explain it plainly.
6. **Prioritise** the risks and **separate** Risk Reduction from Risk Transfer.
7. **Agree** a specific next step.
8. **Complete** the CRM handoff.

---

## 3. Part 1 — Knowledge (20 points)

**Format:** 20 multiple-choice questions, 1 point each, 20 minutes.

Topics drawn from: the 10-step advisory workflow; the assessment and its five layers;
score bands (80–100 Highly Resilient, 60–79 Moderately Resilient, 40–59 Needs
Improvement, 20–39 High Risk, 0–19 Critical Risk); the Advisor Brief's purpose and
structure; the CoverScore Report structure; Risk Reduction vs Risk Transfer; the CARE™
framework (Connect, Analyse, Recommend, Execute); discovery questioning; the SR-001
case facts.

---

## 4. Part 2 — Assessment Interpretation (15 points)

**Format:** written, 30 minutes.

Using the SR-001 report:
- (5) State the CoverScore, band, and the five pillar scores correctly.
- (5) Identify the Highest Priority (Property Protection) and justify it with the
  pillar evidence.
- (5) Write the Biggest Insight for Florence in plain, non-fearful language (80–150
  words).

---

## 5. Part 3 — Advisor Brief Preparation (10 points)

**Format:** written, 20 minutes.

The learner produces a preparation sheet from the Advisor Brief:
- (3) Confirmed facts (from evidence).
- (3) Unknowns (building values, alarm condition, maintenance contracts, budget,
  existing policy details).
- (4) Three strong discovery questions (evidence-based, open, not product-first).

---

## 6. Part 4 — Discovery (20 points)

**Format:** observed live in the simulated Risk Review (with AI or assessor as Florence).

| Skill | Points | Evidence |
|-------|--------|----------|
| Purposeful questioning | 8 | Asks to understand; never asks "tell me everything that is wrong" |
| Hidden information | 8 | Uncovers at least 6 of the 8 hidden facts (budget constraint, untested alarm, unserviced extinguishers, reactive maintenance, playground accident, no written procedures, no driver training, no closure-cost calculation) |
| Listening & rapport | 4 | Reflects, paraphrases, confirms understanding, no lecturing |
| **Total** | **20** | |

---

## 7. Part 5 — Risk Prioritisation (10 points)

**Format:** observed live + brief written justification, 10 minutes.

The learner ranks the five risks and justifies with the client's own evidence:

1. Property Protection (5) — widest gap, most severe consequence.
2. Student Safety (32) — duty of care, incident history.
3. Regulatory Readiness (30) — no written procedures.
4. Business Continuity (35) — no closure plan.
5. Transport Safety (43) — operational but untrained driver.

- (4) Correct priority order.
- (3) Justification using scenario evidence.
- (3) Clear explanation of operational impact.

---

## 8. Part 6 — Recommendation (10 points)

**Format:** observed live + written, 15 minutes.

- (5) **Risk Reduction** first: alarm testing, extinguisher servicing, maintenance
  schedule, written emergency procedures, driver training.
- (5) **Risk Transfer** clearly separated: property protection for buildings and
  contents — presented only after reduction.

**Zero credit** for: mixing Reduction and Transfer; Transfer before Reduction; no
Reduction offered; invented risks; fear-based framing.

---

## 9. Part 7 — Objection Handling (5 points)

**Format:** observed live.

The learner handles at least two of: "We've been operating for years — why is the score
so low?" · "We've never had a major fire." · "We already have fire extinguishers." ·
"How much is all this going to cost?" · "We can't afford anything right now."

- (3) Understands the objection before responding.
- (2) Reframes to resilience/priority; no pressure; no fear.

---

## 10. Part 8 — CRM / Handoff (10 points)

**Format:** written/logged, 15 minutes.

The learner completes the handoff form:

**Client Objective · Key Findings · Client Concern · Risk Reduction · Risk Transfer ·
Agreed Next Step · Advisor Action · Client Action · Follow-Up Date**

| Criteria | Points |
|----------|--------|
| Complete (all sections filled) | 3 |
| Accurate (matches the simulated conversation) | 3 |
| Professional tone (no jargon, no fear) | 2 |
| Clear ownership (owner + date for each action) | 2 |
| **Total** | **10** |

**Handoff quality test:** another advisor should be able to understand who the client
is, what matters, what was discovered, what remains unknown, what was recommended, what
was agreed, and who owns the next action.

---

## 11. Critical Failure Conditions

Regardless of numerical score, **mandatory coaching and no certification on this
attempt** are triggered if the learner:

1. Fabricates client information.
2. Guarantees an insurance claim.
3. Deliberately misrepresents CoverScore.
4. Uses fear to pressure the client.
5. Recommends unsuitable insurance without adequate discovery.
6. Ignores a material client concern.
7. Treats insurance as a substitute for risk reduction (mixes Reduction and Transfer,
   or presents Transfer first).
8. Falsifies CRM information.

## 12. Administration Notes

- Assessors use the SR-001 observation sheet (Worksheet 4) and the CRM handoff form
  (Template 2) during the practical.
- AI marks (knowledge, interpretation, brief prep) feed the instructor debrief; they
  never replace human judgement on ethics.
- Re-sits must use a fresh scenario from the School Resilience™ Scenario Library
  (SR-002–SR-010), following the same eight-part structure and weights.
- Certification must be logged in the Academy with the score breakdown retained for
  audit.
- Final debrief narrative (as supplied): "You did not begin with a policy — you began
  with intelligence. You did not begin by selling — you began by understanding. You did
  not treat the CoverScore as the answer — you used it as a starting point. And you did
  not end with a quotation — you ended with an agreed action."
