# CCA 105 — Conversation Guides (Part 3)

Five guides for the five review moments. Each keeps the advisor human, honest, and
pressure-free.

---

## Guide 1 — Opening a Risk Review

**Goal:** Set a comfortable, trusting, purposeful start.

```
"Hello, [name]. Thank you for taking the time — and thank you for completing the
assessment; it gives us a real head start.

Before I share anything, two things I want you to know: (1) my goal today is that you
understand your position better and leave with one clear next step — nothing more; and
(2) everything I say is based on what your assessment and our conversation show — your
numbers, not a sales pitch.

May I walk you through what I found?"
```

**Rules**
- Acknowledge their effort in completing the assessment.
- State permission-based purpose early.
- Acknowledge a strength before any gap.
- Never open with the score alone.

---

## Guide 2 — Asking Better Questions

**Goal:** Deepen discovery without interrogation.

**Funnel in practice**
- Broad: "Tell me how things have been going since the assessment."
- Probing: "You mentioned the cold-chain is vital — what happens if it fails?"
- Reflective: "So protecting continuity is really the thing you care about most?"
- Consequence: "If that happened for two weeks, what would that cost you?"

**Question hygiene**
- One question at a time.
- Leave silence after each question.
- If the client answers briefly, reflect before probing again: "Thanks — and what would
  that look like in practice?"

**If they answer "I don't know"**
- "No problem — let's explore it together. Who in the business would know?"
- "Roughly is fine. Even a range helps me understand the scale."

---

## Guide 3 — Explaining CoverScore™

**Goal:** Make the score meaningful, calm, and honest.

**Plain-language formula**
1. The number: "Your CoverScore is [X] out of 100."
2. The plain meaning: "It tells us how protected you are *today* — not a grade of who
   you are."
3. The band: "That puts you in [band], which means [plain meaning of band]."
4. The driver: "The main things holding it down are [top 2 gaps]."
5. The reframe: "The good news — each of those has a practical fix, and we decide the
   order together."

**Never**
- Present the score as a judgement of the client.
- Exaggerate what an event would "definitely" cost.
- Invent numbers not in the report.
- Rush the explanation to get to products.

---

## Guide 4 — Discussing Risk Priorities

**Goal:** Agree the top three priorities together.

```
"From everything we've reviewed, I'd prioritise these three — and I'll tell you why,
then you tell me if it matches how you feel.

Number one: [gap]. Why: [financial impact + likelihood].
Number two: [gap]. Why: [reason].
Number three: [gap]. Why: [reason].

Which of these feels most urgent to you — and why?"
```

**If the client disagrees with the order**
- Explore first: "What makes [their choice] feel more urgent?"
- Accept good judgement: "That's fair — and here's the trade-off we should be aware of."
- Never bulldoze the client's priority.

**Sequencing rule:** prevent financial catastrophe first; then the big risks; then
fine-tuning.

---

## Guide 5 — Transitioning to Recommendations

**Goal:** Move cleanly from understanding to action, keeping the client in control.

**Bridges**
- "I have what I need — let me bring this together for you."
- "From everything you shared, your three biggest priorities look like this…"
- "Here's how I'd sequence the fix, starting with the one that protects you most."

**Presenting a recommendation**
1. Name the decision: "I recommend addressing [gap] first."
2. Give the why in their words: "You said revenue takes a hit if the line stops…"
3. Give options: "We can do it in three ways — [A], [B], or [C]. Here's the trade-off."
4. Ask for their agreement: "Which of these fits you best?"

**Close the handoff**
- Confirm the decision.
- Write the next step and date out loud.
- Log it in the CRM together.
- Set the follow-up: "I'll check in on [date]. Does that work?"

**Never**
- Present products before the review's impact stage.
- Recommend anything that cannot be traced to the assessment or the conversation.
- Leave the meeting without a named next step.
