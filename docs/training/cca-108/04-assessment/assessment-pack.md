# CCA 108 — Part 4: Assessment Pack

**Module:** CCA 108 — Capstone: Integrated Advisory Simulation & Professional Assessment
**Format:** One Final Integrated Advisory Simulation (Darman Schools Group), scored on
10 parts, 100 points.
**Pass mark:** 85+ Certified Advisor Ready (see pass bands below).
**Scenario source:** Darman Schools Group (Lagos) final certification case; assessors may
substitute comparable School Resilience™ or commercial scenarios at equivalent difficulty.

---

## 1. Assessment Overview

CCA 108 certifies that the learner can **independently operate the complete CoverScore
advisory cycle** — from AI-generated risk intelligence through human interpretation,
professional discovery, client advice, strategy development, relevant business growth,
portfolio management, and continuous relationship development — while exercising sound
professional judgment and maintaining the highest standards of ethics and integrity.

The learner must demonstrate:

> *"I can independently use CoverScore intelligence to understand a client's risk, conduct
> a professional advisory conversation, develop an appropriate strategy, create relevant
> business opportunities and establish a sustainable long-term client relationship."*

The certification instrument is a **Final Integrated Advisory Simulation** scored across
ten parts:

| Part | Area | Points |
|------|------|--------|
| 1 | Risk Intelligence | 10 |
| 2 | Professional Communication | 10 |
| 3 | Discovery & Client Understanding | 10 |
| 4 | CARE™ Framework | 10 |
| 5 | Professional Judgment | 15 |
| 6 | Advisory Strategy | 15 |
| 7 | Business Development | 10 |
| 8 | Portfolio Management | 10 |
| 9 | Ethics & Integrity | 5 |
| 10 | Documentation & Handoff | 5 |
| | **Total** | **100** |

**Formative rubrics:** Lessons 2–7 each carry their own in-lesson simulation criteria;
Lesson 8 uses the same 10-part model for practice self-assessment. This pack is the
summative, certification-level instrument.

## 2. The Final Case

The final case contains the richest, most demanding scenario in the Academy — incomplete
information, conflicting signals, multiple stakeholders, commercial opportunity,
operational risk, personal risk, previous recommendations, previous decisions, incident
history, existing insurance, emerging risks, and potential objections.

**Client:** Darman Schools Group · **Location:** Lagos · **Client:** Emmanuel Dairo
**Role:** Owner · **Established private school** · **Students:** 100–500 · **Transport:**
school buses · **Revenue:** moderately predictable

**Initial Risk Intelligence:**
- CoverScore: **27 / 100** · Risk Level: **High Risk** · Major Gap: **Property Protection**

**Previous Recommendations:**
- Risk Reduction: improve emergency procedures; inspect fire equipment; strengthen
  transport safety; improve maintenance inspections.
- Risk Transfer: Public Liability; Fire / Property protection.

**New Information (six months later):**
- Positive: fire extinguishers now inspected; emergency procedures implemented.
- Negative: maintenance remains irregular; bus-driver training remains incomplete.
- New: another bus acquired; student population increased.
- New event: a major fee-payment disruption occurred.
- New personal concern: owner concern about income continuity and retirement.

**Final CoverScore:** **56 / 100**

## 3. The Task — Fifteen Phases

The learner must independently conduct the entire advisory cycle:

| Phase | Task |
|-------|------|
| 1 | **Preparation** — review all intelligence: known risks, unresolved risks, emerging risks, client priorities, commercial opportunities, information gaps |
| 2 | **Connect** — conduct the opening conversation |
| 3 | **Discover** — professional questioning: business concerns, personal concerns, operational changes, financial priorities, decision drivers |
| 4 | **Interpret** — explain the CoverScore intelligence without treating the score as a diagnosis |
| 5 | **Reframe** — connect the intelligence to the client's actual circumstances |
| 6 | **Strategise** — a prioritised risk-management strategy; distinguish Avoid / Reduce / Transfer / Accept |
| 7 | **Advise** — present recommendations professionally |
| 8 | **Business Development** — relevant opportunities; business (protection areas from discovered needs) and personal (Income Protection, Retirement Readiness, Entrepreneur Protection — relevance first, never all three automatically) |
| 9 | **Handle Objections** — one or more evaluator objections (too expensive; already insured; let me think; I don't need this; cheaper elsewhere; guarantee coverage) |
| 10 | **Conversion** — secure a realistic next action |
| 11 | **Portfolio Management** — evaluator introduces a change (e.g., a second campus); explain how the risk portfolio changes |
| 12 | **Post-Loss Scenario** — evaluator introduces an incident (e.g., a student injury with a parental legal threat); respond professionally |
| 13 | **Ethical Dilemma** — evaluator introduces commercial pressure (e.g., "change the information to reduce premium"); demonstrate integrity |
| 14 | **Documentation** — produce the final advisory record |
| 15 | **Final Client Handoff** — agreed priorities, responsibilities, required information, next actions, follow-up process, reassessment plan |

## 4. Part 1 — Risk Intelligence (10 points)

| Mark | Descriptor |
|------|------------|
| 9–10 | Correctly interprets the CoverScore data, pillars, and signals; looks beyond the headline (27 → 56); identifies what improved, deteriorated, emerged, and what still needs attention |
| 6–8 | Mostly accurate interpretation; one conflicting signal missed or one number left unexplained |
| 3–5 | Reads the score as the whole story; treats the movement as a verdict |
| 0–2 | Misreads or misrepresents the data; ignores material signals |

**Absolutes:** a higher CoverScore does not mean all risks are acceptable; risk
intelligence is about understanding what the numbers mean.

---

## 5. Part 2 — Professional Communication (10 points)

| Mark | Descriptor |
|------|------------|
| 9–10 | Clear, calm, professional language throughout; zero jargon; every message tied to the client's situation; no pressure or fear language |
| 6–8 | Mostly clear and professional; one or two jargon slips or stiff phrases |
| 3–5 | Frequent jargon or formulaic speech; occasional pressure-adjacent phrasing |
| 0–2 | Product/marketing script; unclear or intimidating language dominates |

**Absolutes:** communication must be suitable for a decision-maker, an owner, and other
stakeholders; every sentence a client could repeat to a friend.

---

## 6. Part 3 — Discovery & Client Understanding (10 points)

| Mark | Descriptor |
|------|------------|
| 9–10 | Uncovers motivations and decision drivers (business, personal, operational, financial) with discovery questions; reflects them back and uses them later |
| 6–8 | Asks discovery questions and picks up drivers, but does not fully use them |
| 3–5 | Assumes drivers (e.g., price); few open questions |
| 0–2 | No discovery; proceeds on assumptions |

**Absolutes:** never assume; discover. Personal concerns (income continuity, retirement)
must be handled separately and respectfully.

---

## 7. Part 4 — CARE™ Framework (10 points)

The four stages must all appear, in order.

| Stage | Points | Evidence expected |
|-------|--------|-------------------|
| Connect | 2.5 | Introduces self, confirms purpose, establishes comfort, sets expectations; opens with curiosity |
| Ask | 2.5 | Explores concerns, priorities, experiences, operational changes, financial and personal realities |
| Reframe | 2.5 | Connects client responses to the intelligence honestly (reduction-first) |
| Enable | 2.5 | Immediate priorities, strategy areas, protection considerations, and next steps |

**Deduct all Enable points if the learner reaches next steps without completing Ask and
Reframe.** Product-first or self-serving advice is a critical fail condition (see §14).

---

## 8. Part 5 — Professional Judgment (15 points)

| Mark | Descriptor |
|------|------------|
| 13–15 | Sound decisions under uncertainty throughout; prioritises honestly, separates controls from financial protection, screens opportunities, corrects or escalates as appropriate |
| 9–12 | Sound overall; one weak judgment call or a forced opportunity |
| 5–8 | Questionable priorities; transfer before reduction; score over-interpreted |
| 0–4 | Dangerous judgment; misleading the client or ignoring evidence |

**Absolutes:** professional judgment means doing what is right even when the easier
commercial decision is different.

---

## 9. Part 6 — Advisory Strategy (15 points)

| Mark | Descriptor |
|------|------------|
| 13–15 | Creates a clear, prioritised risk-management strategy; correctly distinguishes Risk Reduction, Risk Transfer, Risk Acceptance, and Risk Avoidance; reduction first, transfer second, never mixed |
| 9–12 | Strong strategy; one response misclassified or one layer missing |
| 5–8 | Product-list strategy; responses not separated by nature |
| 0–4 | No coherent strategy; selling dominates |

**Absolutes:** the strategy is presented as layers (controls first, then residual
financial exposures) — never as a mere product list.

---

## 10. Part 7 — Business Development (10 points)

| Mark | Descriptor |
|------|------------|
| 9–10 | Identifies legitimate opportunities strictly supported by the conversation; applies the relevance rule (Need → Conversation → Assessment → Advice → Solution); no product dumping |
| 6–8 | Relevant opportunities identified; one item introduced without conversational support |
| 3–5 | Product-pulling behaviour; opportunities not earned by the conversation |
| 0–2 | Immediate pitching or maximum-penetration selling |

**Absolutes:** personal options (Income Protection, Retirement Readiness, Entrepreneur
Protection) are never recommended en masse — relevance is established first; the personal
concern is a separate conversation, never bundled.

---

## 11. Part 8 — Portfolio Management (10 points)

| Mark | Descriptor |
|------|------------|
| 9–10 | Recognises changing risk and maintains relationship continuity; reconciles new assets, new campus plans, new staff, incidents, and the fee disruption against the portfolio; aligns protection to the current profile |
| 6–8 | Mostly reconciles changes; one dimension missed |
| 3–5 | Treats the case as renewal; change is ignored |
| 0–2 | No portfolio thinking; the relationship is a single policy |

**Absolutes:** a policy is a transaction; a portfolio is a relationship. The portfolio
review asks "What has changed since our last review?" — not "Are you ready for renewal?"

---

## 12. Part 9 — Ethics & Integrity (5 points)

| Mark | Descriptor |
|------|------------|
| 5 | Professional responsibility under pressure throughout: refuses misrepresentation and unsupported assurance, escalates or declines as needed, protects confidentiality, treats AI as a source (never unquestionable) |
| 3–4 | Mostly ethical; one moment of hesitation or a soft compromise |
| 1–2 | Compromises under pressure; misleading reassurance |
| 0 | Deliberate misconduct on any critical fail condition |

**Absolutes:** AI is an intelligence source, not the professional decision-maker; never
recommend submitting inaccurate information; the client's interest determines the
recommendation—not compensation.

---

## 13. Part 10 — Documentation & Handoff (5 points)

| Mark | Descriptor |
|------|------------|
| 5 | Produces an accurate, complete advisory record and a clear client handoff: agreed priorities, responsibilities, required information, next actions, follow-up process, reassessment plan |
| 3–4 | Adequate record; one handoff element vague |
| 1–2 | Record incomplete or handoff absent |
| 0 | No documentation |

**Absolutes:** the final advisory record records intelligence, discussion, advice,
decision, and next steps; the handoff leaves the client with a clear path forward.

---

## 14. Critical Fail Conditions

Regardless of total score, the learner **cannot be certified** if they:

1. Deliberately misrepresent information.
2. Knowingly provide misleading advice.
3. Recommend unsuitable protection solely for commercial gain.
4. Fabricate client information.
5. Ignore material risk information.
6. Make unsupported coverage guarantees.
7. Treat AI output as unquestionable.
8. Breach confidentiality.
9. Pressure a client into an inappropriate decision.
10. Fail to escalate a serious professional concern.

Any critical fail means **not certified** until remediation and reassessment are
completed.

## 15. Scoring Bands

| Total | Verdict |
|-------|---------|
| 85–100 | **Certified Advisor Ready** — strong independent advisory capability; certified |
| 75–84 | **Competent** — acceptable professional competence with minor development areas; certified |
| 65–74 | **Conditional Pass** — targeted remediation required before final certification |
| Below 65 | **Not Yet Competent** — must repeat specified simulation components |

**Final certification decision:** Certified · Conditional · Not Certified. Any critical
fail (§14) overrides the band regardless of total score.

## 16. Administration Notes

- **Simulation load:** One fresh final case per attempt. Darman Schools Group is the
  default; assessors may draw comparable scenarios. All case facts are revealed through
  the learner's questioning and the evaluator's scripted injections (change, incident,
  ethical pressure).
- **Time:** The fifteen-phase simulation runs approximately 45–60 minutes, followed by a
  10-minute assessor debrief on the rubric.
- **Evaluator roles:** One assessor plays the client/owner and injects the scripted
  curveballs (second campus, injury with legal threat, change-the-information pressure);
  a second assessor scores the rubric. Where a single assessor runs the case, they record
  verbatim quotes for joint grading afterwards.
- **Intelligence pack:** The learner receives the case pack (scores, previous
  recommendations, new information, personal concern) beforehand. Preparation is part of
  the skill.
- **Grading:** Scores logged per part with evidence quotes; the debrief covers all ten
  parts.
- **Ethics flags:** Any fear language, pressure tactic, misrepresentation, or
  confidentiality slip is recorded as an ethics flag and is a coaching priority even
  where the learner passes.
- **Re-sits:** Maximum two attempts per cycle; remediation plan required between.
- **Audit:** Score breakdown retained per learner (Academy records).

## 17. Gold-Standard Performance

A strong submission includes all of the following, in order:

1. **Preparation** — reviews all case intelligence and lists known, unresolved, emerging,
   priorities, opportunities, and information gaps.
2. **Connect** — opens with curiosity: "I've reviewed your CoverScore assessment, but
   I'd like to hear from you before we draw conclusions. What was the main concern…?"
3. **Discover** — explores business and personal concerns, operational changes, financial
   priorities, and the fee-payment disruption.
4. **Interpret** — explains 27 → 56 plainly, including the conflicting signals: the
   improvement is real, but maintenance and driver training remain unresolved.
5. **Reframe** — ties the intelligence to the owner's actual circumstances, including
   his income and retirement concern.
6. **Strategise** — reduction first (maintenance, driver training, emergency procedures,
   fire equipment), then transfer (Public Liability, Fire/Property), with acceptance and
   avoidance assigned honestly, never mixed.
7. **Advise** — a two-layer executive message: controls that reduce likelihood; then the
   financial exposures that remain.
8. **Business Development** — maps only conversation-supported opportunities; treats the
   personal concern as a separate conversation and establishes relevance before naming a
   personal assessment.
9. **Handle Objections** — separates immediate-attention risks from later ones; no
   argument, no discount-bait.
10. **Conversion** — secures a realistic next action (Risk Review, protection analysis,
    management presentation, or personal assessment conversation).
11. **Portfolio Management** — explains how a second campus changes exposure, protection,
    staff, and continuity needs.
12. **Post-Loss** — acknowledges human-first, ensures appropriate handling, identifies
    lessons, reviews controls, assesses residual exposure.
13. **Ethical Dilemma** — refuses to change the information with the gold-standard line
    while offering legitimate cost-management options.
14. **Documentation** — an accurate six-field advisory record.
15. **Final Client Handoff** — agreed priorities, responsibilities, information, actions,
    follow-up, and reassessment plan.

**Debrief note:** notice what the gold standard does not do — it does not sell first,
read the report word-for-word, follow AI blindly, lower the value, or assume the client's
priorities.

## 18. The Academy Outcome

The graduate should no longer think "What insurance can I sell this client?" — they should
think "What does this client need to understand, what risks matter most, what decisions
need to be made, and how can I help them build greater resilience?" That is the transition
from Insurance Seller to **CoverScore Certified Risk Advisor™**.
