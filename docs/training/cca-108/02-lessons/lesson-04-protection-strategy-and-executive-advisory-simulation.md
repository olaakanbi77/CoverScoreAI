# CCA 108 — Lesson 4: Protection Strategy & Executive Advisory Simulation

**Module:** CCA 108 — Capstone: Integrated Advisory Simulation & Professional Assessment
**Video duration:** ~45 minutes | **Workshop:** ~40 minutes (executive simulation) | **Independent:** ~15 minutes

---

## 1. Lesson Overview

This lesson teaches and tests the Advisor's ability to translate risk intelligence into a
coherent protection strategy and communicate it to a decision-maker. The client — a Board,
Proprietor, or Senior Management team — wants "what we actually need to do." The Advisor
must separate Risk Reduction, Risk Transfer, Risk Acceptance, and Risk Avoidance, build a
strategy map across the college's risk areas, present in plain executive language, and
prioritise honestly.

## 2. Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, learners will be able to:

1. Distinguish Risk Reduction, Risk Transfer, Risk Acceptance, and Risk Avoidance.
2. Build a protection strategy map across student safety, property, transport, and business continuity.
3. Communicate strategy to a decision-maker in two layers: controls first, residual financial exposure second.
4. Prioritise: immediate safety/high severity, then major financial exposure, then lower urgency.
5. Obtain executive agreement on priority, responsibility, timeline, information required, protection review, and next meeting.
6. Self-assess on clarity, prioritisation, strategic thinking, commercial awareness, professionalism, and executive communication.

## 5. Learner Handbook

### 5.1 The decision-maker meeting

The client is the Board, Proprietor, or Senior Management, and wants: "Tell us what we
actually need to do." This is an executive audience — the advisor must be decisive,
strategic, and honest.

### 5.2 The four responses

Risk Reduction (lower likelihood or impact), Risk Transfer (insurance protection), Risk
Acceptance (consciously retained risks), Risk Avoidance (activities discontinued or
changed).

### 5.3 The strategy map

For each risk area, combine controls and protection: Student Safety (operational controls
× emergency procedures × liability protection); Property (fire controls × maintenance ×
property insurance); Transport (driver training × vehicle inspections × transport
procedures × motor/liability protection); Business Continuity (contingency planning ×
financial resilience × BI protection where appropriate).

### 5.4 Executive communication

Weak: a product list ("You need Fire Insurance, Public Liability, Motor Insurance and
Business Interruption"). Strong: two layers — strengthen the controls that reduce the
likelihood of an incident, then examine the financial exposures that remain even after
those controls are improved.

### 5.5 Prioritisation

Priority 1: immediate safety / high-severity exposure. Priority 2: major financial
exposure. Priority 3: important but lower-urgency improvements.

### 5.6 Executive decision

Obtain agreement on priority, responsibility, timeline, information required, protection
review, and next meeting.

### 5.7 Evaluation

Scored on clarity, prioritisation, strategic thinking, commercial awareness,
professionalism, and executive communication. The lesson summary: a Risk Advisor does
not overwhelm the client with recommendations — the Advisor creates a clear path from
risk to action.

## 6. Practical Exercises

**Exercise 4.1 — Four responses (15 min)**
For five exposures, tag each Reduce / Transfer / Accept / Avoid and justify.

**Exercise 4.2 — Strategy map (20 min)**
Build the four-quadrant strategy map for the case, showing controls and protection per
area.

**Exercise 4.3 — The two-layer message (15 min)**
Convert the weak product list into the strong two-layer executive message.

**Exercise 4.4 — Executive simulation (30 min)**
Present to a partner as the Board; obtain agreement on the six decision items; score on
the six evaluation criteria.

## 7. Case Study

**Scenario:** You present to the Flourish College Board after the CoverScore moved to 44.
They want "what we actually need to do."

**Tasks:**
1. Build the strategy map across student safety, property, transport, and continuity.
2. Present your two-layer executive message (controls first, residual financial exposure second).
3. List P1, P2, P3 priorities with responsibility and timeline.
4. List the six decision items you would seek agreement on, and the next meeting.

## 8. Knowledge Check

1. Risk Transfer means:
   a. Discontinuing an activity *(incorrect)*
   b. Insurance protection *(correct)*
   c. Consciously retaining the risk *(incorrect)*
   d. Lowering the likelihood *(incorrect)*

2. The STRONG executive message:
   a. Lists Fire, Public Liability, Motor, and Business Interruption *(incorrect)*
   b. Two layers: strengthen controls, then examine remaining financial exposures *(correct)*
   c. "Sign all four policies today" *(incorrect)*
   d. "Your score means purchase everything" *(incorrect)*

3. Priority 1 should be:
   a. Immediate safety / high-severity exposure *(correct)*
   b. Lower-urgency improvements *(incorrect)*
   c. Any product with a discount *(incorrect)*
   d. Personal products first *(incorrect)*

4. The executive decision requires agreement on all EXCEPT:
   a. Priority, responsibility, timeline *(incorrect)*
   b. Information required, protection review, next meeting *(incorrect)*
   c. The advisor's commission rate *(correct)*
   d. Next steps *(incorrect)*

5. A Risk Advisor's job for a decision-maker is to:
   a. Overwhelm with recommendations *(incorrect)*
   b. Create a clear path from risk to action *(correct)*
   c. Hide the real priorities *(incorrect)*
   d. Sell the highest premium first *(incorrect)*

**Answers:** 1-b, 2-b, 3-a, 4-c, 5-b.
