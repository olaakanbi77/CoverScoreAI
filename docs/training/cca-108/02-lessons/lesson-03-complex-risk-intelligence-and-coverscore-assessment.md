# CCA 108 — Lesson 3: Complex Risk Intelligence & CoverScore Assessment

**Module:** CCA 108 — Capstone: Integrated Advisory Simulation & Professional Assessment
**Video duration:** ~45 minutes | **Workshop:** ~25 minutes | **Independent:** ~15 minutes

## 1. Lesson Overview

This lesson tests the Advisor's ability to interpret complex and sometimes conflicting
intelligence. Three months into the Flourish College relationship, new information
arrives: some positive, some negative, some new, and one new incident. The overall
CoverScore has risen to 44 — but professional judgment requires looking beyond the
headline score. The learner must identify what improved, what deteriorated, what is new,
what needs immediate attention, what needs investigation, and what should be monitored —
and assign the correct risk response (Avoid / Reduce / Transfer / Accept).

## 2. Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, learners will be able to:

1. Sort new information into positive, negative, new, and incident categories.
2. Interpret an improved overall score without treating it as the whole story.
3. Detect conflicting signals between the headline score and individual pillars.
4. Identify what improved, deteriorated, is new, needs immediate attention, needs
   investigation, and should be monitored.
5. Assign the appropriate response — Avoid, Reduce, Transfer, Accept — to each major exposure.
6. Apply the key principle: a higher CoverScore does not mean all risks are acceptable.

## 5. Learner Handbook

### 5.1 The new information (three months on)

Positive: fire extinguishers installed and inspected; emergency procedures introduced.
Negative: a second bus purchased; the new driver has not completed defensive-driving
training. New: a laboratory opened; student population up 25%. Incident: another minor
student accident. The new CoverScore is 44; pillars are Property Protection 68, Student
Safety 40, Transport Safety 38, Business Continuity 35, Regulatory Readiness 62.

### 5.2 The trap

The overall score improved — but the Advisor should not simply congratulate the client.
An improving headline can hide a deteriorating pillar.

### 5.3 Professional interpretation

From the new data: Property Protection improved; Regulatory Readiness improved; Transport
Safety remains weak (Safety 43→38); Laboratory operations are a new exposure; a new
student accident occurred.

### 5.4 The key principle

> **A higher CoverScore does not mean all risks are acceptable.**

### 5.5 Conflicting signals

The headline moved 44 (up), Transport Safety fell 43→38, Student Safety rose 32→40.
Professional judgment requires looking beyond the headline score.

### 5.6 The six-task identification

Identify (1) what improved, (2) what deteriorated, (3) what is new, (4) what requires
immediate attention, (5) what requires further investigation, (6) what should be
monitored.

### 5.7 Risk response

For each major exposure, determine the appropriate response: Avoid, Reduce, Transfer, or
Accept.

### 5.8 Lesson summary

Risk intelligence is not about reading numbers. It is about understanding what the
numbers mean.

## 6. Practical Exercises

**Exercise 3.1 — Sort the signals (15 min)**
Sort the new information into positive / negative / new / incident, and tag each pillar
it touches.

**Exercise 3.2 — Beyond the headline (15 min)**
Explain the 44 → trap: what the headline says vs what the pillars say.

**Exercise 3.3 — The six tasks (20 min)**
Write the six-list for the case: improved, deteriorated, new, immediate attention,
investigate, monitor.

**Exercise 3.4 — Assign responses (20 min)**
For each major exposure (property, transport, laboratory, student safety, business
continuity, regulatory), assign Avoid / Reduce / Transfer / Accept and justify.

## 7. Case Study

**Scenario:** Three months after your first engagement, Florence James shares the new
information and the CoverScore is now 44.

**Tasks:**
1. Create the six-list (improved / deteriorated / new / attention / investigate / monitor).
2. Write the professional interpretation of 44 in plain language — including the
   confining signals.
3. Assign a risk response to laboratory operations and to transport safety.
4. Write the next-step for the new driver's training and the second bus.

## 8. Knowledge Check

1. Which is a NEGATIVE signal in the new information?
   a. Fire extinguishers inspected *(incorrect)*
   b. Second bus purchased, driver not defensive-trained *(correct)*
   c. Emergency procedures introduced *(incorrect)*
   d. Regulatory Readiness improved *(incorrect)*

2. The new CoverScore is:
   a. 18 *(incorrect)*
   b. 44 *(correct)*
   c. 61 *(incorrect)*
   d. 58 *(incorrect)*

3. Despite the higher score, the Advisor should NOT simply:
   a. Congratulate the client and stop *(correct)*
   b. Review Transport Safety (43→38) *(incorrect)*
   c. Investigate the laboratory exposure *(incorrect)*
   d. Follow up on the new incident *(incorrect)*

4. The key principle is:
   a. A higher CoverScore means all risks are acceptable *(incorrect)*
   b. A higher CoverScore does not mean all risks are acceptable *(correct)*
   c. The score is the verdict *(incorrect)*
   d. Transport safety is not important *(incorrect)*

5. Which risk response is NOT in the four-response model?
   a. Avoid / b. Reduce / c. Transfer / d. Delay — *(missing option)*
   a. Avoid *(incorrect)*
   b. Reduce *(incorrect)*
   c. Transfer *(incorrect)*
   d. Delay *(correct)*

**Answers:** 1-b, 2-b, 3-a, 4-b, 5-d.
