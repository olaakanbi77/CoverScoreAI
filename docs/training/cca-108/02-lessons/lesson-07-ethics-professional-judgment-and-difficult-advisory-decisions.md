# CCA 108 — Lesson 7: Ethics, Professional Judgment & Difficult Advisory Decisions

**Module:** CCA 108 — Capstone: Integrated Advisory Simulation & Professional Assessment
**Video duration:** ~45 minutes | **Workshop:** ~40 minutes (ethical dilemma simulation) | **Independent:** ~15 minutes

---

## 1. Lesson Overview

This lesson tests the learner's judgment when commercial pressure conflicts with
professional responsibility. The Advisor faces eight pressure scenarios — a manager
pushing for a signature, a client asking for inflated values, coverage-pressure, a
conflict of interest, an AI error, human oversight of AI, the professional rule, and a
difficult ethical decision. The lesson trains the learner to refuse misrepresentation,
refuse unsupported assurance, let client need determine the recommendation, and correct
AI output with evidence.

## 2. Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, learners will be able to:

1. Respond to commercial pressure from a manager without compromising ethics.
2. Refuse to misrepresent information requested by a client (gold-standard line).
3. Refuse unsupported coverage assurance with a plain explanation.
4. Identify conflicts of interest and let client need, suitability, risk, and professional judgment decide.
5. Detect and correct AI error through human oversight.
6. Apply the professional rule: AI is an intelligence source, not the professional decision-maker.
7. Choose the correct professional action (proceed, pause, seek clarification, escalate, decline, obtain more information).

## 5. Learner Handbook

### 5.1 Commercial pressure

A manager says "This client has a large premium opportunity. Just get the client to sign."
The Advisor must decide how to respond — the professional response is never to execute on
pressure that damages the advisory standard.

### 5.2 The inflated value

The client asks for a higher value to reduce premium. The gold-standard response refuses
to misrepresent information while offering legitimate cost-management options. Never
recommend submitting inaccurate information.

### 5.3 Coverage pressure

"Just tell me I'm covered." Refuse unsupported assurance and offer to explain the key
areas of protection and important limitations so the client can make an informed decision.

### 5.4 Conflict of interest

Where one solution pays more than an equally suitable alternative, the recommendation is
determined by client need, suitability, risk, and professional judgment — not personal
compensation.

### 5.5 AI error

If the AI recommends a priority that does not fit the client's reality (e.g., Cyber Risk
for a business that is not digital-intensive), the Advisor must not automatically follow
the AI.

### 5.6 Human oversight

AI says X → the Advisor investigates → client context reveals Y → the Advisor corrects
the interpretation → final advice is Y.

### 5.7 The professional rule

> AI is an intelligence source, not the professional decision-maker.

### 5.8 The difficult decision

Choose from: proceed, pause, seek clarification, escalate, decline, or obtain additional
information. Lesson summary: professional judgment means doing what is right even when
the easier commercial decision is different.

## 7. Case Study

**Scenario:** The manager pressures you to get the Darman Schools case to sign quickly.
The owner asks for a higher insured value to cut premium. The AI flags Cyber Risk as a
priority although the estate office and school are not digital-intensive.

**Tasks:**
1. Write your response to the manager.
2. Write the gold-standard inflated-value refusal.
3. Run the human-oversight sequence on the Cyber Risk recommendation.
4. State the final advice and the professional rule that governs it.

## 8. Knowledge Check

1. The gold-standard response to an inflated-value request:
   a. Submits the inflated value to keep the client *(incorrect)*
   b. "The information used for the protection needs to be accurate… I cannot recommend submitting inaccurate information." *(correct)*
   c. Blames the underwriter *(incorrect)*
   d. Hides the request *(incorrect)*

2. What determines the recommendation in a conflict of interest?
   a. Personal compensation *(incorrect)*
   b. Client need, suitability, risk and professional judgment *(correct)*
   c. Which policy is easiest to sell *(incorrect)*
   d. The manager's target *(incorrect)*

3. When the AI recommends Cyber Risk for a non-digital business, the Advisor should:
   a. Follow the AI automatically *(incorrect)*
   b. Investigate, correct the interpretation, and advise Y *(correct)*
   c. Assume the AI is always right *(incorrect)*
   d. Ignore the whole report *(incorrect)*

4. The professional rule states:
   a. AI is the decision-maker *(incorrect)*
   b. AI is an intelligence source, not the professional decision-maker *(correct)*
   c. AI replaces the Advisor *(incorrect)*
   d. AI sales are automatic *(incorrect)*

5. The difficult-decision menu includes:
   a. Proceed · pause · clarify · escalate · decline · obtain information *(correct)*
   b. Sell · upsell · cross-sell *(incorrect)*
   c. Discount · waive · ignore *(incorrect)*
   d. Sign · stamp · send *(incorrect)*

**Answers:** 1-b, 2-b, 3-b, 4-b, 5-a.

## 9. AI Tutor Knowledge Base

- **KB-7.1 Commercial pressure:** never let a manager's push compromise the advisory
  standard; respond professionally.
- **KB-7.2 Inflated value refusal:** accuracy first; offer legitimate cost options; never
  recommend submitting inaccurate information.
- **KB-7.3 Coverage pressure:** refuse unsupported assurance; explain key protection and
  limitations.
- **KB-7.4 Conflict of interest:** client need, suitability, risk, judgment — not
  compensation.
- **KB-7.5 AI error:** one recommendation, e.g., Cyber Risk, can misfit a client; the
  Advisor must not follow it blindly.
- **KB-7.6 Human oversight:** AI says X → investigate → context reveals Y → correct
  interpretation → final advice Y.
- **KB-7.7 Professional rule:** AI is an intelligence source, not the professional
  decision-maker.
- **KB-7.8 Decision options:** proceed, pause, seek clarification, escalate, decline,
  obtain additional information.
