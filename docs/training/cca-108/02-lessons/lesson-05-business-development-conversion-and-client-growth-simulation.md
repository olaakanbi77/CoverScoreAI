# CCA 108 — Lesson 5: Business Development, Conversion & Client Growth Simulation

**Module:** CCA 108 — Capstone: Integrated Advisory Simulation & Professional Assessment
**Video duration:** ~45 minutes | **Workshop:** ~40 minutes (simulation) | **Independent:** ~15 minutes

---

## 1. Lesson Overview

This lesson tests whether the Advisor can identify and develop legitimate business
opportunities without compromising advisory judgment. The learner maps the school's
commercial and personal potential, responds to a revealed personal concern correctly,
applies the relevance rule, handles the "already spending too much" objection, converts
the conversation into an appropriate next action, expands the relationship through
discovery, and handles a referral professionally.

## 2. Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, learners will be able to:

1. Map legitimate business and personal opportunities from the discovered needs.
2. Respond to a revealed personal concern separately and respectfully (not product-first).
3. Recommend a relevant personal assessment only where the client's words support it.
4. Apply the relevance rule: Need → Conversation → Assessment → Advice → Solution.
5. Handle the "already spending too much" objection without arguing.
6. Convert to an appropriate next action and handle a referral professionally.

## 5. Learner Handbook

### 5.1 The opportunity map

From discovered needs, map: Public Liability, Property/Fire, Motor, Business Interruption,
Group Life, Group Personal Accident, and healthcare (commercial); plus the proprietor's
revealed personal concern. Every opportunity must be earned by the conversation.

### 5.2 The personal reveal

The proprietor reveals: "If something happened to me, the school and my family would
both be affected." The professional response treats this as a separate conversation on
personal financial resilience — never a product pitch and never mixed into the school's
review.

### 5.3 Personal CoverScore options

Depending on what the client reveals: Income Protection, Entrepreneur Protection, or
Retirement Readiness — recommended only where the client's words support it.

### 5.4 The relevance rule

> Need → Conversation → Assessment → Advice → Solution. NOT Product → Pitch → Pressure.

### 5.5 The objection

"We're already spending too much on insurance." Separate immediate-attention risks from
later risks, then decide the appropriate level of protection within the client's
priorities.

### 5.6 Conversion

Convert to an appropriate next action: information gathering, Risk Review, protection
analysis, quotation, personal assessment, management presentation, or follow-up meeting.

### 5.7 Relationship expansion and referral

Growth is discovering more relevant needs and being useful when they emerge. On a
referral: "I'd be happy to speak with them. If you're comfortable, you can introduce us
and I'll have the same type of risk-focused conversation with them."

### 5.8 Lesson summary

Sustainable growth is a consequence of relevance, trust and value.

## 6. Practical Exercises

**Exercise 5.1 — Map the opportunities (20 min)**
From the case, produce the opportunity map with evidence for each item (which conversation
fact earns it).

**Exercise 5.2 — The personal response (15 min)**
Write the professional response to the personal reveal. Compare with the wrong response;
explain the difference.

**Exercise 5.3 — Objection drill (15 min)**
Practise the "already spending too much" response until it is calm, non-argumentative,
and prioritising.

**Exercise 5.4 — Conversion & referral (20 min)**
For six scenarios, state the appropriate next action, and run the referral response.

## 7. Case Study

**Scenario:** During the Flourish College review, Florence reveals her personal concern.
She also says the school is already spending too much on insurance, and later mentions
another school owner who could benefit.

**Tasks:**
1. Write your response to her personal reveal (separate conversation, no pitch).
2. State which personal assessment you would or would not recommend, and why.
3. Write your objection response separating immediate-attention from later risks.
4. State the conversion outcome and the next action you would agree.
5. Write your referral line.

## 8. Knowledge Check

1. The WRONG response to the personal reveal is:
   a. "We also have a Life Assurance product you should buy." *(correct)*
   b. Treating it as a separate conversation on personal resilience *(incorrect)*
   c. Offering a personal CoverScore assessment *(incorrect)*
   d. Asking what worries her most *(incorrect)*

2. The relevance rule is:
   a. Product → Pitch → Pressure *(incorrect)*
   b. Need → Conversation → Assessment → Advice → Solution *(correct)*
   c. Price → Policy → Premium *(incorrect)*
   d. Discount → Deal → Close *(incorrect)*

3. To "We're already spending too much on insurance," the professional first move is:
   a. Argue the premium is fair *(incorrect)*
   b. Find a cheaper competitor quote *(incorrect)*
   c. Separate immediate-attention risks from those that can be addressed later *(correct)*
   d. Sell something smaller to keep her *(incorrect)*

4. Personal assessments may be recommended:
   a. Only where the client's words support the need *(correct)*
   b. To every client automatically *(incorrect)*
   c. When the school review is failing *(incorrect)*
   d. As a bundle with the school policy *(incorrect)*

5. Sustainable growth is a consequence of:
   a. Relevance, trust and value *(correct)*
   b. Product pressure *(incorrect)*
   c. Discounting *(incorrect)*
   d. Cold calling *(incorrect)*

**Answers:** 1-a, 2-b, 3-c, 4-a, 5-a.
