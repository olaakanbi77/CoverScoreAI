# CCA 108 — Practical Tools (Part 3)

**Module:** CCA 108 — Capstone: Integrated Advisory Simulation & Professional Assessment

Practical, reusable tools for the working advisor and the training room.

## Tool 1 — The Capstone Card

Keep on the desk during any simulation:

> **Knowledge is not competence.** Competence is the ability to apply knowledge correctly
> in a real situation.

> **Do not sell because you found a product. Advise because you found a need.**

## Tool 2 — The Operating Model Card

AI discovers → AI assesses → AI generates intelligence → **YOU interpret** → **YOU
advise** → **YOU build strategy** → AI automates → **YOU manage the relationship** → AI
reassesses → **YOU continuously improve the relationship**.

## Tool 3 — The Four-Response Card

- **Avoid** — remove the exposure where possible.
- **Reduce** — lower the likelihood or impact.
- **Transfer** — move the financial consequence to appropriate protection.
- **Accept** — retain a consciously chosen exposure.

Risk Reduction™ first, Risk Transfer™ second — never mixed.

## Tool 4 — The Relevance Rule Card

> Need → Conversation → Assessment → Advice → Solution. NEVER Product → Pitch → Pressure.

Personal options (Income Protection, Retirement Readiness, Entrepreneur Protection) are
recommended only where the client's words establish relevance — never en masse.

## Tool 5 — The CARE™ Card

**C** Connect (rapport and context) → **A** Ask (concerns, priorities, experiences) →
**R** Reframe (connect concerns to risk intelligence) → **E** Enable (practical next
steps and action).

Memory device: *Connect before you advise. Ask before you assume. Reframe before you
recommend. Enable before you close.*

## Tool 6 — The Gold-Standard Refusals Card

- **Inflated value:** "The information used for the protection needs to be accurate. I
  can help you explore appropriate options for managing the cost, but I cannot recommend
  submitting inaccurate information."
- **Coverage pressure:** "I don't want to give you an assurance that the policy wording
  doesn't support. Let me explain the key areas of protection and the important
  limitations."
- **Personal reveal:** "Because it relates to your personal financial resilience, I'd
  suggest we treat it as a separate conversation rather than mixing it with the school's
  risk review."

## Tool 7 — The Professional Rule Card

> AI is an intelligence source, not the professional decision-maker.

If AI says X and the client context reveals Y: investigate, correct the interpretation,
and advise Y.

## Tool 8 — The Objection Card

> "We're already spending too much on insurance." → "Let's first separate the risks that
> require immediate attention from those that can be addressed later."

Other objections: never argue; anchor to priorities, scope, limits, exclusions, and
service.

## Tool 9 — The Final Advisory Record Card

Six fields: **Situation** (what changed?) · **Intelligence** (what did CoverScore
identify?) · **Discussion** (what was discussed?) · **Advice** (what was recommended?) ·
**Decision** (what did the client decide?) · **Next Step** (what happens next?).

## Tool 10 — The Critical-Fail Poster

Post in the training room. Certification is barred if the learner: misrepresents
information · gives misleading advice · recommends unsuitable protection for gain ·
fabricates client information · ignores material risk · makes unsupported guarantees ·
treats AI as unquestionable · breaches confidentiality · pressures the client · fails to
escalate a serious concern.

## Tool 11 — The Academy Pathway Card

Foundation (Knowledge) → Mindset (Risk Advisor Thinking) → Assessment (Understand the
Client) → Advisory (Create the Conversation) → Growth (Develop the Relationship) →
Portfolio (Manage the Relationship) → **Capstone (Demonstrate Professional Competence).**

## Tool 12 — The Final Principle Card

> **AI can identify the risk. You must understand the person behind the risk.**
