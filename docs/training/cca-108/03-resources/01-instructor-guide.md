# CCA 108 — Instructor Guide (Part 3)

**Module:** CCA 108 — Capstone: Integrated Advisory Simulation & Professional Assessment

## 1. Your Role

You are the instructor for the final module of the CoverScore Certified Advisor pathway.
Your job is not to teach new knowledge — it is to set the certification standard and run
the simulations that let learners prove capability. You model the shift from "Do you
understand?" to "Can you perform?". Keep the Capstone principle on the wall: *knowledge
is not competence — competence is the ability to apply knowledge correctly in a real
situation.* You are the gatekeeper of the professional standard, so integrity and refusal
under pressure are coached before they are assessed.

## 3. Room Setup

- Breakout triads: one advisor, one client/owner, one observer/scorekeeper rotating each round.
- Shared artefacts: Capstone principle poster, CARE™ diagram, four-response
  model card, relevance-rule card, professional-rule card, critical-fail poster, both case
  packs (Flourish College, Darman Schools).
- Strict timekeeping for every simulation; observers record phase transitions.
- A "stop" signal for critical fails, so a learner who pitches first, misrepresents, or
  treats AI as unquestionable can restart and learn in the moment.
- Recording equipment for the final simulation (recorded submissions are permitted per
  the assessment pack).

## 4. Facilitation Script (debrief template)

After every simulation, run the same six questions:

1. What did the Advisor do well? (name it specifically)
2. Which phases (or CARE™ stages) appeared, in order? Which were skipped or rushed?
3. Where did the Advisor listen versus assume — and did they discover the real drivers?
4. Did the strategy separate reduction from transfer, and was it prioritised honestly?
5. Where was ethics tested, and how did the Advisor hold the line?
6. What is the one thing to change for the next round?

Use the critical-fail list and the 10-part scorecard to highlight what would pass or fail
certification.

## 5. Common Learner Errors and Coaching

| Error | Coaching intervention |
|-------|------------------------|
| Opens with a product or conclusion | Replay the gold-standard opening; demand curiosity first (L2) |
| Reads the score as a verdict | Run the 27 → 56 interpretation drill; separate improvement from gaps (L3) |
| Lists products instead of a strategy | Rebuild the two-layer message; enforce the four-response model (L4) |
| Bundles the personal concern | Rehearse the separate-conversation response (L5) |
| Ignores changed circumstances | Drill the "What has changed since our last review?" opening (L6) |
| Compromises under pressure | Rehearse the gold-standard refusals until refusal is comfortable (L7) |
| Treats AI output as final | Run the X→Y human-oversight sequence until correction is normal (L7) |
| Skips documentation or handoff | Make the six-field record and handoff a mandatory part of every scene (L6/L8) |
| Product dumps personal options | Apply the relevance rule; remove every unsupported opportunity (L5/L8) |

## 6. Assessment Logistics

Use the module assessment in `04-assessment/assessment-pack.md`. Book one-on-one Final
Integrated Advisory Simulations (assessor plays Emmanuel Dairo and injects the scripted
curveballs — second campus, injury with legal threat, change-the-information pressure).
Score the 10-part rubric, flag any critical fail, and provide feedback within 48 hours.
85–100 certifies; 75–84 certifies with development notes; 65–74 requires targeted
remediation; below 65 requires repeating specified components. A critical fail always
bars certification until coaching and reassessment.
